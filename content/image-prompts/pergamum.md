# Pergamum — research brief for AI-generated reconstructions

## Setting and layout
Pergamon stood on a steep, cone-shaped hill above the Caicus (Bakırçay) plain in Mysia, near modern Bergama [bib:strabo-geography; bib:worldhistory-pergamon]. Strabo says its people were settled "on the very summit of the mountain," which is cone-like and ends in a sharp peak, and that the Caicus flows past through a very fertile plain [bib:strabo-geography]. The capital of the Attalid kings, Pergamon was bequeathed to Rome in 133 BC and became the first capital of the Roman province of Asia [bib:worldhistory-pergamon; bib:unesco-pergamon; wikidata:Q18986]. By the second half of the first century AD, the monumental acropolis built up by the Attalid kings crowned the hill [bib:strabo-geography; bib:worldhistory-pergamon].

## What stood then
Much of the famous acropolis complex was already centuries old by AD 95. The Great Altar, dedicated to Zeus Soter, with its long frieze of gods battling giants, was commissioned by Eumenes II (reigned 197–159 BC) [bib:worldhistory-pergamon; bib:ramsay-seven-churches]. Eumenes II also founded the library, which rivalled Alexandria's, built or renovated the Temple of Athena, and enlarged the hillside theatre to seat about 10,000; Strabo credits the Attalid kings with adding "sacred buildings and libraries" [bib:worldhistory-pergamon; bib:strabo-geography]. Pergamon's temple of Rome and Augustus, built probably in 29 BC, was the first provincial temple of the imperial cult in Asia, and it stood by AD 95: Tacitus records that the Pergamenes already had this sanctuary of Augustus in AD 26 [bib:ramsay-seven-churches; bib:tacitus-annals]. The temple of Trajan on the acropolis (the Trajaneum), Pergamon's second imperial temple, belongs to Trajan's reign (AD 98–117) and had **not** been built by AD 95 [bib:ramsay-seven-churches]. The Red Basilica in the lower city is thought to have been commissioned under Trajan or Hadrian, also after AD 95 [bib:worldhistory-pergamon]. The sanctuary of Asklepios, the healing god, was one of the city's chief cults [bib:ramsay-seven-churches; bib:worldhistory-pergamon]; the sources read for this brief do not describe its first-century buildings or date the round temple and grand courts seen in ruins today, so these should not be assumed for AD 95, when Pergamon's main Roman building patronage, under Trajan and Hadrian, was still to come [bib:worldhistory-pergamon]. Revelation calls Pergamum the place "where Satan's throne is" (Revelation 2:13) [scripture:Revelation 2:13]. Interpreters differ on the phrase: Ramsay saw in it chiefly the temple of Rome and Augustus and the imperial cult, with the city's commanding, royal hill as a secondary thought; others take it as the Great Altar of Zeus, or the city as a whole [bib:ramsay-seven-churches; bib:worldhistory-pergamon]. This brief does not choose among them.

## Materials and colours
The altar's frieze was relief sculpture [bib:ramsay-seven-churches; bib:worldhistory-pergamon]; the sources read for this brief say little else about materials. Images can follow the general look of Hellenistic Pergamon (pale stone and marble, tiled roofs, terraces cut into the grey-tan hill), but this is general guidance, not a sourced detail. The plain below would show cultivated fields along the Caicus [bib:strabo-geography].

## Landscape and plants
The acropolis rises above a broad plain watered by the Caicus, which Strabo calls very fertile and about the best land in Mysia [bib:strabo-geography]. Fields, olive groves and scattered trees around the hill, and a hill built up in terraces rather than wooded, are a general inference, not a sourced detail.

## People and daily life
As the first capital of the Roman province of Asia and the seat of its first imperial-cult temple, Pergamon would have had Greek-speaking civic elites, Roman officials and priests of the imperial cult, as well as worshippers of Asklepios [bib:worldhistory-pergamon; bib:ramsay-seven-churches]. Dress followed general Greco-Roman provincial patterns (tunics and cloaks); this is general, not a sourced detail.

## Key sites to show
- A distant or elevated view of the steep acropolis hill crowned by its Hellenistic temples and theatre, rising above the Caicus plain — the best single "overall feel" shot.
- The Great Altar's terrace and the hillside theatre, both standing by AD 95.
- The temple of Rome and Augustus (only in general terms and in its general place on the acropolis, since its form is not described).

## Not known
The appearance of the temple of Rome and Augustus is not described by the sources read for this brief; it should be shown only in general terms (a modest classical temple on the acropolis), not with invented detail. The Trajaneum and the Red Basilica, though the most photogenic ruins at the site today, came after AD 95 and should not appear in a reconstruction of the city as it stood when Revelation was written. The first-century form of the Asklepios sanctuary is not known; keep it modest and generic if shown at all, without the round temple and grand courts seen in ruins today.
