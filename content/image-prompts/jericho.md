# *Jericho* — research brief for AI-generated reconstructions

## Setting and layout
Jericho lay in the Jordan Valley near the Dead Sea, in a hot, low desert oasis more than 200 meters below sea level, with a long, barren mountain ridge to its west and an opposite ridge across the Jordan to its east [wikidata:Q5687; bib:josephus-jewish-war]. By the first century AD the inhabited city had shifted about two kilometers south of the old Old Testament mound (Tell es-Sultan) to a new site built up by the Hasmonean and Herodian dynasties as a winter palace complex, set along Wadi Qelt [wikidata:Q2460244; bib:murphy-oconnor-holy-land-guide]. This is the Jericho of the Gospel episodes below [openbible:jericho-2].

## What stood then
Herod the Great's winter palace complex at this southern site included courtyards, bathing facilities and a large artificial pool, built in the grand Roman manner Herod favored for his showcase building projects [wikidata:Q2460244; bib:murphy-oconnor-holy-land-guide]. A fountain near the old city, identified by local tradition with the prophet Elisha's miracle of sweetening the water, watered a large area of gardens and plantations nearby [bib:josephus-jewish-war; scripture:2 Kings 2:19-22].

## Materials and colours
Josephus singles out Jericho's plantations: many varieties of palm tree, the better kinds pressed for a honey "not much inferior in sweetness to other honey"; balsam, "the most precious of all the fruits in that place"; and cypress and myrobalanum trees — so rich a mix of rare trees that, he says, whoever called the place divine "would not be mistaken" [bib:josephus-jewish-war]. The warm, well-watered plain supported fruit in a way Josephus says could not easily be matched elsewhere in the habitable world [bib:josephus-jewish-war].

## Landscape and plants
Date palm groves and balsam plantations, dependent on irrigation from the spring, stood out against surrounding barren, uninhabited mountains and the hot, dry summer climate of the plain [bib:josephus-jewish-war]. The Jordan's own banks supported denser palm growth than land further from the river [bib:josephus-jewish-war].

## People and daily life
Jericho was home to Zacchaeus, "a chief tax collector" who "was rich," which suggests a town where taxes or tolls were collected [scripture:Luke 19:1-2]. The road climbing from Jericho to Jerusalem was a known, sometimes dangerous route, the setting for Jesus' parable of the Good Samaritan and for a blind beggar's encounter with Jesus [scripture:Luke 10:30; scripture:Mark 10:46].

## Key sites to show
1. The Herodian winter palace complex's courtyards and pool, set against the barren western mountains.
2. Date palm and balsam plantations fed by the spring, in a lush strip against the desert.
3. The road climbing west out of the oasis toward the Judean wilderness and Jerusalem.

## Not known
The winter palace complex's full decorative scheme and precise layout beyond excavated foundations are not completely recovered [bib:murphy-oconnor-holy-land-guide]. The exact boundary between episodes set at the old mound and those set at the Herodian-period city is not always distinguishable in the Gospel text itself, though the Gospel-era episodes are set at or near the Herodian site [openbible:jericho-2].
