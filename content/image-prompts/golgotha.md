# *Golgotha* — research brief for AI-generated reconstructions

## Setting and layout
All four Gospels place Golgotha, "place of a skull," outside Jerusalem's walls at the time of the crucifixion [scripture:Matthew 27:33; scripture:Mark 15:22; scripture:Luke 23:33; scripture:John 19:17]. For most of this period Jerusalem had only two walls; the outer "third wall" was merely begun, as foundations, by Herod Agrippa I in the early 40s AD and not completed until decades later, so ground that lay outside the city at the crucifixion could later be enclosed within it [bib:josephus-jewish-war]. This matches both candidate sites: archaeological work beneath the Church of the Holy Sepulchre found a first-century quarry with rock-cut tombs, land that fits a site once outside the wall of Jesus' day though now inside the Old City [wikidata:Q187702; bib:taylor-golgotha-reconsideration]. The Garden Tomb site, a separate candidate about 300 meters north, sits on a rocky escarpment and remains outside today's walls [wikidata:Q1494672; bib:taylor-golgotha-reconsideration].

## What stood then
At the time of the crucifixion there was no built monument at either candidate site: both were a disused stone quarry that had become a burial ground, consistent with John's description of "a garden" containing "a new tomb" near the execution site [scripture:John 19:41; bib:taylor-golgotha-reconsideration]. The rock-cut tombs under the Holy Sepulchre are dated to the first century; the tomb chamber at the Garden Tomb is dated by most archaeologists many centuries earlier, a difficulty for identifying it with the Gospels' "new" tomb [bib:taylor-golgotha-reconsideration]. The churches and monuments that mark both sites today were all built centuries after this period, beginning under Constantine in the fourth century [wikidata:Q187702].

## Materials and colours
The setting is the same pale cream to golden-white Jerusalem limestone common across the city's quarries, left in rough, tool-marked faces where cut for building stone, with smoother natural rock where tombs were carved into it [bib:ritmeyer-temple-mount-quest].

## Landscape and plants
The site combined quarry scars with cultivated ground: a garden adjoining or surrounding the tombs, suited to the olive trees and low scrub typical of the Judean hill country just outside the city walls [scripture:John 19:41; bib:rainey-notley-sacred-bridge]. The Gospels place the site near a road, where passersby could see and mock the execution [scripture:Mark 15:29].

## People and daily life
A Roman execution detail carried out the crucifixion, with a centurion overseeing it [scripture:Mark 15:39]. Women who had followed Jesus from Galilee watched from a distance [scripture:Mark 15:40]. Two others condemned to death were crucified alongside him [scripture:Luke 23:33].

## Key sites to show
1. A quarried limestone escarpment with tool marks, kept generic rather than matching either candidate's exact modern appearance.
2. A rock-cut tomb entrance of the rolling-stone type used in this period.
3. A garden setting among the quarry remains, with scattered olive trees.

## Not known
Which candidate site, if either precisely, is the historical Golgotha is not settled, and this project does not choose between them [bib:taylor-golgotha-reconsideration]. The exact course of Jerusalem's wall at the time of the crucifixion is debated, so the precise distance from the city gate is not fixed. The rock formation popularly compared to a skull near the Garden Tomb is a nineteenth-century observation, not an ancient description, and should not be presented as how the site looked or was understood in the first century. Images should show a generic quarry-and-garden setting outside the wall, without later church buildings, and without favoring one candidate's modern landscape over the other.
