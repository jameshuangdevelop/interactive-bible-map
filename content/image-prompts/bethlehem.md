# *Bethlehem* — research brief for AI-generated reconstructions

## Setting and layout
Bethlehem sits in the Judean hill country about 8 km south of Jerusalem, on a ridge among terraced hills at roughly 775 meters elevation [wikidata:Q5776; dare:28331]. In this period it was a small town rather than a major city, identified in Matthew and Luke as Jesus' birthplace and already known in the Old Testament as David's home town [scripture:Matthew 2:1; scripture:Luke 2:4]. Herod the Great's hilltop palace-fortress of Herodium stood a few kilometers to the southeast, a visible reminder of Herodian building even from this modest town [bib:rainey-notley-sacred-bridge].

## What stood then
No grand public buildings are attested at Bethlehem in this period; it was a rural hill-country town of domestic houses [bib:murphy-oconnor-holy-land-guide]. Tradition places Jesus' birth in a cave used to shelter animals, matching Luke's detail of a manger, since houses in this hill country commonly incorporated a cave or lower story for livestock [scripture:Luke 2:7; bib:murphy-oconnor-holy-land-guide]. The Church of the Nativity, first built under Constantine around AD 339 and rebuilt under Justinian in the mid-sixth century, did not yet exist in this period and should not appear in a reconstruction [bib:unesco-church-of-nativity].

## Materials and colours
Houses were built of local Judean limestone, pale cream to grey, with flat roofs of wooden beams, brushwood and packed earth or plaster typical of the hill country [bib:rainey-notley-sacred-bridge]. Caves and rock-cut spaces beneath or beside houses were commonly incorporated into the building rather than excavated separately [bib:murphy-oconnor-holy-land-guide].

## Landscape and plants
The surrounding slopes were terraced for olives, grapevines and grain, standard Judean hill-country agriculture [bib:rainey-notley-sacred-bridge]. Shepherds grazed flocks on nearby hillsides, the setting for the Gospel's shepherds who were "in the same country, staying in the fields, and keeping watch by night over their flock" [scripture:Luke 2:8].

## People and daily life
Bethlehem's population in this period were rural Judean villagers and shepherds, dressed in simple wool tunics and cloaks [bib:freedman-anchor-bible-dictionary]. The Gospel narratives describe shepherds, a Roman-ordered census bringing travelers to the town, and later wise men arriving from the east [scripture:Luke 2:4; scripture:Matthew 2:1].

## Not known
The precise layout and boundary of first-century Bethlehem are not well recovered archaeologically, since the town has been continuously inhabited and built over [bib:murphy-oconnor-holy-land-guide]. No first-century structure survives at the traditional birth site beneath the later church to show directly; images should depict a generic cave-and-house dwelling rather than the present-day church interior.
