# Corinth — research brief for AI-generated reconstructions

Period shown: about AD 50–52, Paul's eighteen-month stay (Acts 18:11) under the proconsul Gallio (Acts 18:12), roughly a century after Julius Caesar's refounding of the city.

## Setting and layout

Corinth sits on the narrow isthmus joining central Greece to the Peloponnese, with the steep citadel hill of Acrocorinthus (over 1,800 ft) rising directly south of the city [bib:isbe-corinth]; the city lay on a level site at the very base of the mountain, whose northern face is the steepest [bib:strabo-geography]. Its harbors included Lechaeum on the Corinthian Gulf and Cenchreae on the Saronic Gulf, and smaller ships could be hauled overland across the isthmus on the diolkos, a track between the two harbors, rather than sailing the long way round the Peloponnese [bib:isbe-corinth; bib:worldhistory-corinth]. Cenchreae was a village and harbor about seventy stadia from the city, used for the trade with Asia [bib:strabo-geography]; Acts 18:18 has Paul shave his head there before sailing for Syria [scripture:Acts 18:18]. Mummius destroyed the Greek city in 146 BC, and Julius Caesar refounded it as a Roman colony (in 44 BC, per World History Encyclopedia; ISBE gives 46 BC); by Paul's visit it had grown rapidly into, in ISBE's words, "the metropolis of the Peloponnesus" [pleiades:570182; wikidata:Q1363688; bib:isbe-corinth; bib:worldhistory-corinth].

## What stood then

The Doric Temple of Apollo, built about 550–530 BC with columns each cut from a single stone, already long predated the Roman colony; seven of its columns still stand, so it stood through the Roman period [bib:worldhistory-corinth; bib:isbe-corinth]. The celebrated spring of Peirene lay near the temple; the monumental fountain complex visible there today is 2nd-century AD work [bib:isbe-corinth; bib:worldhistory-corinth]. Most of the other surviving buildings, among them a large forum, date from the 1st century AD onward [bib:worldhistory-corinth]. The city was "thoroughly Roman" by Paul's day, reflected in the Latin names of its believers recorded in Acts and Paul's letters: Crispus, Titus Justus, Gaius, Erastus, Quartus, Fortunatus, Achaicus [bib:isbe-corinth; scripture:Acts 18:7-8; scripture:1 Corinthians 16:17]. Nero's attempt to cut a ship canal through the isthmus did not come until AD 66–67, well after Paul's stay, so no canal works should appear [bib:isbe-corinth].

## Landscape and plants

World History Encyclopedia notes the fertile plains and natural springs around the city, and that Caesar's colony divided the farmland into plots for settlers [bib:worldhistory-corinth]. From the summit of Acrocorinthus one looks north across the gulf to the snow-clad peaks of Parnassus and Helicon [bib:strabo-geography]. The sanctuary of Isthmian Poseidon, where the Isthmian Games were held, stood in a grove of pine trees on the isthmus near the Saronic Gulf [bib:strabo-geography; bib:isbe-corinth].

## People and daily life

A busy trade and manufacturing city: Jews "flocked" to Corinth for commerce, and Paul worked there with the tentmakers Priscilla and Aquila (Acts 18:1-3) [bib:isbe-corinth; scripture:Acts 18:1-3]. Paul later stood before Gallio's judgment seat when accused by the city's Jewish community (Acts 18:12) [scripture:Acts 18:12].
