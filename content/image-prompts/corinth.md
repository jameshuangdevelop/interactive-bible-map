# Corinth — research brief for AI-generated reconstructions

Period shown: about AD 50–52, Paul's eighteen-month stay (Acts 18:11) under the proconsul Gallio (Acts 18:12), roughly a century after Julius Caesar's refounding of the city.

## Setting and layout

Corinth sits on the narrow isthmus joining central Greece to the Peloponnese, with the steep citadel hill of Acrocorinthus (over 1,800 ft) rising directly south of the city [bib:isbe-corinth]. It commanded two harbors, Lechaeum on the Corinthian Gulf and Cenchreae on the Saronic Gulf, and smaller ships could be hauled across the isthmus rather than sailing the long way round the Peloponnese [bib:isbe-corinth]; Paul himself sailed from Cenchreae (Acts 18:18) [scripture:Acts 18:18]. Julius Caesar refounded the Greek city, which Mummius had destroyed in 146 BC, as a Roman colony in 44 BC, and by Paul's visit it had grown rapidly into, in Pleiades's description, a major commercial hub on the isthmus and, per the International Standard Bible Encyclopedia, "the metropolis of the Peloponnesus" [pleiades:570182; wikidata:Q1363688; bib:isbe-corinth].

## What stood then

An archaic Temple of Apollo, built around the 6th century BC on a rise overlooking the city, already long predated the Roman colony; its surviving Doric columns were the main structure still visible above ground to later travelers, confirming it stood through the Roman period [bib:isbe-corinth]. The celebrated spring-house of Peirene stood nearby [bib:isbe-corinth]. The city was "thoroughly Roman" by Paul's day, reflected in the Latin names of its believers recorded in Acts and Paul's letters: Crispus, Titus Justus, Gaius, Erastus, Quartus, Fortunatus, Achaicus [bib:isbe-corinth; scripture:Acts 18:7-8; scripture:1 Corinthians 16:17]. Nero's attempt to cut a ship canal through the isthmus did not come until AD 66–67, well after Paul's stay, so no canal works should appear [bib:isbe-corinth].

## Materials and colours

The older Greek-period Temple of Apollo used local limestone columns; Roman-period rebuilding favored brick-faced concrete with painted stucco and marble revetment typical of a prosperous colony [bib:isbe-corinth; bib:worldhistory-corinth].

## Landscape and plants

The isthmus plain lies between two gulfs, with the sea visible on both sides from Acrocorinthus; the countryside shows the dry Mediterranean scrub, olives, and vines typical of the Peloponnese [bib:isbe-corinth]. The Isthmian Games' sanctuary lay just outside the city near the Saronic shore [bib:isbe-corinth].

## People and daily life

A busy trade and manufacturing city: Jews "flocked" to Corinth for commerce, and Paul worked there with the tentmakers Priscilla and Aquila (Acts 18:1-3) [bib:isbe-corinth; scripture:Acts 18:1-3]. Paul later stood before Gallio's judgment seat when accused by the city's Jewish community (Acts 18:12) [scripture:Acts 18:12].
