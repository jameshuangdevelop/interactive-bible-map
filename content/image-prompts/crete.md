## Setting and layout

Crete is a long island, 156 miles long and 7 to 30 miles broad, lying south of the Aegean; its center is a mountain chain rising to 8,193 ft in Mt Ida, "fringed with low valleys beside the coast", and it has, per ISBE, "one or two good harbors" [bib:isbe-crete; wikidata:Q34374].

## What stood then

Modern excavations at Knossos and Phaistos show that Crete was a center of Mediterranean civilization in an early age [bib:isbe-crete]. ISBE names its most important cities as Knossos, Gortyna near the Gulf of Messara, and Cydonia [bib:isbe-crete]. Crete's help to the Cilician pirates gave Rome its pretext for war; Metellus annexed the island in 67 BC, and it was joined with Cyrene as one Roman province [bib:isbe-crete]. On Paul's voyage to Rome, the ship sailed under the lee of Crete and put in at Fair Havens, "near the city of Lasea", a harbor somewhat east of Cape Matala and near Phaistos; the haven was "not suitable to winter in", so the ship set out for Phoenix, farther west, and was struck by a northeast wind that ISBE calls Euraquilo and the WEB "Euroclydon", which drove it out to sea past the island of Cauda (Acts 27:7-16) [bib:isbe-crete; scripture:Acts 27:7; scripture:Acts 27:8; scripture:Acts 27:12-16]. ISBE dates the voyage to AD 59 [bib:isbe-crete].

## Landscape and plants

The mountain chain drops to low valleys beside the coast, and several districts are very fertile [bib:isbe-crete]. The sources used here do not name the crops. ISBE puts the ship at Fair Havens in early October, "in the middle of the season when the equinoxes made sailing impossible", and Acts says a stormy wind "beat down from shore" (Acts 27:14) [bib:isbe-crete; scripture:Acts 27:14].

## People and daily life

Jews had lived in Crete for generations: ISBE notes that in 141 BC the Cretan Jews were influential enough to secure Rome's patronage, and Cretans are named among those present at Pentecost (Acts 2:11) [bib:isbe-crete; scripture:Acts 2:11]. Paul quotes a Cretan poet's proverb about the islanders' reputation for being "liars, evil beasts, and idle gluttons" (Titus 1:12) [scripture:Titus 1:12].
