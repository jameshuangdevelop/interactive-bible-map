# *The Mount of Olives* — research brief for AI-generated reconstructions

## Setting and layout
The Mount of Olives is the ridge east of Jerusalem's Old City, separated from the Temple Mount by the Kidron Valley, with its summit directly overlooking the Temple precinct [wikidata:Q205976; openbible:mount-of-olives]. Its eastern slope descends toward Bethany and Bethphage and, further on, the Judean wilderness that falls toward Jericho and the Jordan Valley [bib:rainey-notley-sacred-bridge]. Travellers from Jericho crossed this ridge to reach Jerusalem: the Gospels describe Jesus leaving Jericho and then reaching Bethphage, at the Mount of Olives, before entering the city [scripture:Matthew 20:29; scripture:Matthew 21:1].

## What stood then
The Mount of Olives was not built up with monuments in this period: no source describes temples, churches or other major structures there, and the later Byzantine churches of the Ascension and Eleona were built centuries afterward and should not appear in a reconstruction. The valley at its base, the Kidron, already held monumental rock-cut tombs by the first century, including ones Josephus names in passing when describing nearby city walls, consistent with the area's long use as a burial ground [bib:josephus-jewish-war].

## Materials and colours
The slope was terraced with dry-stone retaining walls, in pale local limestone; terracing hillsides for cultivation was standard practice in this hill country [bib:isbe-agriculture; bib:rainey-notley-sacred-bridge]. Its name reflects its most visible planting: olive trees, grey-green and gnarled, covering the terraces [wikidata:Q205976].

## Landscape and plants
Olive groves dominated the slope, with other fruit trees including figs also present; the Gospels record Jesus encountering a fig tree on this route near Bethany [scripture:Mark 11:12-13]. The hillside would have looked brown and dry through the long rainless summer and greener after the autumn and winter rains, typical of this climate zone [bib:rainey-notley-sacred-bridge].

## People and daily life
Travellers coming up from Jericho, like the great multitude that left Jericho with Jesus, passed the villages of Bethphage and Bethany on its slopes [scripture:Mark 10:46; scripture:Mark 11:1]. Jesus and his disciples went to the mount regularly, and Luke says he spent his nights there during his last days in Jerusalem [scripture:Luke 22:39; scripture:Luke 21:37].

## Key sites to show
1. The ridge's summit view across the Kidron Valley to the Temple Mount — one of the most recognizable sightlines in the region.
2. Terraced olive groves on the slope, in pale stone-walled terraces.
3. The Kidron Valley's rock-cut tombs at the base of the ridge.

## Not known
No source describes any built structure standing on the summit or slopes themselves in this period; images should show only terraced cultivation and open hillside, not any identifiable building. The precise field boundaries and the full extent of the Second Temple-period burial ground in the Kidron Valley are not completely mapped.
