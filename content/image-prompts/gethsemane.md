# *Gethsemane* — research brief for AI-generated reconstructions

## Setting and layout
Gethsemane lay at the foot of the Mount of Olives, across the Kidron Valley from the Temple Mount, a short walk from the city [wikidata:Q205333; openbible:gethsemane]. The name derives from an Aramaic or Hebrew phrase meaning an olive-oil press, consistent with its traditional identification as a working olive grove rather than an ornamental garden [bib:bdag-greek-lexicon; openbible:gethsemane]. The exact plot described in the Gospels is not fixed: two distinct modern sites, about 200 meters apart, each claim the location, and no first-century boundary of "the garden" is archaeologically established [bib:murphy-oconnor-holy-land-guide].

## What stood then
In Jesus' time the site held no permanent building described in any source — only the garden itself, likely terraced, with an olive press of the kind the name implies [openbible:gethsemane; bib:bdag-greek-lexicon]. Both the Franciscan-custodied Church of All Nations and the Russian Orthodox church that mark the area today were built many centuries later and should not appear in a reconstruction of this period.

## Materials and colours
Olive trees in this region grow as gnarled, grey-green, multi-trunked specimens, often with hollow, fissured trunks as they age [bib:bernabei-gethsemane-olive-trees]. The ground is the pale Jerusalem-area limestone common throughout the Kidron Valley, likely terraced with dry-stone retaining walls typical of hillside groves in the Judean hill country [bib:rainey-notley-sacred-bridge].

## Landscape and plants
The setting is a mature olive orchard on a valley-floor or lower-slope terrace, with the Mount of Olives rising behind it and the Kidron Valley's dry streambed nearby [wikidata:Q205333]. The Gospels set the key scene at night: Jesus prayed there with his disciples on the night of his arrest, and the arresting party arrived carrying lanterns and torches [scripture:Matthew 26:36; scripture:John 18:3].

## People and daily life
Jesus came with a small group of disciples, leaving most of them at one point to pray further apart [scripture:Mark 14:32]. The arresting party that came for him included an armed crowd with the chief priests' officers, carrying lanterns, torches and weapons [scripture:John 18:3].

## Not known
Radiocarbon dating of the oldest surviving olive trees now standing in the traditional Gethsemane garden found that the three trees that could be successfully dated all began growing in the twelfth century, during the Crusader period — centuries after Jesus' time — so today's trees are not witnesses to the Gospel events, and whether they regrew from older first-century root stock (as olives can do) is not established [bib:bernabei-gethsemane-olive-trees]. Which, if either, of the two modern denominational plots matches the Gospels' garden is also not settled [bib:murphy-oconnor-holy-land-guide]. Images should treat the oldest-looking trees as suggestive of great age in general rather than claiming they are the very trees of the Gospel account.
