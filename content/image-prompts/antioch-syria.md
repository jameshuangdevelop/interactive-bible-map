# Antioch on the Orontes — research brief for AI-generated reconstructions

Period shown: about AD 47–48, the years Antioch served as the base from which Barnabas and Saul were sent out on the first missionary journey (Acts 13:1-3), after the city had been under direct Roman rule for a century.

## Setting and layout

Antioch stood on the left (east) bank of the Orontes river about 15 miles from the Mediterranean, overlooked by Mount Silpius, with its outlet port Seleucia Pieria at the river mouth and the famous pleasure-suburb of Daphne about five miles away [bib:isbe-antioch-syria; pleiades:658381; wikidata:Q200441].

## What stood then

Founded around 300 BC by Seleucus I Nicator, Antioch had grown by Paul's day into the "queen of the East," the third city of the Roman world after Rome and Alexandria, enlarged by successive Seleucid kings and then adorned further after Pompey annexed Syria to Rome in 64 BC [bib:isbe-antioch-syria; pleiades:658381]. Decades before Paul, Herod the Great paved the city's great central street — about twenty furlongs long, roughly 2.3 miles — with polished marble and lined it on both sides with covered colonnades of the same length, replacing a street that, in Josephus's words, had before been "shunned by all men... because it was full of dirt and filthiness" [bib:josephus-jewish-war]. The city held a large, privileged Jewish community dating to its founding, alongside Greek and (by now) Roman populations [bib:isbe-antioch-syria]. It was from this city's church that Barnabas and Saul were sent out, and here the disciples were first called "Christians" (Acts 11:26) [scripture:Acts 11:26; scripture:Acts 13:1-3].

## Materials and colours

The city's defining feature was its polished-marble colonnaded street, flanked the full length by stone porticoes [bib:josephus-jewish-war].

## Landscape and plants

Mount Silpius rises directly behind the city above the Orontes; the suburb of Daphne, per the Encyclopedia, was "beautified by groves and fountains" and adorned with temples and baths [bib:isbe-antioch-syria].

## People and daily life

Ancient writers described Antiochenes as commercially sharp, pleasure-loving, and quick-witted; the large resident Jewish colony offered an early field for Christian teaching among a cosmopolitan Greek-Syrian-Roman population [bib:isbe-antioch-syria].
