# Antioch on the Orontes — research brief for AI-generated reconstructions

Period shown: about AD 47–48, the years Antioch served as the base from which Barnabas and Saul were sent out on the first missionary journey (Acts 13:1-3), after the city had been under direct Roman rule for a century.

## Setting and layout

Antioch stood on the left bank of the Orontes river about 15 miles from the sea, with a mountain (Barrington's Silpion Oros) rising behind it [bib:isbe-antioch-syria; pleiades:658381; pleiades:658598; wikidata:Q200441]. Seleucia, near the river's outlets, was its port, and inland voyages from the sea up to Antioch took a single day [bib:isbe-antioch-syria; bib:strabo-geography]. The suburb of Daphne lay about five miles away [bib:isbe-antioch-syria]. Strabo describes Antioch as four settlements, each with its own wall inside a common wall [bib:strabo-geography].

## What stood then

Founded about 300 BC by Seleucus I Nicator, Antioch had grown by Paul's day into the "queen of the East," the third city of the Roman world after Rome and Alexandria, enlarged by successive Seleucid kings and made still more splendid by its Roman patrons after Pompey annexed Syria in 64 BC; it was the capital of the Roman province of Syria [bib:isbe-antioch-syria; pleiades:658381]. Decades before Paul, Herod the Great paved the broad street that ran through the city lengthways, twenty furlongs long, with polished stone, and built cloisters along it on both sides; in Josephus's words the place had before been "shunned by all men... because it was full of dirt and filthiness" [bib:josephus-jewish-war; bib:josephus-antiquities]. Seleucus had settled numbers of Jews in the city with equal rights with the Greeks; Syrians, Greeks, Jews and later Romans made up its population [bib:isbe-antioch-syria]. It was from this city's church that Barnabas and Saul were sent out, and here the disciples were first called "Christians" (Acts 11:26) [scripture:Acts 11:26; scripture:Acts 13:1-3].

## Materials and colours

Josephus describes the street's pavement as polished marble, with a cloister along it of the same length [bib:josephus-jewish-war; bib:josephus-antiquities]. The sources used here do not describe the city's other building materials or colours.

## Landscape and plants

The suburb of Daphne, sacred to Apollo and Artemis, was "beautified by groves and fountains" and embellished with temples and baths [bib:isbe-antioch-syria]; Strabo describes "a large, thickly-shaded grove intersected by fountain-streams", with a temple of Apollo and Artemis [bib:strabo-geography].

## People and daily life

Antioch was cosmopolitan from its foundation and absorbed much of the trade of the Levant; Daphne was the city's pleasure resort, and "Daphnic morals" became a byword [bib:isbe-antioch-syria].
