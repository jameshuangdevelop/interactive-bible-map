# Damascus — research brief for AI-generated reconstructions

Period shown: about AD 33–37, the years around Paul's conversion near Damascus and his early preaching there (Acts 9:1-25), when the city was for a time under Nabataean rather than direct Roman control.

## Setting and layout

Damascus lies in the fertile Ghuta oasis plain northwest of the Syrian desert, watered by the river Barada (the biblical Abana), with bare hills on three sides and open desert to the east [bib:isbe-damascus; pleiades:678106; wikidata:Q3678596]. The walled city itself was about a mile long and half a mile broad [bib:isbe-damascus].

## What stood then

Already one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world, Damascus had been part of the Roman province of Syria since Pompey's conquest in 64 BC, but around the time of Paul's visit it was governed, at least in part, by an ethnarch appointed by the Nabataean king Aretas IV — the same official who, Paul writes, guarded the city gates to arrest him (2 Corinthians 11:32) [bib:isbe-damascus; bib:isbe-aretas; bib:livius-nabataeans; bib:livius-damascus; scripture:2 Corinthians 11:32]. A long colonnaded street ran east–west through the Greek-period city; part of it is still identifiable along the course of the "street called Straight" named in Acts, with ancient column remains traced along its route [bib:isbe-damascus; scripture:Acts 9:11].

## Materials and colours

The typical dwelling was a flat-roofed house built around an internal courtyard with a fountain [bib:isbe-damascus].

## Landscape and plants

The Ghuta's orchards of apricot, pomegranate, and walnut trees, fed by the Barada's many channels, were long celebrated by Arab writers as an earthly paradise, with bare desert hills visible beyond the green plain [bib:isbe-damascus].

## People and daily life

Damascus was already a manufacturing and caravan-trade city; Paul came bearing letters to "the synagogues of Damascus," implying an organized resident Jewish community there, among whom he began to preach almost immediately after his sight was restored (Acts 9:2, 9:20) [bib:isbe-damascus; scripture:Acts 9:2; scripture:Acts 9:20].

## Key sites to show

1. The Barada river and its orchard oasis set against the desert backdrop [bib:isbe-damascus].
2. The colonnaded Straight Street running through the city [bib:isbe-damascus; scripture:Acts 9:11].
3. A city wall and gate, evoking Paul's night escape lowered in a basket over the wall (2 Corinthians 11:33) [scripture:2 Corinthians 11:33].
4. A flat-roofed courtyard-house quarter [bib:isbe-damascus].

## Not known

No source used here gives a block-by-block reconstruction of mid-1st-century Damascus, since its ancient street plan lies beneath the much later Old City; beyond Straight Street's course and the oasis setting, building façades should stay generic Greco-Roman Syrian rather than copy any surviving but much later structure, such as the Great Mosque, which only reached its present form centuries after Paul [bib:isbe-damascus].
