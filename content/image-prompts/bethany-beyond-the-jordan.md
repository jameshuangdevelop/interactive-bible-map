# *Bethany beyond the Jordan* — research brief for AI-generated reconstructions

## Setting and layout
John's Gospel names "Bethany beyond the Jordan" as the place where John the Baptist was baptizing, including Jesus [scripture:John 1:28]. The site lies east of the Jordan River, in the Jordan Valley, flanked by the Judean hills to the west and the mountains of Moab and Perea to the east [bib:hutton-bethany-beyond-jordan]. The leading candidate, Al-Maghtas/Wadi al-Kharrar on the river's east bank, is supported by Byzantine-period churches, hermit caves and baptismal structures that match the site's depiction on the sixth-century Madaba mosaic map [bib:unesco-baptism-site-al-maghtas]. A second candidate, Qasr al-Yahud, lies on the opposite, west bank, with its own long tradition of Christian commemoration but less archaeological investigation of a first-century date [wikidata:Q1574073]. This brief, and any image, treats the two banks neutrally rather than favoring either.

## What stood then
No fixed settlement is attested at the baptism site itself in this period: even the third-century scholar Origen could not find a village called Bethany east of the Jordan in his own day, and proposed the area was instead known by the name "Bethabara," a reading that entered some later manuscripts [bib:hutton-bethany-beyond-jordan; scripture:John 1:28]. The likeliest picture is a riverbank location near a ford or crossing point, not a built-up town. The Byzantine churches and chapels later built at both Al-Maghtas and Qasr al-Yahud belong to a period centuries after Jesus and should not appear in a reconstruction of this period [bib:unesco-baptism-site-al-maghtas].

## Materials and colours
The Jordan at this point is a modest, often muddy or greenish river, lined with dense riparian vegetation that stands out as a green ribbon against the surrounding tan desert hills [bib:hutton-bethany-beyond-jordan].

## Landscape and plants
Tamarisk, willow and reed beds typically line this stretch of the Jordan, with the surrounding Jordan Valley otherwise dry scrubland and desert [bib:rainey-notley-sacred-bridge]. Date palms may have grown near any springs, consistent with the wider Jordan Valley's oasis vegetation pattern seen at Jericho [bib:josephus-jewish-war].

## People and daily life
John the Baptist's clothing is described as "camel's hair, with a leather belt around his waist," and his food as "locusts and wild honey," a deliberately austere, wilderness appearance [scripture:Matthew 3:4]. Crowds came out from Jerusalem, all Judea and the region around the Jordan to be baptized by him [scripture:Matthew 3:5].

## Key sites to show
1. A wide Jordan River bend lined with reeds and tamarisks, desert hills rising on both sides, without favoring either bank.
2. A riverside baptism scene framed by riparian vegetation, generic rather than tied to either candidate's modern site.
3. The contrast between the green river corridor and the surrounding dry Jordan Valley hills.

## Not known
Which bank of the river, if either exactly, hosted the Gospel events is not settled, nor is it certain that "Bethany" named any fixed hamlet at all rather than a general locality [bib:hutton-bethany-beyond-jordan]. The river's ancient course may have shifted over the centuries, so today's riverbed does not necessarily match the first-century channel precisely. No first-century structure is attested at either site; images should show only the natural riverside setting.
