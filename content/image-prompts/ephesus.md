# Ephesus — research brief for AI-generated reconstructions

Period shown: about AD 52–55, Paul's roughly three-year stay in Ephesus (Acts 19:8-10; 20:31), decades before the city's best-known surviving monuments were built.

## Setting and layout

Ephesus stood near the mouth of the Cayster river, on the sloping sides and at the base of two hills, Prion and Coressus, about three miles from the Aegean coast opposite Samos, with an artificial harbor accessible to the largest ships [bib:isbe-ephesus; pleiades:599612; wikidata:Q47611]. Its position made it, per the International Standard Bible Encyclopedia, "the most easily accessible city in Asia, both by land and sea," connected by highways to the interior of Asia Minor [bib:isbe-ephesus].

## What stood then

The city's greatest landmark was the Temple of Artemis (the Artemision), one of the Seven Wonders of the ancient world, rebuilt over roughly 220 years after a 356 BC fire; nineteenth-century excavation found its platform reached by ten steps, the building itself 425 ft long and 220 ft wide, with 127 columns 60 ft high and an interior open to the sky [bib:isbe-ephesus]. Acts 19:35 confirms the city's own pride in guarding this temple [scripture:Acts 19:35]. The Hellenistic city wall built under Lysimachus ran about 36,000 ft, enclosing over 1,000 acres, pierced by six gates and strengthened by towers every 100 ft [bib:isbe-ephesus]. A huge theatre cut into the hillside was the scene of the riot Acts describes, when the crowd shouted for two hours (Acts 19:29, 19:34) [bib:isbe-ephesus; scripture:Acts 19:29]. The colonnaded Library of Celsus and the marble-paved harbor street most associated with Ephesus today belong to the later Roman Imperial period and must not appear in a Pauline-era scene [bib:unesco-ephesus].

## Materials and colours

The temple's stone columns were partly the gift of the wealthy king Croesus centuries earlier [bib:isbe-ephesus]; the theatre and harbor installations were built of worked local stone.

## Landscape and plants

The river valley soil was unusually fertile and the climate, per the Encyclopedia, "exceptionally fine," with views from the slopes of Prion and Coressus down to the harbor and sea [bib:isbe-ephesus].

## People and daily life

Pilgrims from across the Mediterranean supported a whole trade in silver shrines of Artemis, the livelihood Demetrius the silversmith defends in Acts 19 [scripture:Acts 19:24-27]. Paul taught daily in the lecture hall of Tyrannus (Acts 19:9) [scripture:Acts 19:9].

## Key sites to show

1. The Temple of Artemis on its stepped platform [bib:isbe-ephesus].
2. The great hillside theatre [bib:isbe-ephesus; scripture:Acts 19:29].
3. The harbor and the road leading to it [bib:isbe-ephesus].
4. The Hellenistic city wall and one of its gates [bib:isbe-ephesus].

## Not known

The exact decorative state of the Artemision's sculpture in Paul's specific years is not detailed by the sources used here and should stay generic. The Library of Celsus, the fully marble-paved harbor street (the "Arcadiane"), and other monuments UNESCO groups as "Roman Imperial period" post-date Paul's visit and must not appear in an AD 50s reconstruction [bib:unesco-ephesus].
