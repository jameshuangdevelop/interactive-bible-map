# Ephesus — research brief for AI-generated reconstructions

Period shown: about AD 52–55, Paul's roughly three-year stay in Ephesus (Acts 19:8-10; 20:31), decades before the city's best-known surviving monuments were built.

## Setting and layout

Ephesus stood near the mouth of the Cayster river, on the sloping sides and at the base of two hills, Prion and Coressus, about three miles from the Aegean coast opposite Samos, with an artificial harbor accessible to the largest ships [bib:isbe-ephesus; pleiades:599612; wikidata:Q47611]. Its position made it, per the International Standard Bible Encyclopedia, "the most easily accessible city in Asia, both by land and sea," connected by highways to the interior of Asia Minor [bib:isbe-ephesus].

## What stood then

The city's greatest landmark was the Temple of Artemis (the Artemision), one of the Seven Wonders of the ancient world, rebuilt over roughly 220 years after a 356 BC fire; nineteenth-century excavation found its platform reached by ten steps, the building itself 425 ft long and 220 ft wide, with 127 columns 60 ft high and an interior open to the sky [bib:isbe-ephesus]. Acts 19:35 confirms the city's own pride in guarding this temple [scripture:Acts 19:35]. The Hellenistic city wall built under Lysimachus ran about 36,000 ft, enclosing over 1,000 acres, pierced by six gates and strengthened by towers every 100 ft [bib:isbe-ephesus]. A huge theatre in the side of the hill Prion was the scene of the riot Acts describes, when the crowd shouted for two hours (Acts 19:29, 19:34) [bib:isbe-ephesus; scripture:Acts 19:29]. UNESCO describes the grand monuments lining the old processional way, among them the Library of Celsus and the terrace houses, as Roman Imperial [bib:unesco-ephesus]. The Library of Celsus was built in the 2nd century AD, after Paul's stay, and must not appear in a Pauline-era scene [bib:oeaw-celsus-library].

## Materials and colours

An earlier temple on the same site had received many of its stone columns from King Croesus; that temple burned in 356 BC and was rebuilt [bib:isbe-ephesus]. The theatre's marble facing has since been torn away [bib:isbe-ephesus]. The sources used here do not describe the other buildings' materials, so they should stay generic.

## Landscape and plants

The river valley soil was unusually fertile and the climate, per the Encyclopedia, "exceptionally fine," with views from the slopes of Prion and Coressus down to the harbor and sea [bib:isbe-ephesus].

## People and daily life

UNESCO notes that the Temple of Artemis attracted pilgrims from across the Mediterranean world [bib:unesco-ephesus]. Demetrius the silversmith "made silver shrines of Artemis" and "brought no little business to the craftsmen", and claimed that "all Asia and the world" worshiped her (Acts 19:24-27) [scripture:Acts 19:24-27]. Paul was "reasoning daily in the school of Tyrannus" (Acts 19:9) [scripture:Acts 19:9].
