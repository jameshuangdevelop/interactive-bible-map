# Philippi — research brief for AI-generated reconstructions

Period shown: about AD 49–50, Paul's first visit (Acts 16:12-40), when Philippi was a young Roman colony about eight decades removed from the famous 42 BC battle fought on its plain.

## Setting and layout

Philippi lay inland on a fertile, partly marshy plain of eastern Macedonia, on the Via Egnatia, bounded by mountains to the east and north and by Mt Pangaeus to the west, linked to its port Neapolis nine miles south over the pass of Mt Symbolum [bib:isbe-philippi; pleiades:501482; wikidata:Q379652].

## What stood then

Refounded by Philip II of Macedon in 358 BC to work the district's gold mines, the settlement had sunk, per Strabo (as summarized in the Encyclopedia), to "a small settlement" by Julius Caesar's day, before the decisive battle of 42 BC — in which Octavian and Antony defeated Brutus and Cassius on this plain — led to its refounding as the Roman colony Colonia Iulia Philippensis, reinforced after Actium (31 BC) as Colonia Augusta Iulia (Victrix) Philippensium, with the tax-exempting ius Italicum [bib:isbe-philippi]. By Paul's visit it was, as Acts says, "a city of Macedonia, the foremost of the district, a Roman colony" (Acts 16:12), with a forum, magistrates attended by lictors, and a prison (Acts 16:19-24, 16:35-38) [scripture:Acts 16:12; scripture:Acts 16:19-24; bib:isbe-philippi]. The grand marble forum, theatre renovations, and octagonal church that visitors see at the site today were built mostly from the 2nd century AD onward; the Encyclopedia itself notes almost nothing is recorded of the three centuries between Philip II's refounding and Paul's visit, so Paul's Philippi was a smaller, earlier colonial town, not the later rebuilt forum [bib:isbe-philippi].

## Materials and colours

As a Roman colony, Philippi was built, in Aulus Gellius's phrase quoted by the Encyclopedia, as a kind of "miniature and picture of the Roman people" — a colonial townscape rather than a Greek one [bib:isbe-philippi].

## Landscape and plants

The plain is still partly marsh today as in antiquity, crossed by the river the Greeks called the Gangites or Angites, where Paul and his companions went on the Sabbath to find a place of prayer by the riverbank (Acts 16:13) [bib:isbe-philippi; scripture:Acts 16:13].

## People and daily life

Unlike most cities on Paul's route, Philippi had very few resident Jews and no synagogue; the small group that gathered for prayer met instead on the riverbank, and most were women, some proselytes [bib:isbe-philippi; scripture:Acts 16:13]. Among them was Lydia, a dealer in purple cloth from Thyatira in Asia Minor (Acts 16:14) [scripture:Acts 16:14].
