# Galatia — research brief for AI-generated reconstructions

Galatia is an area, not a single city. This brief covers the highland landscape of the Roman province and the southern cities named in its own record's history: Antioch in Pisidia, Iconium, Lystra, and Derbe [pleiades:619161; wikidata:Q26847; wikidata:Q1249412; bib:zondervan-who-were-galatians]. Scholars are divided, without a clear consensus, on whether Paul's letter to "the assemblies of Galatia" addressed the ethnic-Celtic north or these southern cities, and this project takes neither side; the images for this brief should likewise not be read as settling that question [pleiades:619161; wikidata:Q26847; wikidata:Q1249412; bib:zondervan-who-were-galatians; scripture:Galatians 1:2].

## Setting and layout

Geographically, "Galatia" named a highland country in the north-central Anatolian plateau, around the headwaters of the Sangarios and the middle Halys rivers, settled by Celtic migrants after 278 BC [bib:isbe-galatia]. Politically, the much larger Roman province of the same name (from 25 BC) reached south to very different terrain [bib:isbe-galatia; bib:zondervan-who-were-galatians]. Antioch in Pisidia stands on high ground at the edge of the Pisidian mountains [bib:isbe-pisidia]. Iconium (modern Konya) lies on a Phrygian tableland reaching about 4,000 ft, at the boundary of Phrygia and Lycaonia [bib:isbe-phrygia; bib:isbe-lycaonia]. Lystra and Derbe lie out on the flat, waterless, treeless Lycaonian plain, broken by the volcanic mass of Kara Dagh, where Strabo records that the Galatian king Amyntas pastured large flocks of sheep [bib:isbe-lycaonia].

## What stood then

Antioch in Pisidia was refounded under Augustus as the Roman colony Colonia Caesarea, garrisoned and linked by military roads to sister colonies (Cremna, Comama, Olbasa, Parlais) planted to pacify the highlands [bib:isbe-pisidia]. Iconium and Lystra were likewise provincial towns of the Roman south-Galatian regions of Phrygia and Lycaonia by Paul's day [bib:isbe-lycaonia]. Derbe was a minor Lycaonian town whose precise ruins are still debated by modern scholarship [bib:wagner-wilson-why-derbe].

## Materials and colours

Highland towns were built of local stone under the big open sky typical of the Anatolian plateau; Phrygia's wider countryside is dotted with rock-cut tombs carved into the region's soft volcanic rock [bib:isbe-phrygia].

## Landscape and plants

The northern Celtic homeland is temperate highland steppe [bib:isbe-galatia]. The southern cities range from Pisidia's forested mountain slopes to Lycaonia's dry, nearly treeless plain used mainly for grazing sheep and goats [bib:isbe-pisidia; bib:isbe-lycaonia].

## People and daily life

Paul and Barnabas preached first in the synagogues of Antioch in Pisidia and Iconium before turning to Gentile hearers (Acts 13:14; 14:1) [scripture:Acts 13:14; scripture:Acts 14:1]. At Lystra, which had no synagogue tradition like the others, a crowd hailed Paul and Barnabas as Hermes and Zeus in the local Lycaonian speech after a healing (Acts 14:11) [scripture:Acts 14:11]. The region's population mixed Phrygian and Lycaonian natives, Greek colonists, and Roman veterans [bib:isbe-lycaonia].

## Key sites to show

2. Antioch in Pisidia's hillside Roman-colony townscape at the edge of the mountains [bib:isbe-pisidia].
3. Iconium at the Phrygian–Lycaonian boundary on its high tableland [bib:isbe-phrygia; bib:isbe-lycaonia].
4. The flat Lycaonian plain around Lystra and Derbe, with the volcanic Kara Dagh hills on the horizon [bib:isbe-lycaonia].

## Not known

Whether Paul's Galatian letter addresses the northern homeland or the southern cities is a live scholarly dispute that this project does not resolve [bib:zondervan-who-were-galatians]. Little controlled excavation grounds a precise, building-by-building reconstruction of 1st-century Antioch in Pisidia, Iconium, Lystra, or Derbe in the sources consulted here, so images should keep specific architecture generic — Roman-colonial in form, but without invented decorative detail — beyond the broad layout these sources describe [bib:isbe-pisidia; bib:isbe-lycaonia; bib:wagner-wilson-why-derbe].
