# Galatia — research brief for AI-generated reconstructions

Galatia is an area, not a single city. This brief covers the highland landscape of the Roman province and the southern cities named in its own record's summary: Antioch in Pisidia, Iconium, Lystra, and Derbe [pleiades:619161; wikidata:Q26847; wikidata:Q1249412; bib:zondervan-who-were-galatians]. Scholars are divided, without a clear consensus, on whether Paul's letter to "the assemblies of Galatia" addressed the ethnic-Celtic north or these southern cities, and this project takes neither side; the images for this brief should likewise not be read as settling that question [pleiades:619161; wikidata:Q26847; wikidata:Q1249412; bib:zondervan-who-were-galatians; scripture:Galatians 1:2].

## Setting and layout

Geographically, "Galatia" named a highland country in the north-central Anatolian plateau, around the headwaters of the Sangarios and the middle Halys rivers, settled by Celtic migrants after 278 BC [bib:isbe-galatia]. Politically, the much larger Roman province of the same name (from 25 BC) reached south to very different terrain, including parts of Phrygia, Pisidia and Lycaonia [bib:isbe-galatia; bib:zondervan-who-were-galatians]. Antioch in Pisidia stood in a strong position on a plateau beside the river Anthios, which flows down from the Sultan Dagh, near the northern border of Pisidia's mountain country [bib:isbe-antioch-pisidia; bib:isbe-pisidia]. Iconium (modern Konya) lay in the part of Phrygia within the province, a country of mountains and table-lands reaching 4,000 ft; the boundary between Phrygia and Lycaonia passed between Iconium and Lystra [bib:isbe-phrygia; bib:isbe-lycaonia; pleiades:648647]. Lycaonia, the country of Lystra and Derbe (Acts 14:6), is a level plain, waterless and treeless, broken on its eastern side by the volcanic mass of Kara-Dagh; ISBE cites Strabo for the Galatian king Amyntas feeding many flocks of sheep there [bib:isbe-lycaonia; scripture:Acts 14:6].

## What stood then

Not much before 6 BC Antioch was made a Roman colony, Caesareia Antiocheia, the chief of a series of military colonies that Augustus founded to hold down the mountain tribes [bib:isbe-antioch-pisidia]. As the main garrison city, it was linked by military roads to five of these colonies: Cremna, Comama, Olbasa, Parlais and Lystra [bib:isbe-pisidia]. Iconium lay in Galatian Phrygia and Lystra in Lycaonia, both within the Roman province of Galatia [bib:isbe-phrygia; bib:isbe-lycaonia]. Derbe was a minor Lycaonian town whose precise ruins are still debated by modern scholarship [bib:wagner-wilson-why-derbe].

## Landscape and plants

The southern cities range from the edge of Pisidia's "huge block of mountain country" to Lycaonia's level, waterless and treeless plain, where flocks of sheep grazed [bib:isbe-pisidia; bib:isbe-lycaonia]. The sources used here do not describe the vegetation in more detail.

## People and daily life

Paul and Barnabas preached first in the synagogues of Antioch in Pisidia and Iconium before turning to Gentile hearers (Acts 13:14; 14:1) [scripture:Acts 13:14; scripture:Acts 14:1]. Acts mentions no synagogue at Lystra; there, after a healing, the crowd cried out "in the language of Lycaonia" that the gods had come down, calling Barnabas "Jupiter" and Paul "Mercury" (Acts 14:11-12) [scripture:Acts 14:11; scripture:Acts 14:12]. Lystra and Antioch were Roman military colonies [bib:isbe-pisidia; bib:isbe-antioch-pisidia].

## Not known

Whether Paul's Galatian letter addresses the northern homeland or the southern cities is a live scholarly dispute that this project does not resolve [bib:zondervan-who-were-galatians]. Little controlled excavation grounds a precise, building-by-building reconstruction of 1st-century Antioch in Pisidia, Iconium, Lystra, or Derbe in the sources consulted here, so images should keep specific architecture generic — Roman-colonial in form, but without invented decorative detail — beyond the broad layout these sources describe [bib:isbe-pisidia; bib:isbe-lycaonia; bib:wagner-wilson-why-derbe].
