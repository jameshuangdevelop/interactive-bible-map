# *Jerusalem* — research brief for AI-generated reconstructions

## Setting and layout
Jerusalem in about AD 30–60 stood on a cluster of ridges divided by valleys: the Tyropoeon (Cheesemakers') Valley ran through the city's middle, the Kidron Valley bounded it on the east below the Temple Mount, and the Hinnom Valley wrapped the south and west [bib:rainey-notley-sacred-bridge]. For most of this period the city was enclosed by two walls; a third, outer wall was only begun under Herod Agrippa I (AD 41–44), who stopped after laying its massive foundation courses out of fear that Emperor Claudius would read a finished wall as rebellion, and it was not raised to full height until the Jewish defenders rushed to complete it around AD 66, after this project's period [bib:josephus-jewish-war]. The whole circuit, even unfinished, ran about thirty-three furlongs (roughly 6 km), reinforced by square towers roughly every two hundred cubits [bib:josephus-jewish-war]. Three monumental towers next to Herod's palace on the western hill — Hippicus, Phasael and Mariamne, the tallest about eighty cubits — anchored the old wall near today's Citadel [bib:josephus-jewish-war]. The newer northern quarter, "Bezetha," lay across a deliberately cut defensive ditch from the Antonia fortress [bib:josephus-jewish-war; wikidata:Q1218; dare:15896; pleiades:687928].

## What stood then
Herod the Great's rebuilt Temple dominated the eastern ridge; John's Gospel notes it was still being finished forty-six years into construction during Jesus' ministry [scripture:John 2:20]. Herod's royal palace and the three towers stood on the western hill; the Antonia fortress, a Roman garrison post, overlooked the Temple's northwest corner [bib:josephus-jewish-war]. Priestly and upper-class residences occupied the western hill's "Upper City" [bib:rainey-notley-sacred-bridge]. The city fell to Rome and the Temple was destroyed in AD 70, after this period [wikidata:Q1003997].

## Materials and colours
Buildings used local Jerusalem limestone, pale cream to golden-white, often cut as large ashlars with smooth drafted margins, a hallmark of Herodian masonry still visible in the Temple Mount's retaining walls [bib:ritmeyer-temple-mount-quest]. Some Temple-platform foundation stones reached forty cubits [bib:josephus-jewish-war]. Temple cloister columns were single blocks of white marble; their roofs were carved cedar [bib:josephus-jewish-war].

## Landscape and plants
Terraced hillsides around the city carried olives and vines, typical of the Judean hill country [bib:rainey-notley-sacred-bridge]. The Kidron Valley's slope toward the Mount of Olives was planted with olive groves and held rock-cut tombs, including monuments Josephus names near the valley [bib:josephus-jewish-war]. Water came from cisterns, pools (including Siloam) and aqueducts rather than a river [bib:rainey-notley-sacred-bridge].

## People and daily life
At festivals the city filled with pilgrims "from every nation under heaven" [scripture:Acts 2:5]. Priests served in the Temple courts; Roman soldiers watched festival crowds from the Antonia; merchants and money changers worked the outer court [scripture:Matthew 21:12]. Ordinary dress for Judeans was a linen or wool tunic with an outer cloak and sandals [bib:freedman-anchor-bible-dictionary].

## Key sites to show
1. The Temple Mount's massive retaining walls and courts, with the Mount of Olives beyond the Kidron Valley.
2. The western hill's towers (Hippicus, Phasael, Mariamne) beside Herod's palace.
3. A hillside view of the Upper City's limestone houses and terraced approaches.
4. The Kidron Valley with olive groves and rock-cut tombs below the eastern wall.

## Not known
The exact course of the second wall, and therefore exactly which ground lay outside the city at the time of the crucifixion, is debated among scholars [bib:taylor-golgotha-reconsideration]. The street plan and appearance of most residential quarters beyond a few excavated sites are not recovered. Images should keep ordinary streets and houses generic rather than inventing specific unexcavated buildings, and should not show the third wall at full height, since it stood only as foundations for most of this period [bib:josephus-jewish-war].
