# *Jerusalem* — research brief for AI-generated reconstructions

## Setting and layout
Jerusalem in about AD 30–60 stood on a cluster of ridges divided by valleys: the Tyropoeon (Cheesemakers') Valley ran through the city's middle, the Kidron Valley bounded it on the east below the Temple Mount, and the Hinnom Valley wrapped the south and west [bib:rainey-notley-sacred-bridge]. For most of this period the city was enclosed by two walls; a third, outer wall was only begun under Herod Agrippa I (AD 41–44), who stopped after laying its massive foundation courses out of fear that Emperor Claudius would read a finished wall as rebellion; only later did the Jews raise it to its full height of about twenty-five cubits, and it stood complete by the Roman siege of AD 70, after this project's period [bib:josephus-jewish-war]. By that siege the whole circuit ran about thirty-three furlongs (roughly 6 km), with square towers along each wall — ninety on the third wall, set two hundred cubits apart, forty on the middle wall and sixty on the old wall [bib:josephus-jewish-war]. Three monumental towers next to Herod's palace on the western hill — Hippicus, Phasael and Mariamne, about eighty, ninety and fifty cubits high — anchored the old wall near today's Citadel [bib:josephus-jewish-war]. The newer northern quarter, "Bezetha," lay across a deliberately cut defensive ditch from the Antonia fortress [bib:josephus-jewish-war; wikidata:Q1218; dare:15896; pleiades:687928].

## What stood then
Herod the Great's rebuilt Temple dominated the eastern ridge; in John's Gospel, Jesus' hearers say it "took forty-six years to build" [scripture:John 2:20], and Josephus dates the end of the work to the 60s AD, under the governor Albinus [bib:josephus-antiquities]. Herod's royal palace and the three towers stood on the western hill; the Antonia fortress, a Roman garrison post, overlooked the Temple's northwest corner [bib:josephus-jewish-war]. The western hill's "Upper City" held the city's wealthier houses (Rainey and Notley, the Jerusalem chapter) [bib:rainey-notley-sacred-bridge]. The city fell to Rome and the Temple was destroyed in AD 70, after this period [bib:josephus-jewish-war; wikidata:Q1003997].

## Materials and colours
Buildings used local Jerusalem limestone, pale cream to golden-white; Josephus describes the Temple's great foundation stones and the "niceness of the joints, and the beauty of the stones" in Herod's towers, and Ritmeyer's architectural survey identifies the precisely cut, drafted-margin ashlars of this style still visible in the Temple Mount's retaining walls [bib:josephus-jewish-war; bib:ritmeyer-temple-mount-quest]. Some Temple-platform foundation stones reached forty cubits [bib:josephus-jewish-war]. Temple cloister columns were single blocks of white marble; their roofs were carved cedar [bib:josephus-jewish-war].

## Landscape and plants
Terraced hillsides around the city carried olives and vines: terracing hillsides for cultivation was standard practice in this hill country, and old terraces of this kind still survive [bib:isbe-agriculture; bib:rainey-notley-sacred-bridge]. The Kidron Valley below the Mount of Olives held rock-cut tombs and monuments, some of which Josephus names when tracing the walls near the valley [bib:josephus-jewish-war]. Josephus's account of the city's walls also names the fountain of Siloam and "Solomon's pool" along the valley, and water otherwise came from cisterns rather than a river [bib:josephus-jewish-war].

## People and daily life
At festivals the city filled with pilgrims; Acts describes devout Jews dwelling in Jerusalem "from every nation under the sky" [scripture:Acts 2:5]. Priests served in the Temple courts; on festivals the Roman garrison of the Antonia went armed along the cloisters to watch the crowds [bib:josephus-jewish-war]; merchants and money changers worked in the Temple [scripture:Matthew 21:12]. Ordinary dress for Judeans was a linen or wool tunic with an outer cloak and sandals [bib:freedman-anchor-bible-dictionary].

## Key sites to show
1. The Temple Mount's massive retaining walls and courts, with the Mount of Olives beyond the Kidron Valley.
2. The western hill's towers (Hippicus, Phasael, Mariamne) beside Herod's palace.
3. A hillside view of the Upper City's limestone houses and terraced approaches.
4. The Kidron Valley with olive groves and rock-cut tombs below the eastern wall.

## Not known
The exact course of the second wall, and therefore exactly which ground lay outside the city at the time of the crucifixion, is debated among scholars [bib:taylor-golgotha-reconsideration]. The street plan and appearance of most residential quarters beyond a few excavated sites are not recovered. Images should keep ordinary streets and houses generic rather than inventing specific unexcavated buildings, and should not show the third wall at full height, since it stood only as foundations for most of this period [bib:josephus-jewish-war].
