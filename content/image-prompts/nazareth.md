# *Nazareth* — research brief for AI-generated reconstructions

## Setting and layout
Nazareth was a small village in the hills of Lower Galilee, set in a natural bowl among the surrounding ridges [wikidata:Q430776; dare:22899; pleiades:678299]. It lay about 6 km from Sepphoris, a much larger city that Herod Antipas walled and made the chief city of Galilee in the early first century; Josephus calls it "the security of all Galilee" and says Antipas "made it the metropolis of the country" [bib:josephus-antiquities; wikidata:Q1132798; pleiades:678299]. Excavations around the Basilica of the Annunciation and the nearby Sisters of Nazareth Convent site place the ancient village's core within a small area around those locations; the Franciscan custodians who conducted the Basilica-area excavations describe "an agricultural village... gradually structured with simple dwellings constructed around caves used for their domestic work and for the recovery of animals" [bib:custodia-nazareth; bib:dark-nazareth-archaeology].

## What stood then
Archaeological work at both sites has found cisterns, grain-storage pits and dwellings built around natural caves, consistent with a small agricultural village; the Franciscan excavators also found tombs in the area, though theirs are dated to the Middle Bronze Age rather than the Roman period, so this evidence speaks to the village's general caves-and-cisterns building tradition rather than to Roman-period tombs specifically [bib:custodia-nazareth; bib:dark-nazareth-archaeology]. No public building, synagogue or civic structure has been confirmed archaeologically for this period at the site; Luke says Jesus "entered, as was his custom, into the synagogue on the Sabbath day" at Nazareth, but its physical form is not recovered [scripture:Luke 4:16; bib:dark-nazareth-archaeology].

## Materials and colours
Houses combined rough local limestone walls, often incorporating natural caves cut into the hillside as additional rooms, with flat roofs of timber beams and packed earth — ISBE's general account of the region's houses describes the same progression from cave dwelling to stone hut with "a roof of brushwood and mud" — typical of small Galilean villages of the period [bib:isbe-house; bib:dark-nazareth-archaeology].

## Landscape and plants
The village was surrounded by terraced agricultural slopes carrying olives, vines and grain, typical of Galilean hill farming; terracing hillsides for cultivation was standard practice in this country [bib:isbe-agriculture; bib:rainey-notley-sacred-bridge]. Water came from a local spring; cisterns found in the excavated area supplemented this supply [bib:custodia-nazareth; bib:dark-nazareth-archaeology].

## People and daily life
Nazareth's residents were rural Galilean farmers and craftsmen; Jesus is identified in the Gospels as "the carpenter's son" and as "the carpenter" himself [scripture:Matthew 13:55; scripture:Mark 6:3]. The village was close-knit enough that Jesus was known there by family and reputation [scripture:Luke 4:22].

## Not known
The full street plan and total extent of the first-century village beyond the limited excavated areas are not established, and its population size is only estimated as small [bib:dark-nazareth-archaeology]. No first-century synagogue structure has been identified at the site, so its appearance should be left generic or out of frame rather than invented in detail.
