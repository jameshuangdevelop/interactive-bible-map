# *Nazareth* — research brief for AI-generated reconstructions

## Setting and layout
Nazareth was a small village in the hills of Lower Galilee, set in a natural bowl among the surrounding ridges [wikidata:Q430776; dare:22899; pleiades:678299]. It lay about 6 km from Sepphoris, a much larger city that Herod Antipas was rebuilding as his Galilean capital in the early first century, which Josephus calls "the ornament of all Galilee" [bib:josephus-antiquities]. Excavations around the Basilica of the Annunciation and the nearby Sisters of Nazareth Convent site place the ancient village's core within a small area around those locations [bib:dark-nazareth-archaeology].

## What stood then
Archaeological work has found rock-cut tombs, cisterns and a domestic structure consistent with a small first-century Jewish village, matching the modest size the Gospels imply [bib:dark-nazareth-archaeology]. No public building, synagogue or civic structure has been confirmed archaeologically for this period at the site; the Gospel of Luke mentions Jesus reading in "their synagogue," but its physical form is not recovered [scripture:Luke 4:16; bib:dark-nazareth-archaeology].

## Materials and colours
Houses combined rough local limestone walls, often incorporating natural caves cut into the hillside as additional rooms, with flat roofs of timber beams and packed earth, typical of small Galilean villages of the period [bib:dark-nazareth-archaeology].

## Landscape and plants
The village was surrounded by terraced agricultural slopes carrying olives, vines and grain, typical of Galilean hill farming [bib:rainey-notley-sacred-bridge]. Water came from a local spring; cisterns found in the excavated area supplemented this supply [bib:dark-nazareth-archaeology].

## People and daily life
Nazareth's residents were rural Galilean farmers and craftsmen; Jesus is identified in the Gospels as a carpenter's son and, later, "the carpenter" himself [scripture:Mark 6:3]. The village was close-knit enough that Jesus was known there by family and reputation [scripture:Luke 4:22].
