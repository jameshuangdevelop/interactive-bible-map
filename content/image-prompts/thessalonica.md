# Thessalonica — research brief for AI-generated reconstructions

Period shown: about AD 50, Paul's visit of at least three Sabbaths (Acts 17:1-10), when Thessalonica had long been established as the capital of Roman Macedonia.

## Setting and layout

Thessalonica lies at the northernmost point of the Thermaic Gulf, just east of the mouth of the Vardar (Axius) river; Cassander, a successor of Alexander the Great, enlarged and renamed the older town Therma in 315 BC by gathering the population of several neighboring towns into it [bib:isbe-thessalonica; pleiades:491741; wikidata:Q17151].

## What stood then

By Paul's day Thessalonica was the capital of the Roman province of Macedonia and, per Strabo as quoted in the Encyclopedia, "the most populous town in Macedonia" [bib:isbe-thessalonica]. Having backed Octavian and Antony after the battle of Philippi, it held the privileged status of a "free city," keeping its own constitution, popular assembly, and Greek-style magistrates called "politarchs" rather than Roman-style officials [bib:isbe-thessalonica]. It sat on the Via Egnatia, the great highway from the Adriatic to the Hellespont, as that road's principal station [bib:isbe-thessalonica]. An inscribed arch at the western end of the city's main street (the "Vardar Gate," its inscription now in the British Museum) names six politarchs, including a Sosipater, Gaius, and Secundus — names that also belong to Paul's Macedonian traveling companions (Acts 20:4) [bib:isbe-thessalonica; scripture:Acts 20:4].

## Landscape and plants

The city overlooks the Thermaic Gulf, with the plain of the Vardar's mouth stretching away to the west [bib:isbe-thessalonica].

## People and daily life

Paul found a synagogue and, over three Sabbaths, reasoned from the Scriptures; some Jews, "a great multitude" of god-fearing Greeks, and "not a few" leading women became believers (Acts 17:2-4) [scripture:Acts 17:1-4]. Paul supported himself "working night and day" so as not to burden the new church (1 Thessalonians 2:9) [bib:isbe-thessalonica].
