# Thessalonica — research brief for AI-generated reconstructions

Period shown: about AD 50, Paul's visit of at least three Sabbaths (Acts 17:1-10), when Thessalonica had long been established as the capital of Roman Macedonia.

## Setting and layout

Thessalonica lies at the northernmost point of the Thermaic Gulf, a short distance east of the mouth of the Vardar (Axius) river; about 315 BC the Macedonian king Cassander enlarged and strengthened the town, usually identified with the earlier Therma, by gathering into it the population of several neighboring towns, and renamed it [bib:isbe-thessalonica; pleiades:491741; wikidata:Q17151].

## What stood then

By Paul's day Thessalonica was the capital of the Roman province of Macedonia and, per Strabo as quoted in the Encyclopedia, "the most populous town in Macedonia" [bib:isbe-thessalonica]. Having backed Octavian and Antony after the battle of Philippi, it held the privileged status of a "free city," keeping its own constitution, popular assembly, and Greek-style magistrates called "politarchs" rather than Roman-style officials [bib:isbe-thessalonica]. It sat on the Via Egnatia, the great highway from the Adriatic to the Hellespont, as that road's principal station [bib:isbe-thessalonica]. An inscribed arch at the western end of the city's main street (the "Vardar Gate," perhaps built to mark the victory at Philippi, though some date it later; its inscription is now in the British Museum) names six politarchs, including a Sosipater, Gaius, and Secundus, names that also belong to Paul's Macedonian companions (Acts 20:4) [bib:isbe-thessalonica; scripture:Acts 20:4].

## People and daily life

Paul found a synagogue and, over three Sabbaths, reasoned from the Scriptures; some Jews, "a great multitude" of god-fearing Greeks, and "not a few" leading women became believers (Acts 17:2-4) [scripture:Acts 17:1-4]. Paul supported himself, "working night and day, that we might not burden any of you" (1 Thessalonians 2:9) [bib:isbe-thessalonica; scripture:1 Thessalonians 2:9].

## Not known

The exact course of the mid-1st-century city wall and the appearance of the forum in Paul's time are not specified by the sources consulted here. The Roman forum visible in Thessaloniki today goes back to building work of the late 2nd century AD, which replaced an earlier forum of the early Imperial period, so images should keep the 1st-century civic buildings generic rather than copying it [bib:odysseus-thessaloniki-forum]. The palace complex of Galerius, with the Rotunda and the Arch of Galerius, was built about AD 300 and must not appear [bib:odysseus-galerius-palace].
