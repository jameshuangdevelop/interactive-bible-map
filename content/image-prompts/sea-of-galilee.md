# *The Sea of Galilee* — research brief for AI-generated reconstructions

## Setting and layout
Josephus gives the lake's dimensions as forty furlongs wide and one hundred forty long, lying in the Jordan Rift Valley and fed by the Jordan River, which enters from the north and exits to the south [bib:josephus-jewish-war; wikidata:Q126982]. He describes its water as sweet, cool and notably clear compared to other inland waters, holding distinctive kinds of fish [bib:josephus-jewish-war]. Steeper hills rose to the east, gentler slopes and the fertile Gennesaret plain lay along the northwest shore [bib:rainey-notley-sacred-bridge].

## What stood then
Several towns ringed the lake. Herod Antipas founded Tiberias on its southwest shore, naming it for the emperor and populating it with settlers, including some forced to move there and freed persons given houses and land; Josephus notes the site had to be cleared of old graves first, which some Jews considered to make the city ritually unclean [bib:josephus-antiquities]. Taricheae (also called Magdala), on the western shore, was a fortified town known for its fishing fleet and fish-processing trade, and fielded boats fitted out for fighting during the Jewish revolt [bib:josephus-jewish-war]. Capernaum lay on the northwest shore [wikidata:Q59174].

## Materials and colours
The lake's water was described as sweet and notably cool; its surrounding hills show dark basalt to the east and paler limestone to the west and north [bib:josephus-jewish-war; bib:rainey-notley-sacred-bridge]. Fishing boats were wooden, generally propelled by oar and a single sail, of the general type recovered from the lakebed near Ginosar [wikidata:Q7440096].

## Landscape and plants
The Gennesaret plain was exceptionally fertile: Josephus describes walnut trees (needing cold air), palms (needing heat), and figs and olives (needing a milder climate) all flourishing together, with grapes and figs available for about ten months of the year [bib:josephus-jewish-war]. Reed beds and fishing nets lined parts of the shore.

## People and daily life
Fishing was the lake's defining trade: several Gospel episodes describe boats, large catches and fishermen mending or casting nets [scripture:Mark 1:16; scripture:John 21:1]. Magdala/Taricheae processed and salted fish for wider trade [bib:josephus-jewish-war]. Tiberias, as Antipas's new capital, had an administrative and more cosmopolitan population mixed with the surrounding Jewish Galilean population [bib:josephus-antiquities].
