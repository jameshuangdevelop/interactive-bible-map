# *Capernaum* — research brief for AI-generated reconstructions

## Setting and layout
Capernaum was a fishing village on the northwest shore of the Sea of Galilee [wikidata:Q59174; dare:33337; pleiades:678231]. Josephus describes the adjoining Gennesaret plain as watered by an exceptionally fertile spring the local people called "Capharnaum," which fed gardens producing fruit almost year-round [bib:josephus-jewish-war]. The village lay on a trade route, consistent with the Gospels' mention of a tax-collection post there [scripture:Matthew 9:9].

## What stood then
Archaeological excavation has found a limestone synagogue at the site, but it is generally dated several centuries after Jesus' lifetime, built over the foundations of an earlier, more modest structure — the synagogue of the Gospels' own time [bib:murphy-oconnor-holy-land-guide]. A Byzantine octagonal church, also centuries later, was built over a first-century house that early Christian graffiti traditions identify as Peter's house; neither the octagonal church nor the later synagogue's fine stonework should appear in a reconstruction of this period [bib:murphy-oconnor-holy-land-guide]. Ordinary village houses of the period were modest basalt-built dwellings, not the pale limestone synagogue visible at the site today [bib:murphy-oconnor-holy-land-guide].

## Materials and colours
Capernaum's ordinary buildings used local black basalt fieldstone, rough-cut and dark, in contrast to the pale limestone used in grander buildings elsewhere in the region [bib:murphy-oconnor-holy-land-guide]. Basalt was also the local stone for millstones and oil-press weights, reflecting its hardness [bib:murphy-oconnor-holy-land-guide].

## Landscape and plants
Josephus describes the Gennesaret plain as uniquely fertile, with walnut, palm, fig and olive trees all flourishing together, and grapes and figs available for about ten months of the year [bib:josephus-jewish-war]. The lakeshore itself would have held reed beds, fishing nets drying, and small wooden boats drawn up on the beach [wikidata:Q7440096; bib:murphy-oconnor-holy-land-guide].

## People and daily life
Capernaum's economy centered on fishing: Peter, Andrew, James and John are described working as fishermen there [scripture:Mark 1:16]. A Roman centurion is mentioned stationed in or near the town, and a tax-collection post operated there [scripture:Matthew 8:5; scripture:Matthew 9:9]. Residents gathered at the synagogue on the Sabbath [scripture:Mark 1:21].

## Key sites to show

3. A modest basalt synagogue building, distinct from the later limestone structure visible at the site today.

## Not known
The precise appearance of the first-century synagogue, known only from foundation remains beneath the later limestone building, is not recovered in detail [bib:murphy-oconnor-holy-land-guide]. The exact original extent of the house venerated as Peter's is likewise uncertain beneath later building work. No boat remains have been found at Capernaum itself; the well-known first-century lake boat recovered nearby indicates the general type in use on the lake but is not proof of the specific vessels used at this village [wikidata:Q7440096].
