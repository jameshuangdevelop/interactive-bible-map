# *Capernaum* — research brief for AI-generated reconstructions

## Setting and layout
Capernaum was a fishing village on the northwest shore of the Sea of Galilee [wikidata:Q59174; dare:33337; pleiades:678231]. Josephus describes the adjoining Gennesaret plain as watered by an exceptionally fertile spring the local people called "Capharnaum," which fed gardens producing fruit almost year-round [bib:josephus-jewish-war]. The Gospels place a tax office there: Levi (Matthew) was called from it as Jesus passed by [scripture:Mark 2:1; scripture:Mark 2:14; scripture:Matthew 9:9].

## What stood then
The Franciscan excavators who uncovered Capernaum's synagogue date the building, "as it has come down to us," to the fifth century AD, built "in marked contrast to the black basalt used for building local houses... using white limestone"; it stands over the foundations of an earlier, more modest basalt structure, which may be the synagogue of the Gospels' own time [bib:custodia-capernaum; bib:murphy-oconnor-holy-land-guide]. A Byzantine octagonal church, built in the second half of the fifth century, stands over a house identified by the excavators as Peter's: built in the Late Hellenistic period, turned into a "domus-ecclesia" (house-church) by the late first century AD, and only later enclosed and then built over by the church [bib:custodia-capernaum]. Neither the octagonal church nor the later synagogue's fine stonework should appear in a reconstruction of this period. Ordinary village houses of the period were modest basalt-built dwellings, not the pale limestone synagogue visible at the site today [bib:custodia-capernaum].

## Materials and colours
Capernaum's ordinary buildings used local black basalt fieldstone, rough-cut and dark, in contrast to the pale limestone used for the later synagogue [bib:custodia-capernaum]. Basalt was also the local stone for millstones and oil-press weights, reflecting its hardness [bib:murphy-oconnor-holy-land-guide].

## Landscape and plants
Josephus describes the Gennesaret plain as uniquely fertile, with walnut, palm, fig and olive trees all flourishing together, and grapes and figs available for about ten months of the year [bib:josephus-jewish-war]. The lakeshore itself would have held reed beds, fishing nets drying, and small wooden boats drawn up on the beach [wikidata:Q7440096; bib:murphy-oconnor-holy-land-guide].

## People and daily life
Capernaum's economy centered on fishing: Simon (Peter) and Andrew, and James and John, were fishermen on the lake, and Simon and Andrew had a house in Capernaum [scripture:Mark 1:16-21; scripture:Mark 1:29]. A centurion came to Jesus in Capernaum, and a tax office operated there [scripture:Matthew 8:5; scripture:Mark 2:14]. Residents gathered at the synagogue on the Sabbath [scripture:Mark 1:21].

## Key sites to show
1. The lakeshore with dark basalt house foundations and fishing boats drawn up on the beach.
2. A view of the fertile Gennesaret plain behind the village, with mixed orchards.
3. A modest basalt synagogue building, distinct from the later limestone structure visible at the site today.

## Not known
The precise appearance of the first-century synagogue, known only from foundation remains beneath the later limestone building, is not recovered in detail [bib:custodia-capernaum; bib:murphy-oconnor-holy-land-guide]. The exact original extent of the house venerated as Peter's is likewise uncertain beneath later building work; the identification itself rests on a combination of literary and archaeological evidence rather than a labelled inscription [bib:custodia-capernaum]. No boat remains have been found at Capernaum itself; the well-known first-century lake boat recovered nearby indicates the general type in use on the lake but is not proof of the specific vessels used at this village [wikidata:Q7440096].
