# Caesarea Maritima — research brief for AI-generated reconstructions

Period shown: about AD 57–59, Paul's roughly two years in custody there under governors Felix and Festus (Acts 23:33–26:32), about seven decades after Herod the Great finished building the city.

## Setting and layout

Caesarea sits on the Mediterranean coast about midway between Joppa and Dora, a stretch of coastline with no natural harbors because south winds drive sand onto the shore [bib:josephus-antiquities; pleiades:678401; wikidata:Q319242].

## What stood then

Herod the Great built the entire city from the ground up on the site of the older Strato's Tower, over about twelve years, in white stone, with sumptuous palaces, large public buildings for the people, a theatre, and, south of the harbor facing the sea, an amphitheatre [bib:josephus-antiquities]. Its showpiece was an artificial harbor "always free from the waves of the sea," which Josephus compares in size to Athens's Piraeus: it was built by sinking huge stone blocks (up to 50 by 18 by 9 ft) into twenty fathoms of water to form a mole 200 ft wide, with a breakwater half called Procymatia, several towers (the largest named Drusus), a quay for walking, and a narrow entrance on the stiller north side flanked by a turret and twin standing stones [bib:josephus-antiquities]. On a raised platform within the circling harbor stood a temple visible far out to sea, holding statues of Roma and of Caesar [bib:josephus-antiquities]. Underground, a system of vaulted sewers carried rain and filth to the sea, flushed clean by the tide [bib:josephus-antiquities]. By Paul's day the city was the official residence of the Roman governor of Judea, and it was here that Paul was held, addressed Felix, Festus, and Agrippa, and sailed for Rome [pleiades:678401; wikidata:Q319242; scripture:Acts 23:33; scripture:Acts 25:1].

## Materials and colours

White stone throughout the city and its harbor works, per Josephus's description [bib:josephus-antiquities].

## Landscape and plants

A straight sandy Mediterranean coastline, with the great harbor mole projecting out into open sea [bib:josephus-antiquities].

## People and daily life

A mixed Jewish-and-Gentile port population whose tensions, per the Encyclopedia, eventually helped spark the war with Rome [bib:isbe-caesarea]. Cornelius, a centurion of the "Italian Regiment," was stationed here when Peter visited his household (Acts 10:1) [scripture:Acts 10:1].
