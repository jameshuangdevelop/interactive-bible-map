# Caesarea Maritima — research brief for AI-generated reconstructions

Period shown: about AD 57–59, Paul's roughly two years in custody there under governors Felix and Festus (Acts 23:33–26:32), about seven decades after Herod the Great finished building the city.

## Setting and layout

Caesarea sits on the Mediterranean coast between Joppa and Dora, a stretch of coast with no good havens because the south winds roll the sand against the shore [bib:josephus-antiquities; pleiades:678401; wikidata:Q319242].

## What stood then

Herod the Great built the entire city from the ground up on the site of the older Strato's Tower, over about twelve years, in white stone, with sumptuous palaces, large public buildings for the people, a theatre, and, south of the harbor facing the sea, an amphitheatre [bib:josephus-antiquities]. Its showpiece was an artificial harbor "always free from the waves of the sea," which Josephus compares in size to Athens's Piraeus: it was built by sinking huge stone blocks (over 50 by 18 by 9 ft) into twenty fathoms of water to form a mole 200 ft wide, one half a breakwater called Procymatia and the other carrying a wall with several towers (the largest named Drusus), a quay that ran round the haven, and an entrance on the stiller north side, with a strong round turret on one hand and two vast upright stones on the other [bib:josephus-antiquities]. On an elevation among the buildings around the haven stood a temple, seen a great way off by ships, holding statues of Rome and of Caesar [bib:josephus-antiquities]. Underground, a system of vaulted sewers carried rain and filth to the sea, flushed clean by the tide [bib:josephus-antiquities]. By Paul's day the city was the residence of the Roman procurator, and Paul was imprisoned here until his final departure for Rome [bib:isbe-caesarea; scripture:Acts 23:33; scripture:Acts 25:1]; he was kept "in Herod's palace" (Acts 23:35) [scripture:Acts 23:35].

## Materials and colours

Josephus says Herod built the city's many edifices "of white stone", and the buildings along the haven "of the politest stone" [bib:josephus-antiquities].

## Landscape and plants

A sandy Mediterranean coastline, with the great harbor mole projecting into the open sea [bib:josephus-antiquities]. The sources used here do not describe the vegetation.

## People and daily life

The population was mixed: riots between Gentiles and Jews in Caesarea gave rise to the war with Rome [bib:isbe-caesarea]. Cornelius, a centurion of the "Italian Regiment," was stationed here when Peter visited his household (Acts 10:1) [scripture:Acts 10:1].

## Key sites to show

1. The harbor mole and its sea-facing temple platform, viewed from the water [bib:josephus-antiquities].
2. The theatre [bib:josephus-antiquities].
3. The amphitheatre in the south quarter, behind the port, with a view of the sea [bib:josephus-antiquities].
4. The governor's palace district, evoking Paul's imprisonment "in Herod's palace" and his hearings there [scripture:Acts 23:35; scripture:Acts 25:6].

## Not known

Josephus does not give exact measurements for the theatre's or amphitheatre's seating capacity, and no source used here specifies the governor's palace's appearance in detail, so these buildings should be shown as generic but grand Herodian Roman architecture in white stone rather than with invented decorative specifics [bib:josephus-antiquities].
