# *Cana* — research brief for AI-generated reconstructions

## Setting and layout
Cana of Galilee, the setting for Jesus' first recorded miracle, is not identified with certainty: the leading candidates are Khirbet Qana, a hillside site overlooking the Beit Netofa valley, and Kafr Kanna, the traditional pilgrim site nearer the main Nazareth–Tiberias road [bib:mccollough-khirbet-qana; scripture:John 2:1]. This brief describes what the candidates share, without favoring either. Both sit within the cultivated hill country of Lower Galilee, among villages of similar modest scale [bib:rainey-notley-sacred-bridge].

## What stood then
Excavations at Khirbet Qana found a Jewish village occupied from the Hellenistic through Roman periods, including caves later adapted for Christian pilgrimage veneration [bib:mccollough-khirbet-qana]. Kafr Kanna has been venerated by pilgrims since at least the medieval period but has seen less archaeological exposure beneath its continuously inhabited modern town [bib:mccollough-khirbet-qana]. Neither candidate preserves a building identifiable as the wedding house of John's account; any specific house shown in an image is necessarily a generic reconstruction, not an excavated structure.

## Materials and colours
Village houses in this part of Galilee were built of local limestone, generally pale grey to cream, in simple one- or two-room plans around shared courtyards [bib:rainey-notley-sacred-bridge]. The Gospel account specifies large stone water jars used for Jewish ceremonial washing, "containing twenty or thirty gallons apiece," a detail consistent with stone vessels favored in this period for ritual purity, since stone was not considered to transmit impurity in Jewish law [scripture:John 2:6].

## Landscape and plants
The surrounding hills carried vineyards, olive terraces and grain fields typical of Galilean village agriculture [bib:rainey-notley-sacred-bridge].

## People and daily life
The Gospel describes a village wedding with a feast, wine and a bridegroom's household responsible for the provisions, consistent with general Jewish wedding customs of the period [scripture:John 2:1-10]. Village life otherwise combined farming, small-scale crafts and strict observance of purity customs such as those behind the stone water jars [scripture:John 2:6].

## Not known
Which candidate site, if either exactly, is the historical Cana is not settled among scholars, and this project does not choose between them [bib:mccollough-khirbet-qana]. The specific house associated with the wedding is not identified or preserved at either site. Population and the precise extent of either village in this period are only broadly estimated.
