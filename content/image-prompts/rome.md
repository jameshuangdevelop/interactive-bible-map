# Rome — research brief for AI-generated reconstructions

Period shown: about AD 60–62, the likely span of Paul's custody in Rome (Acts 28:16, 28:30), roughly two to four years before the great fire of July AD 64.

## Setting and layout

Rome lies mainly on the left (east) bank of the Tiber, about 15 miles from the Mediterranean [bib:isbe-rome; pleiades:423025; wikidata:Q220]. The Forum Romanum, the old civic and market center, fills the valley between the Palatine and Capitoline hills [bib:platner-ashby-topographical-dictionary]. The Circus Maximus occupies the Vallis Murcia between the Palatine and Aventine [bib:platner-ashby-topographical-dictionary]. South of the city the Via Appia leaves through the Porta Capena of the Servian wall and runs between tombs toward Capua [bib:platner-ashby-topographical-dictionary]. The Market of Appius, as far as which believers from Rome came out to meet Paul (Acts 28:15), stood at the road's forty-third milestone [bib:isbe-appii-forum; scripture:Acts 28:15].

## What stood then

The Forum's core buildings (Curia, Rostra, Temple of Saturn, Temple of Concord) already stood as a crowded, centuries-old civic square [bib:platner-ashby-topographical-dictionary]. The Circus Maximus, as rebuilt under Caesar and Augustus, measured about 621 by 118 meters, seated some 150,000 on a lower tier of stone and two upper tiers of wood, was faced with marble inside, and carried Augustus's Egyptian obelisk on its spina; Claudius assigned fixed seating to senators and Nero later did the same for knights [bib:platner-ashby-topographical-dictionary]. The Aqua Claudia, begun by Caligula in AD 38 and dedicated by Claudius in AD 52, newly carried water into the city on tall arches [bib:platner-ashby-topographical-dictionary]. The Carcer, the old state prison at the foot of the Capitol beside the Forum, with its underground chamber, the Tullianum, was used as a place of detention; a new travertine façade had been added earlier in the century, perhaps in AD 22, though Platner allows a later date [bib:platner-ashby-topographical-dictionary]. None of this is the Rome of later postcards: the Colosseum (dedicated AD 80), the Arch of Titus (after AD 81), and Nero's Domus Aurea and its lake (built only after the fire of July AD 64) must not appear [bib:tacitus-annals; bib:platner-ashby-topographical-dictionary].

## Materials and colours

Public buildings combined stone and concrete with travertine or marble facing: the Temple of Saturn's podium was travertine and peperino around a concrete core, faced with marble; the Circus was faced with marble inside; the Carcer had a travertine front [bib:platner-ashby-topographical-dictionary]. Most people lived in insulae, houses of many apartments [bib:platner-ashby-topographical-dictionary]. After the fire Nero required new buildings to be "solid, untimbered structures" of stone, so timber had been common, and the fire had spread through "the narrow, twisting lanes and formless streets typical of old Rome" [bib:tacitus-annals].

## Landscape and plants

Grain ships came up the Tiber to the city [bib:tacitus-annals]. Outside the walls the Via Appia ran between tombs and columbaria [bib:platner-ashby-topographical-dictionary]. The sources used here do not describe the planting of the city's hills or the countryside, so trees and fields should stay generic.

## People and daily life

Senators wore the tunic with a broad purple border (the latus clavus), and equestrians a narrow purple band [bib:isbe-rome]. Augustus had ordered that no one appear in the Forum or near it except in the toga [bib:suetonius-twelve-caesars]. Jews had been numerous in Rome since Pompey's time [bib:isbe-rome]. When Paul arrived, the centurion handed the prisoners to "the captain of the guard", and Paul was allowed to stay by himself with a soldier guarding him (Acts 28:16) [scripture:Acts 28:16].
