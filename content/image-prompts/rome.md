# Rome — research brief for AI-generated reconstructions

Period shown: about AD 60–62, the likely span of Paul's custody in Rome (Acts 28:16, 28:30), roughly two to four years before the great fire of July AD 64.

## Setting and layout

Rome straddles the Tiber about 15 miles from the Mediterranean, built across its hills on the river's east bank, with the Trastevere district and the Vatican plain across the water [bib:isbe-rome; pleiades:423025; wikidata:Q220]. The Forum Romanum, the old civic and market center, fills the valley between the Palatine and Capitoline hills [bib:platner-ashby-topographical-dictionary]. The Circus Maximus occupies the long, narrow Vallis Murcia between the Palatine and Aventine [bib:platner-ashby-topographical-dictionary]. South of the city the Via Appia leaves through the old republican Porta Capena and runs between roadside tombs toward Campania [bib:platner-ashby-topographical-dictionary]; it was by this road, via the Forum of Appius and Three Taverns, that the brothers came out to meet Paul (Acts 28:15) [scripture:Acts 28:15].

## What stood then

The Forum's core buildings (Curia, Rostra, Temple of Saturn, Temple of Concord) already stood as a crowded, centuries-old civic square [bib:platner-ashby-topographical-dictionary]. The Circus Maximus, as rebuilt under Caesar and Augustus, measured about 621 by 118 meters, seated some 150,000 on a lower tier of stone and two upper tiers of wood, was faced with marble inside, and carried Augustus's Egyptian obelisk on its spina; Claudius assigned fixed seating to senators and Nero later did the same for knights [bib:platner-ashby-topographical-dictionary]. The Aqua Claudia, begun by Caligula in AD 38 and dedicated by Claudius in AD 52, newly carried water into the city on tall arches [bib:platner-ashby-topographical-dictionary]. The Carcer (Tullianum), an old two-level state prison at the foot of the Capitoline beside the Forum, had recently received a new travertine façade and was still in use [bib:platner-ashby-topographical-dictionary]. None of this is the Rome of later postcards: the Colosseum (opened AD 80), the Arch of Titus (AD 81), and Nero's Domus Aurea and its lake (built only after the fire of July AD 64) must not appear [bib:tacitus-annals; bib:platner-ashby-topographical-dictionary].

## Materials and colours

New public building used brick-faced concrete, dressed in travertine or marble veneer for prestige work, as on the Circus's marble-faced interior and the Carcer's travertine front [bib:platner-ashby-topographical-dictionary]. Ordinary housing was timber-framed brick, stuccoed, packed along narrow lanes; Tacitus describes exactly this fabric when the fire two years later swept through "the narrow, twisting lanes and formless streets typical of old Rome" [bib:tacitus-annals].

## Landscape and plants

The Tiber ran silt-brown past the city, with river traffic serving its warehouse district; the Campus Martius to the north was kept open for monuments and gardens [bib:platner-ashby-topographical-dictionary]. Outside the walls, the Campagna's farmland and the tomb-lined Via Appia gave way to open countryside [bib:platner-ashby-topographical-dictionary].

## People and daily life

A dense, multiethnic capital: senators wore togas with a broad purple stripe, equestrians a narrower one [bib:isbe-rome]. Soldiers' escorts, carts, and litters moved along roads like the Via Appia, the same route by which Paul's guard brought him into the city (Acts 28:16) [scripture:Acts 28:16].

## Key sites to show

1. The Forum Romanum valley between the Palatine and Capitoline, with the Curia, Rostra, and Temple of Saturn [bib:platner-ashby-topographical-dictionary].
2. The Circus Maximus, marble-faced, obelisk on the spina, part-stone and part-timber seating [bib:platner-ashby-topographical-dictionary].
3. The Via Appia approaching from the south between tombs, near the Porta Capena [bib:platner-ashby-topographical-dictionary].
4. A dense insula quarter of timber-and-brick apartment fronts along a narrow lane [bib:tacitus-annals].
