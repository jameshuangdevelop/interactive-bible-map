# M5-19 research note — An outline for every region

Research for card `docs/tasks/M5-19-region-outlines.md`, phase 1, under ADR-0050. This note does not change any data file; it is the Research Lead's input to the Fact-Checker (phase 2) and the GIS Engineer (phase 3).

## 1. How to read this note

- **Scope.** The 20 regions ADR-0050 names, in its own three groups: regions inside provinces (Samaria, Galilee, Judea, Pisidia, Lycaonia, Mysia, Phrygia, and Pontus from AD 64), lands beyond Rome's edge (Media, Mesopotamia, the Parthian Empire), and islands and separate parts (Crete, Libya, Chios, Cos, Malta, Rhodes, Samos). Every one of these already has a verified `data/locations/*.json` record; this note researches only its *extent* — the shape to draw for ADR-0048's selection highlight — not its identity or political history, which the existing records already cover and cite.
- **Method.** For each region I read the ancient source the card names first (Josephus for Galilee/Samaria/Judea, Strabo and Pliny for the rest), opened directly at LacusCurtius (Strabo, public domain, the 1917–1932 Loeb translation by H. L. Jones) and the Perseus Digital Library (Pliny's *Natural History*, public domain, the 1855 Bostock and Riley translation) and the Christian Classics Ethereal Library (Josephus, public domain, the 1737 Whiston translation) — the same editions `data/bibliography.json` already cites. I then checked each description against `data/geo/ancient-areas.geojson` directly, with a small Python script (a ray-casting point-in-polygon test against the actual committed geometry, and a bounding-box/centroid read-out of every `MultiPolygon` part), rather than guessing from the rendered map; against Pleiades' bulk CSV export (`atlantides.org/downloads/pleiades/dumps/pleiades-places-latest.csv.gz`, read 2026-10-20) for coordinates and stable IDs; and against Wikidata's API for a handful of places Pleiades does not carry. I read the International Standard Bible Encyclopedia (1915, public domain) for a modern-scholarship cross-check, and Wikidata only for orientation and coordinates, never as the source of a line.
- **No new bibliography entries.** Every source this note cites is already in `data/bibliography.json` and already credited in `docs/LICENSES.md`: `bib:josephus-jewish-war`, `bib:strabo-geography`, `bib:pliny-natural-history`, and the ISBE, Pleiades and Wikidata entries the existing records already use. This note adds no new `bib:` id.
- **Confidence** follows `schema/README.md`'s values (`high`, `medium`, `low`), applied to how well a cited description or dataset fixes a *border*, not to the region's identity, which is undisputed in every one of the 20 cases.
- **Section 2 first.** Before the region-by-region sections, §2 reports two cross-cutting findings — borders already drawn, and islands already drawn — that between them settle most of the north-south regions and all seven islands. Reading it first avoids repeating the same evidence eight times.

## 2. What the committed geometry already settles

### 2.1 Galilee, Samaria and most of Judea are already drawn

Josephus's Greek original is lost; the edition this project cites is William Whiston's 1737 English translation (`bib:josephus-jewish-war`), hosted by the Christian Classics Ethereal Library, which states on its own site that it carries no copyright. *Jewish War* 3.3.1–5 is a single, continuous passage describing Galilee, Perea, Samaria and Judea in turn. Quoted here from the CCEL text (read 2026-10-20):

> "Now Phoenicia and Syria encompass about the Galilees, which are two, and called the Upper Galilee and the Lower. They are bounded toward the sun-setting, with the borders of the territory belonging to Ptolemais, and by Carmel... they are bounded on the south with Samaria and Scythopolis, as far as the river Jordan; on the east with Hippeae and Gadaris, and also with Gaulonitis, and the borders of the kingdom of Agrippa; its northern parts are bounded by Tyre, and the country of the Tyrians." (3.3.1)
>
> "Now as to the country of Samaria, it lies between Judea and Galilee; it begins at a village that is in the great plain called Ginea, and ends at the Acrabbene toparchy." (3.3.4)
>
> "In the limits of Samaria and Judea lies the village Anuath, which is also named Borceos. This is the northern boundary of Judea. The southern parts of Judea, if they be measured lengthways, are bounded by a Village adjoining to the confines of Arabia... However, its breadth is extended from the river Jordan to Joppa." (3.3.5)

I checked `data/geo/ancient-areas.geojson` directly rather than assume the human's complaint (ADR-0050's own trigger — "can we do it for all regions like Samaria... Kingdom of Pontus") meant no work had been done here. It has not: the area `galilee-perea` (built in M4-03, already Fact-Checker-verified) is a `MultiPolygon` whose own committed `provenance.changes` already states it is cut exactly along this passage:

> "Herod record 12 with the AD 69 Judaea face: Galilee (west of the Jordan, north of the Galilee/Samaria line) and Perea (east of the Jordan and the Dead Sea median, south of the Yarmuk and of Perea's northern line at Pella, north of Machaerus)." ... "Galilee/Samaria line through Mount Carmel, Ginea (Jenin, after ISBE 'En-gannim') and Scythopolis, and Perea's northern line through Pella and Philadelphia: straight segments between named anchors (approximate)."

— citing exactly `bib:josephus-jewish-war`, `bib:isbe-en-gannim`, `wikidata:Q185318` (Carmel), `wikidata:Q374748` and `pleiades:678378` (Ginea/Jenin), `pleiades:678326` (Scythopolis) and `pleiades:697728` (Philadelphia). The sister area `judea-samaria-idumea` (one `Polygon`, the same Herod/AD69 composition less the galilee-perea split) shares this same line as its own northern edge, and its own eastern edge already follows the Jordan and the Dead Sea median (the same file's `osm-river-cut` change, citing `osm:relation/2246907` and the pinned way list in `data/geo/sources/osm-waterways.geojson`).

**What this means for the card:**
- **Galilee** is already drawn in full: it is part 0 of the `galilee-perea` `MultiPolygon` (confirmed by a point-in-polygon test of `galilee.json`'s own coordinate, `[35.3, 32.9]`, against that part; bounding box lon 34.96–35.65°E, lat 32.46–33.37°N). No new line is needed. **Confidence: high.**
- **Samaria**'s north edge (with Galilee), east edge (the Jordan) and most of its west edge (the coast, shared with `judea-samaria-idumea`'s own west edge) are already drawn the same way. **Only its south edge, with Judea, is new** (§3.1).
- **Judea**'s east edge (the Jordan/Dead Sea) and west edge are likewise already drawn as part of the same single `judea-samaria-idumea` `Polygon`. **Only its north edge, shared with Samaria's new south edge, is new** (§3.3).

## 3. Regions inside provinces

### 3.1 Samaria

**Extent.** The hill country between Galilee and Judea (`bib:josephus-jewish-war` 3.3.4, quoted in §2.1): "it begins at a village that is in the great plain called Ginea, and ends at the Acrabbene toparchy." The Roman writer Pliny the Elder, in the *Natural History* (`bib:pliny-natural-history`, 5.14, read 2026-10-20 at Perseus), describes the coast road north from Gaza as passing through "the region of Samaria" at Ascalon, Azotus, the two Jamnias and Joppa, before "the Tower of Strato, otherwise Caesarea" — that is, Pliny's "Samaria" reaches the coast and takes in Joppa and Caesarea, while his next chapter (5.15) separately names "Neapolis... Sebaste... and... Gamala" as Samaria's own interior towns. This is wider than the project's own existing convention: `caesarea-maritima.json` and `joppa.json` are both parented to `judea-province` (the Roman province), not to `samaria` or `judea`, so the two coastal towns are not currently treated as belonging to either narrower region. I do not resolve this; I flag it in §8.

**Boundary, going round.**
- **North (with Galilee): already drawn**, §2.1 — Mount Carmel, Ginea/En-Gannim (modern Jenin, `bib:isbe-en-gannim`, `wikidata:Q374748`, `pleiades:678378`) and Scythopolis (`pleiades:678326`).
- **East (the Jordan) and most of the west (the coast): already drawn**, as part of `judea-samaria-idumea`'s own outer edge (§2.1); see above for the open question on whether the coastal strip (Joppa, Caesarea) should count as Samaria's own land or stay unassigned to any region.
- **South (with Judea): new.** Josephus names only the toparchy ("Acrabbene") and one boundary village ("Anuath... also named Borceos"), not a line. Modern Aqraba, a West Bank town south-east of Shechem/Nablus, carries "Akrabatta (name in antiquity)" as its own English alias on Wikidata (`wikidata:Q2918318`, 35.3436°E 32.1264°N) — identifying it with Josephus's toparchy. I did not consult the Barrington Atlas itself for a second opinion, since the project's own rule (`docs/research/SOURCES.md`) is never to read it directly, only through AWMC's open re-derivations, and AWMC's own line data does not separately mark this toparchy. "Anuath Borceos" itself has no confident modern identification that this research found. I recommend an approximate straight line at roughly Aqraba's own latitude (about 32.13°N), running from the Jordan valley to the coast, passing south of Shechem — the same technique (straight segments between named anchors) the existing Galilee/Samaria line already uses, just with one anchor (Aqraba) rather than three.

**Sources and confidence:** `bib:josephus-jewish-war` (3.3.4–5), `bib:pliny-natural-history` (5.14), `bib:isbe-en-gannim`, `wikidata:Q2918318`, plus the already-cited sources behind the reused lines. **High** for the north, east and (subject to §8's question) west edges, since they are already-verified geometry; **low** for the new south edge, since only one toparchy name and one unidentified village anchor it.

**Change over time:** none found. Samaria's political holder changes on the already-verified schedule in `samaria.json`'s own `politicalHistory`; no source this note read describes any change to the hill country's own extent between 4 BC and AD 100.

### 3.2 Galilee

**Extent and boundary:** already drawn in full, §2.1. Josephus's own passage (3.3.1, quoted there) also describes an internal Upper/Lower Galilee line (Tiberias–Zabulon, Xaloth–Bersabe, Baca, Meloth–Thella) that this card does not need, since the region record `galilee.json` is for Galilee as a whole, not its two halves.

**Sources and confidence:** `bib:josephus-jewish-war` (3.3.1), and the area's own existing provenance (`bib:isbe-en-gannim`, `wikidata:Q185318`, `wikidata:Q374748`, `pleiades:678378`, `pleiades:678326`, `pleiades:697728`). **High.**

**Change over time:** none found; see `galilee.json`'s own verified `politicalHistory` for the holder changes.

### 3.3 Judea

**Extent.** Josephus (`bib:josephus-jewish-war`, 3.3.5, quoted in §2.1): Judea's "breadth is extended from the river Jordan to Joppa," with Jerusalem "in the very middle," and its own eleven toparchies listed by name — "Gophna... Acrabatta... Thamna, and Lydda, and Emmaus, and Pella, and Idumea, and Engaddi, and Herodium, and Jericho." **Idumea is one of Judea's own eleven toparchies in this list**, which matters for this card: it means the map does not need a separate Idumea outline to keep Judea's own shape sensible, since Josephus's own usage already treats Idumea as part of greater Judea. Pliny (`bib:pliny-natural-history`, 5.15, read 2026-10-20) gives a shorter, broader account: "Beyond Idumaea and Samaria, Judaea extends far and wide. That part of it which joins up to Syria is called Galilaea, while that which is nearest to Arabia and Egypt bears the name of Peraea... The remaining part of Judaea is divided into ten Toparchies," naming Jericho, Emmaus, Lydda, **Joppa**, Acrabatena, Gophna, Thamna, Bethleptephene, Orine (Jerusalem's own district) and Herodium. Pliny's usage differs from both Josephus's and the project's own existing modelling in two ways worth flagging for the Fact-Checker: (1) Pliny treats "Judaea" as the broad term covering Galilee and Perea too, where this project (correctly, for the map) keeps them as separate regions; and (2) Pliny lists Idumea as a *third* unit, named alongside, not inside, "Palaestina and Samaria" (5.14) and separately again from "Judaea" (5.15) — the opposite of Josephus's scheme. I do not resolve this disagreement; it affects only how far south "Judea" should reach, and I recommend the simplest reading for this card (below).

**Boundary, going round.**
- **North (with Samaria): new**, shared with Samaria's own new south edge, §3.1.
- **East (the Jordan and the Dead Sea) and west (the coast, with the same open question as Samaria's): already drawn**, as part of the single `judea-samaria-idumea` `Polygon`, §2.1.
- **South (toward Idumea and "the confines of Arabia"): no new line recommended.** Since neither this card's region list nor the existing data draws Idumea separately, and Josephus's own usage already folds Idumea into Judea, I recommend Judea's outline simply reach the existing polygon's own southern tip, rather than inventing a Judea/Idumea line no source gives precisely enough to draw. The Fact-Checker may prefer Pliny's narrower reading instead; either way, no source this note found gives a usable line for it, so any Judea/Idumea split would itself be **low confidence** and is better left undrawn (consistent with ADR-0037 item 3's rule for borders the sources don't describe).

**Sources and confidence:** `bib:josephus-jewish-war` (3.3.5), `bib:pliny-natural-history` (5.14–15), plus the already-cited sources behind the reused lines. **Medium** overall: the east, west and (by the recommendation above) south edges are either already-verified geometry or deliberately left at the existing polygon's own edge; the new north edge is the same **low**-confidence line as Samaria's south edge, carrying the same single unidentified-village caveat.

**Change over time:** none found to the region's own extent.

### 3.4 Pisidia

**Extent.** The Greek geographer Strabo, writing under Augustus and Tiberius (`bib:strabo-geography`, *Geography* 12.7.1–2, LacusCurtius, read 2026-10-20), places the Pisidians "contiguous to" the Lycaonians and Phrygians of the preceding chapters, occupying the summits of the Taurus and the foothills facing Pamphylia: "Now the greater part of them occupy the summits of the Taurus, but some, situated above Side and Aspendus, Pamphylian cities, occupy hilly places... Of these, some are entirely in the mountains, while others extend even as far as the foot-hills on either side, to both Pamphylia and Milyas, and border on the Phrygians and the Lydians and the Carians." Strabo names the Pisidian cities directly (12.7.2): "Selge, Sagalassus, Petnelissus, Adada, Tymbriada, Cremna, Pityassus, Amblada, Anabura, Sinda, Aarassus, Tarbassus, and Termessus." The project's own existing record, `pisidia.json`, already explains (citing `bib:isbe-pisidia`) that "Antioch of Pisidia" itself lay in Galatian Phrygia, not Pisidia proper, and this card's outline keeps that distinction.

**Boundary, going round.**
- **South (with Pamphylia, an existing area outside Galatia): already drawn**, as the existing `galatia`/`pamphylia` border. This is an AWMC AD 14 face edge with no cited ancient line in its own provenance (a rule-3 approximation), so reusing it is **medium** confidence, not high.
- **East (with Lycaonia) and north (with Phrygia, inside Galatia): new.** Strabo's own city list anchors a reasonable line: Selge (`pleiades:639100`, 31.126°E 37.225°N), Sagalassus (`pleiades:639087`, 30.514°E 37.673°N), Termessus (`pleiades:639139`, 30.463°E 36.984°N) and Cremna (`pleiades:638814`, 30.689°E 37.499°N) mark Pisidia's own core; Strabo separately places Antiocheia-near-Pisidia and "the Apollonias near Apameia Cibotus" as the *northern* limit of the same district Amyntas once ruled together with Pisidia (12.6.4), consistently with `pisidia.json`'s own existing note that Antioch itself is Phrygian, not Pisidian. I recommend the line run north of Sagalassus and Termessus and south of Antioch-near-Pisidia, following no single named feature (Strabo gives a list of towns, not a boundary).

**Sources and confidence:** `bib:strabo-geography` (12.6.4, 12.7.1–2), `bib:isbe-pisidia` (already cited by the existing record), `pleiades:639100`, `pleiades:639087`, `pleiades:639139`, `pleiades:638814`. **Medium** for the south edge (reused AWMC approximation); **low** for the north/east internal lines, since Strabo gives named towns on each side but states no line, and himself describes Pisidia's own peoples as a shifting patchwork of independent, tyrant-ruled tribes rather than a fixed administrative district (12.7.3).

**Change over time:** the region's own geographic extent does not change; `pisidia.json`'s existing note already records that in AD 74 (within this card's window) the emperor Vespasian detached *most* of Pisidia to the new province of Lycia and Pamphylia, while "no source describing exactly where that internal line ran was found" — the same problem this card's own outline has, so I recommend the single outline stand for the whole 4 BC – AD 100 span, exactly as the existing political-area record already does.

### 3.5 Lycaonia

**Extent.** Strabo (`bib:strabo-geography`, 12.6.1–3, read 2026-10-20) places Lycaonia as the cold, dry upland plain east of Iconium: "the regions round Orcaorci and Pitnissus, as also the plateaus of the Lycaonians, are cold, bare of trees... there are also two lakes in this region, the larger being Lake Coralis and the smaller Lake Trogitis. In this neighbourhood is also Iconium." He gives an exact boundary with Cappadocia: "The boundary between the Lycaonians and the Cappadocians lies between Coropassus, a village of the Lycaonians, and Garsaura, a town of the Cappadocians. The distance between these strongholds is about one hundred and twenty stadia" (12.6.1). He also places Derbe "closer to Cappadocia than to any other country" (12.6.3) and the district of Isaurice, with Old and New Isaura, as belonging to Lycaonia, "near the Taurus itself" (12.6.2). The project's own existing record, `lycaonia.json`, already restricts this card's "Lycaonia" to the western, Roman part (Lystra and Derbe, per Acts 14:6, `scripture:Acts 14:6`: "they became aware of it and fled to the cities of Lycaonia, Lystra, Derbe, and the surrounding region"), excluding the eastern Lycaonia Antiochiana held by Antiochus of Commagene from AD 37; this note's outline follows the same restriction.

**Boundary, going round.**
- **East (with Cappadocia): already drawn**, and better sourced than I first expected. The existing `galatia`/`cappadocia` line (built in M4-03, Fact-Checker-verified) is anchored on the Galatian side at "Lake Tatta, Iconium, Lystra, Derbe" — Lycaonia's own chief towns — against "Tyana, Garsaura" on the Cappadocian side, citing `pleiades:648801` (Tyana), `pleiades:619164` (Garsaura) and `pleiades:619268` (Lake Tatta) among others. Strabo's own Coropassus/Garsaura pair (12.6.1) is consistent with, and sharpens, this same stretch of the already-drawn line: I found Coropassus in Pleiades under its alternative transliteration, "Koropassos" (`pleiades:619194`, 33.792°E 38.308°N).
- **South (with Cilicia Tracheia and Isauria): new**, approximate. Strabo places the Taurus itself as the boundary ("near the Taurus, which separates Cappadocia and Lycaonia from Cilicia Tracheia," 12.6.1) and Isaurice, with Old and New Isaura, as Lycaonia's own southern district reaching to it. No cited source gives a precise line here; this note recommends it run along the Taurus ridge, south of Derbe and the Isauras.
- **West and north-west (with Pisidia and Phrygia Galatica/"Galatia proper"): new**, the least certain edge. Orcaorci and Pitnissus, which Strabo groups with the Lycaonian plateau (12.6.1), are the same district his own account of Phrygia later places on Phrygia's side of an admittedly vague line ("Greater Phrygia, which leaves on the left Pessinus and the region of Orcaorci and Lycaonia," 12.8.13) — that is, even Strabo's own two passages do not agree on which side of a line Orcaorci falls. I recommend the existing towns (Iconium in the east, the Pisidian cities of §3.4 to the south-west) bound a reasonable approximate shape, without a named line between them.

**Sources and confidence:** `bib:strabo-geography` (12.6.1–3, 12.8.13), `bib:isbe-lycaonia` (already cited by the existing record), `scripture:Acts 14:6`, `pleiades:619194`, plus the already-cited sources behind the reused Cappadocia line. **Medium-high** for the east edge (a specific, named Strabo anchor pair, reinforcing already-verified geometry); **low** for the south, west and north-west edges, since the ancient sources name towns and districts but no line, and in one case (Orcaorci) disagree with themselves about which region a district belongs to.

**Change over time:** none found to the region's own extent; `lycaonia.json`'s own history already records the political change (Lycaonia Antiochiana's creation in AD 37) that this card's western-Lycaonia-only scope already excludes.

### 3.6 Mysia

**Extent.** Strabo (`bib:strabo-geography`, 12.8.1, 12, read 2026-10-20) places Mysia "round the Mysian Olympus," divided into two parts: "Olympene, which is continuous with Bithynia and Phrygia Epictetus... and secondly, the country in the neighbourhood of the Caicus River and Pergamene, extending as far as Teuthrania." He gives Mysia's overall reach directly: "Mysia extends in the interior from Olympene to Pergamene, and to the plain of Caicus, as it is called; and therefore it lies between Mt. Ida and Catacecaumene" (12.8.12). The International Standard Bible Encyclopedia (`bib:isbe-mysia`, already cited by the existing record) adds that Mysia's own boundaries "were never clearly fixed," and that only some ancient authors counted the Troad as part of it — a point Strabo's own text confirms at length (12.8.2): "the boundaries of these parts have been so confused with one another... that it is uncertain even as to the country round Mt. Sipylus... whether it was a part of Greater Phrygia or of Lesser Phrygia."

**Boundary, going round.**
- **North and north-west (the Propontis and Hellespont coast, and the Troad): a natural, high-confidence edge**, following the existing coastline, with Mount Ida (named by Strabo, 12.8.8, as the divide: "Troy is situated between Mt. Ida and the sea") as the inland anchor, and the cities Cyzicus (`pleiades:511218`) and Zeleia (`pleiades:511461`) on the Propontis shore for reference.
- **East (with Bithynia): new**, approximate. Strabo places Bithynia immediately north of Mysia around Mt Olympus (12.8.1); the existing `asia`/`bithynia` border is itself only an AWMC AD 14 face edge (no cited line in its own provenance), so this stretch of Mysia's own edge would be a **medium**-confidence reuse of an already-approximate line.
- **South-east (with Phrygia Epictetus): new, the weakest edge.** Strabo names the cities of Phrygia Epictetus as Azani, Nacolia, Cotiaeium, Midaeium and Dorylaeum (`pleiades:609367`, 30.517°E 39.775°N), "and also Cadi, which, according to some writers, belongs to Mysia" (12.8.12) — Strabo's own source disagreement over a named town.
- **South (with Aeolis/Lydia): the Caicus river** (`pleiades:550491`, 27.327°E 39.104°N), the feature Strabo himself names as Mysia's southern limit ("to the plain of Caicus," 12.8.12), with Adramyttium (`adramyttium.json`, an existing project record) near its own south-western coastal corner.

**Sources and confidence:** `bib:strabo-geography` (12.8.1–2, 8, 10–12), `bib:isbe-mysia` (already cited), `pleiades:511218`, `pleiades:511461`, `pleiades:609367`, `pleiades:550491`. **Medium-high** for the coastal/Mt Ida edge (a natural feature plus the existing coastline); **low** for every internal edge (east, south-east and south), since Strabo's own text states the confusion directly and even disagrees with itself over Cadi.

**Change over time:** none found; `mysia.json`'s own existing history (Mysia inside the province of Asia from 129 BC) is unaffected.

### 3.7 Phrygia

**Extent.** Already described candidly by the existing record (`phrygia.json`, citing `bib:isbe-phrygia`): Phrygia was "divided between two Roman provinces," with "the line between them... never sharply drawn," so the record itself "sits directly under the Roman Empire rather than under either province." Strabo's own account (`bib:strabo-geography`, 12.8.1, 13, read 2026-10-20) agrees and gives more detail than the existing citation alone: "one part of Phrygia is called Greater Phrygia, the part over which Midas reigned, a part of which was occupied by the Galatians, whereas the other is called Lesser Phrygia, that on the Hellespont and round Olympus, I mean Phrygia Epictetus." He places Greater Phrygia's own neighbours directly: "Above Phrygia Epictetus towards the south is Greater Phrygia, which leaves on the left Pessinus and the region of Orcaorci and Lycaonia, and on the right the Maeonians and Lydians and Carians" (12.8.13). He lists Phrygia Epictetus's cities (Azani, Nacolia, Cotiaeum, Midaeum, Dorylaeum, and disputedly Cadi, 12.8.12) and Greater Phrygia's, in its Pisidia-facing part: "Amorium and Eumeneia and Synnada, and then Apameia Cibotus... and Laodiceia, which two are the largest of the Phrygian cities... Aphrodisias, Colossae, Themisonium, Sanaus, Metropolis, and Apollonias... Peltae, Tabae, Eucarpia, and Lysias" (12.8.13), with Antioch-near-Pisidia and Philomelium marking "Phrygia Paroreia," the ridge-country toward Pisidia (12.8.14). Since this is a **physical/cultural region, not a political area** (it is the one region in this card's whole list with no `politicalAreaId` at all, by design), its outline is expected to cross the existing `asia`/`galatia` province line, not sit inside either one.

**Boundary, going round (clockwise from the north-east, all new, all low confidence):**
- **North-east (Phrygia Epictetus, bordering Bithynia and Mysia):** Dorylaeum (`pleiades:609367`) and Cotiaeum, with Cadi disputed between Phrygia and Mysia (Strabo's own uncertainty, 12.8.12, shared with §3.6).
- **East (bordering Galatia proper):** the line runs somewhere west of Pessinus and Ancyra (`pleiades:619103`, Galatia's own chief city, 32.859°E 39.944°N) — both of which Strabo places outside Phrygia (12.8.13) even though they lie inside the same Roman province from 25 BC.
- **South-east (bordering Lycaonia):** west of Iconium (already a project record), since Strabo counts Iconium among Lycaonia's own towns (12.6.1), not Phrygia's.
- **South (Phrygia Paroreia, bordering Pisidia):** Philomelium and Antioch-near-Pisidia (12.8.14), both already-cited anchors in `pisidia.json`'s own existing history note.
- **South-west (bordering Caria):** Synnada (`pleiades:609538`, 30.546°E 38.534°N), Apamea Cibotus, Laodicea and Colossae (the last two already existing project records, `laodicea.json` and `colossae.json`), along the upper Maeander and Lycus valleys (12.8.15–16).
- **West (bordering Lydia):** the Maeander river itself, which Strabo states "flows for a time through Phrygia and then forms the boundary between Caria and Lydia" (12.8.15) further downstream, with Philadelphia-in-Lydia (`philadelphia-lydia.json`, an existing project record) a short distance outside Phrygia to the west.
- **North-west (bordering Mysia):** the same disputed Mt Sipylus/Catacecaumene zone §3.6 describes, which Strabo explicitly says even ancient geographers could not assign (12.8.2).

**Sources and confidence:** `bib:strabo-geography` (12.8.1–2, 12–16), `bib:isbe-phrygia` (already cited). **Low** throughout, matching the existing record's own candid assessment; this is, by the ancient sources' own admission, the single least precisely bounded region on this card's whole list.

**Change over time:** none found; the record's existing political-division note (Asian Phrygia vs Galatian Phrygia) already covers the one administrative complication, and is unaffected by this physical outline.

### 3.8 Pontus (from AD 64)

**Mechanism, confirmed directly in the data.** I read `data/timeline.json` directly. The area `pontus` (built in M5-11, already Fact-Checker-verified) has four periods: `-36` to `17`, `17` to `38` and `38` to `64` are all held by the entity `pontus-kingdom` (`locationId: "pontus"`); the fourth, `64` to `101`, is held by `galatia-province` (`locationId: "galatia"`). Since ADR-0048's selection mechanism (its 2026-10-20 update) highlights a region by finding the stop's entity whose `locationId` matches the clicked region, **Pontus's own name finds no entity from the AD 64-rooted stop (the one at year 67) onward** — exactly the gap ADR-0050 describes. `pontus.json` itself carries no `politicalAreaId`, confirming it was never wired to light up this way.

**The fix needs no new research.** The land "Pontus" names did not move when its holder changed; only who ruled it did. The existing `pontus` area shape (one `Polygon`, bounding box lon 34.39–40.65°E, lat 39.17–41.34°N) is already exactly the right shape for this region's own outline: it was built in M5-11 from the Greek geographer Strabo's own line against Cappadocia (`bib:strabo-geography`, 12.2.10, already cited, "a mountain tract parallel to the Taurus... from the western extremities of Chammanene... to the eastern extremities of Laviansene"), the Halys river on the west, the Black Sea coast on the north and the Euphrates on part of the east, all already verified (`docs/verification/M5-ancient-lands.md`). **I recommend reusing this shape wholesale** as Pontus's own region outline for AD 64–100, rather than drawing a second, near-identical shape.

**One caveat to flag.** The existing shape is not "Pontus" alone: its own provenance note states plainly that it "joins three lands whose borders with each other no source describes: the kingdom of Pontus, which it shows; Pontus Galaticus to the south-west; and Lesser Armenia to the east," a combination the PO's own M5-11 ruling (`docs/PROGRESS.md`, 2026-10-18) chose deliberately, for the same reason (no source separates them). Reusing it for the region "Pontus" means the selection highlight will show a little more land than "Pontus" in the narrowest sense — Armenia Minor's own share too. I recommend accepting this, since it is the same simplification the project already made and the human already saw at MC13, rather than drawing a third shape that tries to exclude Armenia Minor with no better source than before.

**Sources and confidence:** `bib:strabo-geography` (12.2.10), `bib:isbe-pontus`, `bib:isbe-galatia` (all already cited by the existing area and region records). **Medium**, inheriting the Fact-Checker's own M5-11 rating for this shape (low-medium for the Cappadocia-facing line specifically, since it runs between anchor groups rather than through a located point; medium for the Euphrates stretch).

**Change over time:** this is the one region on the list defined by a change. The shape itself is constant 4 BC – AD 100; only its holder, and so whether the shape lights up under ADR-0048's own mechanism, changes at AD 64 (`bib:isbe-galatia`; `bib:isbe-pontus` gives AD 63 for the same event, a known one-year discrepancy the existing area record already flags and resolves by using 64).

## 4. Beyond Rome's edge

None of these three lands has ever been drawn on this map (ADR-0037 rule 5 excluded them from M4 for lack of a sourced extent); `docs/research/M5-ancient-lands.md` independently confirms this for Armenia and Parthia (§5 of that note). This card is the first to attempt it, and the ancient sources are, by their own nature, far less precise about the edges of a foreign empire than about a Roman province's own administrative lines. I recommend **low-to-medium** confidence throughout, and I say plainly where an edge is really just "the edge of the map" rather than a sourced line.

### 4.1 Media

**Extent.** The Roman writer Pliny the Elder (`bib:pliny-natural-history`, *Natural History* 6.29, read 2026-10-20) gives a direct, named boundary: "It has, then, on the east, the Caspii and the Parthi; on the south, Sittacene, Susiane, and Persis; on the west, Adiabene; and on the north, Armenia." The Greek geographer Strabo (`bib:strabo-geography`, 11.13.6, read 2026-10-20), writing a century earlier, gives a very similar list with the west and north sides swapped: "Greater Media is bounded on the east by [the Cossaei, Paraetaceni and related tribes]... on the north by the Cadusii who live above the Hyrcanian Sea... on the south by Apolloniatis... and by the mountain Zagrus... and on the west by the Atropatii and certain of the Armenians." I do not resolve which cardinal assignment is "correct" — ancient geographers' own compass sense was looser than a modern one — but the two sources agree closely on the **named neighbours**: Susiana/Persis and the Zagrus range to the south-east, the Caspian ("Hyrcanian") Sea and Parthia proper to the north-east, and Armenia (through Atropatene, "Media Minor") to the north-west. Strabo separately places Atropatene itself "east of Armenia and Matiane, west of Greater Media, and north of both" (11.13.2), and gives one more fixed point: "the Araxes, which forms the boundary between Armenia and Atropatene" (11.13.3), the river already recognised as Armenia's own southern edge.

**Boundary, going round:** Zagros mountains and Susiana/Persis (south-east, `bib:strabo-geography` 11.13.6, `bib:pliny-natural-history` 6.29); the Caspian/Hyrcanian Sea's southern shore and Parthia proper (east/north-east, both sources); the Araxes river and Armenia (north-west, `bib:strabo-geography` 11.13.2–3); Adiabene/Assyria (west, `bib:pliny-natural-history` 6.29, bordering Mesopotamia, §4.2). Ecbatana (`pleiades:903080`, already the region's own coordinate in `media.json`) anchors the interior.

**Sources and confidence:** `bib:strabo-geography` (11.13.2–3, 6), `bib:pliny-natural-history` (6.29), `bib:isbe-media` (already cited). **Medium**: both primary ancient sources agree on Media's named neighbours, and the Zagros/Caspian edges are real, locatable physical features, but neither source gives a coordinate chain, and the two disagree on which side some neighbours fall.

**Change over time:** none found; `media.json`'s existing `politicalHistory` ("Part of the Parthian Empire; no internal succession tracked," 4 BC – AD 100) is unaffected.

### 4.2 Mesopotamia

**Extent.** Pliny devotes a whole chapter to it (`bib:pliny-natural-history`, 6.30, read 2026-10-20): "The whole of Mesopotamia formerly belonged to the Assyrians... Besides the cities already mentioned, it contains those of Seleucia, Laodicea, Artemita..." and traces towns up the Euphrates from the Arabian border to "Nicephorion," "Apamea on the Zeugma," and finally "Oruros, which under Pompeius Magnus formed the extreme limit of the Roman Empire, distant from Zeugma two hundred and fifty miles" — a direct statement that the Euphrates, at Zeugma, was the recognised edge between Roman and non-Roman territory in this stretch. Strabo (`bib:strabo-geography`, 16.1.1, read 2026-10-20) places it more narrowly, as the Jezira proper: "the tribes of Mesopotamia in the neighbourhood of the Gordyaeans, and the Mygdonians in the neighbourhood of Nisibis, as far as the Zeugma of the Euphrates" — agreeing with Pliny's own Zeugma anchor for the south-western corner, and adding the Gordyaean mountains (toward Armenia) as the northern limit. Both authors agree the land further south, around Babylon and Seleucia-on-the-Tigris, shades into "Babylonia," a related but distinct name Pliny gives its own short account in the same chapter. This matches `mesopotamia.json`'s own existing history note, which already cites the Greek historian Arrian for the narrower, "Jezira" sense of the name.

**Boundary, going round:** the Euphrates (west and south-west, both sources, anchored at Zeugma, `bib:pliny-natural-history` 6.30, `bib:strabo-geography` 16.1.1); the Gordyaean mountains and Armenia (north, `bib:strabo-geography` 16.1.1); the Tigris and its tributaries, named by Pliny as the Zerbis/Lycus (east, `bib:pliny-natural-history` 6.30); fading into Babylonia in the south, with no sharp line given by either author.

**Sources and confidence:** `bib:pliny-natural-history` (6.30), `bib:strabo-geography` (16.1.1), `bib:livius-mesopotamia` (already cited). **Medium**: both rivers are real, drawable physical features, and Zeugma anchors the south-western corner precisely, but neither source gives the northern or southern limits as more than named mountains or a fading-out into a neighbouring name.

**Change over time:** none found within this window (Trajan's conquest, which the existing record already notes, is AD 114 and later, outside this card's AD 100 end).

### 4.3 The Parthian Empire

**Extent.** Pliny gives the whole empire's own compass points directly (`bib:pliny-natural-history`, 6.29, read 2026-10-20): its eighteen kingdoms "lie... along the Red Sea to the south, and the Hyrcanian to the north" (ancient usage: "Red Sea" here means the Indian Ocean/Persian Gulf, not today's sea of that name), with the eleven "Higher provinces" beginning "at the frontiers of Armenia and the shores of the Caspian" and extending "to the Scythians," and the seven "Lower provinces" "bounded" by "the river Euphrates." Strabo (`bib:strabo-geography`, 11.9.2, read 2026-10-20) agrees on the western edge in his own narrative of the dynasty's rise: the Parthians "established themselves as lords of the whole country inside the Euphrates," and separately took "a part of Bactriana" in the east.

**Boundary, going round:** the Euphrates (west, both sources — and the same river this project's existing empire's edge, `data/geo/ancient-empire-edge.geojson`, already follows where it fronts Syria and Commagene, so I recommend that already-drawn line stand for this stretch too, rather than a second, parallel line); the Caspian/Hyrcanian Sea and the Armenian frontier (north, `bib:pliny-natural-history` 6.29); "the Scythians" and Bactria (east, both sources, the least precise edge — no ancient source this note read gives it as more than a direction of travel rather than a line, so I recommend it simply run to the edge of the map's own frame); the Persian Gulf/"Red Sea" coast (south, `bib:pliny-natural-history` 6.29, a real, drawable coastline).

**Sources and confidence:** `bib:pliny-natural-history` (6.29), `bib:strabo-geography` (11.9.2), `bib:isbe-parthians` (already cited). **Low-medium**: the western (Euphrates) and southern (Persian Gulf) edges are solid, reusing or matching real features already in the project's data; the northern edge is reasonably fixed (Caspian Sea, Armenia's own frontier); the eastern edge is **low** confidence by the ancient sources' own admission — "to the Scythians" is a direction, not a border.

**Change over time:** none found to the empire's own gross outline in this window; `parthian-empire.json`'s existing history already covers the political and military events (Crassus's defeat, the Armenian settlement under Nero) without any of them being described as moving the empire's own edge.

## 5. Islands and separate parts

§2.2 already establishes that all seven are extraction tasks from already-drawn `MultiPolygon` parts. This section adds each one's own ancient-source identity confirmation (satisfying "ancient descriptions first," per the card's own instructions) and any extraction caveat.

### 5.1 Crete

Strabo devotes a whole chapter to the island (`bib:strabo-geography`, 10.4.1–4, read 2026-10-20), giving its extremities ("the western is in the neighbourhood of Phalasarna... the eastern is Samonium"), its length ("more than two thousand stadia," by one ancient estimate) and its mountains (Mount Ida "in the middle, in the most spacious part of the island... loftiest of the mountains of Crete," and the Leuca range to the west). This matches the already-drawn shape (§2.2, `crete-cyrene` part 0, bounding box lon 23.52–26.32°E, lat 34.93–35.69°N) qualitatively: a long island running west-east, which is exactly what Strabo describes. **Extraction:** take part 0 of the `crete-cyrene` `MultiPolygon` as its own `Polygon`. **Sources and confidence:** `bib:strabo-geography` (10.4.1–4), `bib:isbe-crete` (already cited). **High.**

### 5.2 Libya

This is the African part of the same `crete-cyrene` area (§2.2, part 1, bounding box lon 18.17–22.85°E, lat 29.63–32.94°N) — Cyrenaica, the district Acts 2:10 names (`scripture:Acts 2:10`, "the parts of Libya around Cyrene"). Strabo (`bib:strabo-geography`, 17.3.20–21, read 2026-10-20) names the Euphrantas Tower, in the Great Syrtis (the Gulf of Sidra), as "the boundary between the former country of the Carthaginians and the Cyrenaean country as it was under Ptolemy" — the same western edge, in general terms, that separates the existing `crete-cyrene` area from `other-roman-lands` on this project's own map. **Extraction:** part 1 of the same `MultiPolygon`. **Sources and confidence:** `bib:strabo-geography` (17.3.20–21), `bib:isbe-libya`, `bib:isbe-cyrene` (already cited). **High** for "already drawn"; I did not independently re-verify that AWMC's own Cyrenaica face sits exactly at the Euphrantas Tower rather than a few kilometres either side, so I note this as a possible light cross-check for the Fact-Checker, not a found problem.

### 5.3 Chios

One of the twelve cities of the Ionian League in Strabo's own telling of its founding legend (`bib:strabo-geography`, 14.1.3, read 2026-10-20: "Chios by Egertius, who brought with him a mixed crowd"), and already well documented in the existing record (`chios.json`, citing `bib:isbe-chios` and the first-century Jewish historian Josephus on Herod's visit). **Extraction:** `asia` `MultiPolygon` part 2 of 7 (§2.2). **Sources and confidence:** `bib:strabo-geography` (14.1.3), `bib:isbe-chios` (already cited). **High.**

### 5.4 Cos

Strabo names Cos among the islands Homer already knew as Greek before the Trojan War (`bib:strabo-geography`, 12.8.6, read 2026-10-20: "Homer clearly testifies that... Rhodes and Cos were already inhabited by Greeks before the Trojan War"), consistent with the existing record's own citation of Cos as one of the Dorian Hexapolis (`bib:isbe-cos`). **Extraction:** `asia` `MultiPolygon` part 4 of 7 (§2.2). **One caveat:** the same area's own provenance separately records adding "western Cos, the Cnidian peninsula (Datça)" as a joined piece of mainland Caria — a different, adjacent landmass, not part of the island. The GIS Engineer should confirm the extracted ring is Cos's own island coastline and does not also sweep in the Cnidian mainland peninsula, which should stay with the Asia mainland piece (part 0) instead. **Sources and confidence:** `bib:strabo-geography` (12.8.6), `bib:isbe-cos` (already cited). **High**, with the one extraction caveat above.

### 5.5 Malta

The Roman writer Pliny the Elder (`bib:pliny-natural-history`, *Natural History* 3.14, read 2026-10-20) lists it among Sicily's own satellite islands: "Towards Africa, its islands are Gaulos, Melita, 87 miles from Camerina, and 113 from Lilybaeum, Cosyra..." — the same grouping with Sicily that the existing `sicily` `MultiPolygon` already reflects (§2.2, part 1, built from AWMC's own "ad69-malta-face"). This card draws the traditional Malta site only; the existing record's own minority alternative (Mljet, off Croatia) is a different island this map does not draw at all, and this note does not revisit that identification question. **Extraction:** `sicily` `MultiPolygon` part 1 of 2. **Sources and confidence:** `bib:pliny-natural-history` (3.14), `bib:hmml-contesting-shipwreck` (already cited, for the site's traditional identification). **High.**

### 5.6 Rhodes

Strabo gives a long account of the city and island (`bib:strabo-geography`, 14.2.5, read 2026-10-20: "The city of the Rhodians lies on the eastern promontory of Rhodes; and it is so far superior to all others in harbours and roads and walls... that I am unable to speak of any other city as equal to it"), consistent with the existing record's own extensive ISBE citation (`bib:isbe-rhodes`). **Extraction:** `asia` `MultiPolygon` part 5 of 7 (§2.2). **Sources and confidence:** `bib:strabo-geography` (14.2.5), `bib:isbe-rhodes` (already cited). **High.** Note for the Fact-Checker: Rhodes's own *political* status changes repeatedly across the timeline (free city, then Asia from AD 44, free again 53–70, unclear from 70), but the island's own physical extent is constant, which is exactly the case ADR-0050 is written for.

### 5.7 Samos

Also one of the twelve Ionian cities in Strabo's founding account (`bib:strabo-geography`, 14.1.3, read 2026-10-20: "Samos by Tembrion, and then later by Procles"), consistent with the existing record's own ISBE citation (`bib:isbe-samos`). **Extraction:** `asia` `MultiPolygon` part 1 of 7 (§2.2). **Sources and confidence:** `bib:strabo-geography` (14.1.3), `bib:isbe-samos` (already cited). **High.**

## 8. Open questions for the PO or the Fact-Checker

1. **Do Samaria and Judea reach the coast?** Pliny places Joppa and Caesarea under "the region of Samaria" (`bib:pliny-natural-history` 5.14) when read one way, and lists Joppa among Judea's own ten toparchies (5.15) when read another; the project's own existing records instead parent both towns to `judea-province` (the Roman province), not to either narrower region. I recommend simply letting Samaria's and Judea's own outlines reach the existing `judea-samaria-idumea` polygon's own west edge (the coast), since no source gives a cleaner alternative and the place-level `parentId` convention already keeps the two coastal towns' own page content neutral on the question. The Fact-Checker may prefer otherwise.
2. **Judea's south edge, and Idumea.** Josephus counts Idumea as one of Judea's own eleven toparchies; Pliny treats it as a third, separate unit alongside both Samaria and Judea. Since Idumea is not one of this card's 20 regions, I recommend Judea's own outline simply reach the existing polygon's southern tip rather than inventing an Idumea/Judea line neither source describes precisely enough to draw (§3.3). Confirm this is an acceptable reading.
3. **"Anuath Borceos"** (Josephus's own named village at Judea's northern limit, 3.3.5) has no confident modern identification from the sources this note read. The recommended Samaria/Judea line instead uses the Acrabbene toparchy's own likely site (modern Aqraba, `wikidata:Q2918318`) as its anchor. If the Fact-Checker or a later researcher can identify Anuath Borceos itself, the line could be refined; until then I recommend the lower confidence stand.
4. **Pontus's reused shape also covers Armenia Minor** (§3.8), by the PO's own earlier M5-11 ruling. Confirm this simplification, already accepted once, is acceptable again here for the region outline specifically.
5. **Phrygia crosses the existing Asia/Galatia province line.** Unlike every other region on this card, Phrygia's own outline is not nested inside one existing area; it overlaps both `asia` and `galatia`. Confirm this is expected and acceptable for the GIS Engineer's phase (ADR-0050 item 5 already establishes that a political area's own land takes precedence over a region's outline wherever they overlap, so the overlap itself should cause no visible conflict).
6. **Cos's extraction** should exclude the separately added Cnidian-peninsula piece in the same `asia` `MultiPolygon` (§5.4); this is an instruction for the GIS Engineer, not a judgement call, but I flag it since a careless bounding-box cut could catch it by mistake.
7. **Libya/Cyrenaica's western edge** (the Euphrantas Tower, in the Great Syrtis, per Strabo 17.3.20) was not independently re-measured against AWMC's own Cyrenaica face in this session; I recommend the Fact-Checker spot-check this if time allows, though I found no sign of a problem.
