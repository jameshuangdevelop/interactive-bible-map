# M5-24 research note — Jesus' journeys in a few parts

Research for card `docs/tasks/M5-24-jesus-journey-parts.md`, phase 1. Written after reading the `jesus` route in `data/routes/routes.json`, `docs/research/M5-jesus-journeys.md` and `docs/verification/M5-jesus-journeys.md` (M5-03), ADR-0038 (with its three 2026-10-13/14 updates), ADR-0041 and ADR-0042 in `docs/DECISIONS.md`, and `docs/LICENSES.md`. It proposes how to split the existing 32 well-attested stages into a handful of parts, in what order to list each part's stages, and how the data and the Routes tab could show this. It does not change any data file, any disputed site's ruling, or the rule a stage must meet to appear at all (ADR-0038 item 3); M5-03's own research and verification stand.

## 1. How this note was built

The 32 stages already exist, fact-checked, in `data/routes/routes.json`. M5-03's own note explains that they are listed "in five broad periods every Gospel shares, and within each period by each segment's first passage... a way of listing them, not a claim about when each happened" (the route's own `notes[1]`), and its verification report confirms the periods are segments 1–10, 11–21, 22–28, 29–30 and 31–32 (`docs/verification/M5-jesus-journeys.md` §16, "S2, the order"). This note keeps those five periods as the starting point for "parts" (they already follow the Gospels' own broad order, and the Fact-Checker has already checked their membership), but goes further than M5-03 did: for each part, it proposes an actual order for its stages, not just a listing convention, because the card asks for "its order within the part" with "the evidence for that order" and a confidence. Where that meant deciding how two Gospels' separate accounts relate in time, a judgment call M5-03 deliberately avoided (its brief §1.6: no harmonized itinerary), this note makes the call openly, names the source for it, states a confidence, and gives the alternative where a second cited harmony disagrees.

Three kinds of evidence support an "order within part" claim here, in decreasing strength:
1. **The same Gospel's own continuous text** states or clearly implies the order (for example, "immediately," "again," "from there," or simply the next verse with no change of scene). Marked **high**.
2. **A cited harmony of the Gospels** places two stages from different Gospels in a given order, as part of fitting all four accounts into one reading. Marked **medium**, whether one or both cited harmonies agree, since the order still rests on a scholarly judgment call rather than a verse that states it.
3. **The cited harmonies themselves disagree** on where a stage belongs. Marked **low**; both placements are given.

No stage in this note's proposed order has a *textually certain* placement relative to a stage from a different Gospel; "high" confidence here always means same-Gospel continuity. Only one stage (segment-22, in Part 3) drew real disagreement between the two cited harmonies; it is marked low and both readings are given in §4.3 and in the table.

Five parts, each shown on the Routes tab on its own, as Paul's four journeys already are. Every one of the 32 stages belongs to exactly one part. §7 gives a four-part alternative that follows the backlog's own example more closely, merging the last two parts below into one.

**Sources for the parts themselves** (which stages belong together, and that this broad order is uncontroversial): the existing route's own sources `bib:isbe-chronology-nt` and `bib:worldhistory-jesus-christ`, already in `routes.json`; `docs/verification/M5-jesus-journeys.md` §16 ("S2, the order") for the already-checked five-way split; and, for the order claims this note adds within each part, A. T. Robertson's and Stevens and Burton's harmonies (§6). Every stage's own scripture and attestation sources are unchanged from `routes.json`.

Parts 4 and 5 are much shorter than the others (two stages each, against seven to eleven). This note keeps them separate because each is a single, well-known, self-contained movement that most readers will recognize on its own (the last week in Jerusalem; the appearances after the resurrection), and because Paul's own shortest journey (the voyage to Rome) already has only eight stages, so a two-stage part is a difference of degree, not of kind. §7 gives the alternative.

## 4. The proposed order within each part

This section explains the reasoning behind the harder calls. §5 gives the full table for all 32 stages, including the easier ones only briefly noted here.

### 4.1 Part 1 — the early ministry

Both cited harmonies agree on the same order, and it is also the order most readers would recognize from a traditional reading of the Gospels: baptism, temptation, John's early Judea/Galilee/Samaria material (Robertson §24-36; Stevens & Burton §19-34), and only then the Synoptic Gospels' own account of Jesus settling at Nazareth and Capernaum (Robertson §37-40; Stevens & Burton §34-37). The one real wrinkle is segment-02 (Bethany beyond the Jordan → Galilee): its own citations cover two different moments. Matthew 4:1 and 4:11a, Mark 1:12-13 and Luke 4:1 are the temptation, which both harmonies place straight after the baptism (Robertson §25; Stevens & Burton §20). But Matthew 4:11b-12, Mark 1:14 and Luke 4:14 ("Jesus heard that John was delivered up, and withdrew into Galilee" and its parallels) are placed by both harmonies much later, matching John's own "he left Judea and departed into Galilee" (John 4:1-4), only after the whole Judea/Samaria section (Robertson §34; Stevens & Burton §31). This note keeps segment-02 as the single stage it already is (it is not proposing new stages) and places it second, since the temptation is its earliest event; the table flags the alternative reading.

### 4.2 Part 2 — Galilee and the regions beyond it

Most of this part's order is high confidence, because segments 11 through 18 form one continuous chain within the Synoptic Gospels: Luke's own chapters 7 and 8 run from Nain (segment-19) into the crossing to the Gadarenes (segment-11) with nothing between them in his own text; and Mark's chapters 5 through 9 run from the Gadarenes' return (segment-12) through Tyre and Sidon (13), the Decapolis (14), Magdala (15), Bethsaida (16), Caesarea Philippi (17) and back to Capernaum (18), each stage's own wording picking up directly from the one before ("from there," "again," "immediately"). Matthew's text confirms the same chain independently.

The two real judgment calls are where the two round trips to Jerusalem (segments 20 and 21, both "Galilee → Jerusalem") fit into that chain, since John never says where Jesus was in Galilee just before either trip. Both cited harmonies place segment-20 (John 5:1, an unnamed feast) early, right before the Sabbath disputes over the standing grain and the man with the withered hand, which this part's data does not include as stages (Robertson §49, grouped with §50-51; Stevens & Burton §44, grouped with §45-46). The International Standard Bible Encyclopedia's own discussion of the length of Jesus' ministry supports an early date for this feast on different grounds: if the feast was itself a Passover, "the subject of the controversy with the Jews in Jerusalem as well as the season of the year would harmonize with the Synoptic account of the Sabbath controversy in Galilee which probably followed this Passover" (`bib:isbe-chronology-nt`, §I.5). Which feast John 5:1 names is not settled (the article says so plainly, and so does this route's own `yearNote`), but the order question is separate from that identification, and the two cited harmonies agree on it. Both harmonies also agree that segment-21 (John 7:1, the Feast of Booths, which the text itself ties to autumn) comes last in this part, after the Caesarea Philippi journey (Robertson §94-96; Stevens & Burton §82).

### 4.3 Part 3 — the final journey

This part has the one genuinely disputed placement in all 32 stages. The Gospels give two different routes to Jerusalem for the last Passover, already noted as such in M5-03's own research (`docs/research/M5-jesus-journeys.md` §3.3's note and §8): the Synoptic Gospels have Jesus come up through Jericho (segments 22 and 23), while John has him withdraw beyond the Jordan after the Feast of Dedication, return to raise Lazarus, withdraw again to Ephraim, and come back to Bethany (segments 25 through 28). Both threads converge at segment-24, the triumphal entry, which all four Gospels tell and which does not conflict (already established in `routes.json`'s own attestation rule for that stage).

The two cited harmonies disagree on how these two threads relate:
- **Robertson's harmony** (followed in this note's proposed order) places the whole of John's withdrawal-Lazarus-Ephraim sequence (§111-112, 118-119) *before* the Synoptic departure from Galilee, reading Matthew 19:1/Mark 10:1 ("departed from Galilee ... into the borders of Judea beyond the Jordan") as a second, later entry into the same region, after a trip that briefly touches Galilee again (§120-121, built on Luke 17:11, which this project's own data does not cite for any stage). On this reading, segment-22 is §122, well after segment-27.
- **Stevens and Burton's harmony** instead reads Matthew 19:1/Mark 10:1 as the single "final departure from Galilee" that opens the entire period (§86), placed *before* the Dedication Feast material (§92) rather than after it.
- Read on their own, without either harmony's synthesis, Matthew's and Mark's own texts give no scene between segment-18 (ending at Capernaum, Mark 9:33/Matthew 17:24) and segment-22 (Mark 10:1's "he arose from there") besides non-travel teaching (Mark 9:33-50; Matthew 18), which argues, if anything, for segment-22 following not long after segment-18, closer to Stevens and Burton's reading than to Robertson's.

Since the cited harmonies disagree, segment-22's position in the table is marked **low** confidence, with both readings given. This note's proposed order follows Robertson's reading only for consistency with the rest of this note (§1); it does not judge the question. Within each thread, the order is high confidence (same-Gospel continuity: John 10:40→11:1 for segments 25-26; John 11:54's own "departed... to Ephraim" for 26-27; "as they went out from Jericho" for 22-23). Segment-28 (Ephraim → Bethany, "six days before the Passover") and segment-23 (Jericho → Bethphage) both lead into segment-24 without the text saying which thread arrived first; Robertson's own section numbers (§126-127 before §128a) put the Synoptic arrival first, which this note follows at medium confidence.

### 4.4 Parts 4 and 5

High confidence throughout. Segment-29 (the nightly pattern of returning to Bethany) spans the whole week up to the Last Supper, which all four Gospels place before segment-30 (going out to Gethsemane). Segment-31 (the road to Emmaus) is told as happening "that very day" in Luke's own text, before segment-32 (the ascension at Bethany), which Luke narrates as his Gospel's last scene; Acts 1:3, by the same writer, clarifies that about forty days in fact separated the resurrection from the ascension, which Luke 24 alone does not state, but this does not disturb the two stages' narrated order.

## 7. Alternative: four parts

The backlog's own example groups the material differently: "the early ministry in Judea and Samaria; Galilee; the journeys north and east of the lake; and the last journey to Jerusalem, with the appearances after the resurrection." Read closely, this is one way to reach either four or five parts:

- **Splitting "Galilee" from "the journeys north and east of the lake"** (keeping this note's Part 2 as two parts) does not divide cleanly along stage boundaries. The Gospels' own order interleaves a crossing east to the Gadarenes (segments 11-12), a stay in Galilee itself (segment 19, Nain), and the withdrawals north to Tyre, the Decapolis, Bethsaida and Caesarea Philippi (segments 13-18) with two separate round trips to Jerusalem (segments 20-21). Splitting by place-type rather than by this note's single ordered list would require two parts whose stages are not contiguous in the proposed order, so this note does not recommend it.
- **Merging this note's Parts 3, 4 and 5** ("the last journey to Jerusalem, with the appearances after the resurrection") is straightforward: it simply keeps segments 22-32 together as one eleven-stage part, titled along the lines of "The last journey to Jerusalem, the last week, and the resurrection." This reaches **four parts** in total: Part 1 and Part 2 as above, and one combined final part.

This note recommends the five-part split in §2, both because it already matches the checked five-way grouping in M5-03's verification, and because "the last week in Jerusalem" and "after the resurrection" are each well-known enough on their own that combining them with the final approach to the city may read as less clear, not more. The four-part alternative is a reasonable choice too, and is noted as an open question for the PO in §10.

## 8. The 12 stages without a line

Twelve of the 32 stages carry `noLineReason` instead of `lineId`, because one or both of their places has more than one proposed site and this project's data favors none of them (ADR-0038 item 4). Splitting the route into parts does not change this: the rule, the candidates, and which stages lack a line are all unchanged. This section gives, for each of the five disputed places, the candidate sites as `data/locations/` already has them (§6, point 5), and which stages are affected and why, regardless of which part they fall in. The visual treatment on the map is for the human to choose (the card's own instruction); §8.6 offers options.

### 8.1 Bethany beyond the Jordan — 5 stages (01, 02, 05, 25, 26)

Two candidate sites, on opposite banks of the Jordan River, neither favored:
- **Al-Maghtas / Wadi al-Kharrar** (east bank, Jordan), confidence `medium`. A UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2015, with excavated Roman- and Byzantine-period churches, hermit caves and baptismal structures.
- **Qasr al-Yahud** (west bank, Israel/West Bank), confidence `low`. A parallel pilgrimage site with two fifth-century Byzantine churches, less archaeologically investigated.

Regardless of which bank is meant, the Gospels' own order is unaffected: segment-01 (the baptism) opens Part 1; segment-02 (the temptation and, on its later wording, the return to Galilee) follows it; segments 25-26 (the withdrawal after the Feast of Dedication, and the move on to raise Lazarus) open Part 3, in that order (§5).

### 8.2 Cana — 3 stages (05, 06, 10)

Three candidate villages, all in Galilee, none favored:
- **Khirbet Qana**, confidence `disputed`. An excavated Jewish village (Hellenistic through Roman periods) with caves later adapted for Christian pilgrimage; favored by many recent archaeologists.
- **Kafr Kanna**, confidence `disputed`. The traditional site shown to pilgrims and tourists since at least the medieval period; its own first-century archaeological evidence is thinner.
- **Qana** (southern Lebanon), confidence `low`. A minority tradition, mainly in Lebanese Christian communities.

Segment-05 (arriving at Cana for the wedding) and segment-06 (leaving for Capernaum) are stages 3 and 4 of Part 1; segment-10 (returning to Cana from Sychar) is stage 8 (§5).

### 8.3 The country of the Gadarenes (Gerasenes/Gergesenes) — 2 stages (11, 12)

Three candidate sites, reflecting a real difference between the ancient manuscripts over the region's name (not only a translation choice; see the route's own note, quoted in the card): WEB reads "Gergesenes" at Matthew 8:28 and "Gadarenes" at Mark 5:1 and Luke 8:26.
- **Kursi (Gergesa)**, confidence `disputed`. The only one of the three directly on the lake shore.
- **Gadara** (modern Umm Qais), confidence `disputed`. A real Decapolis city about 6 miles from the lake.
- **Gerasa** (modern Jerash), confidence `low`. The best-known ancient place with this name, but much further inland.

Segments 11 and 12 (the crossing there and back) are stages 3 and 4 of Part 2 (§5).

### 8.4 Bethsaida — 2 stages (16, 17)

Two excavated sites, 2.1 km apart, neither favored:
- **et-Tell**, confidence `disputed`.
- **el-Araj**, confidence `disputed`.

Segments 16 and 17 (arriving at Bethsaida, then going on to Caesarea Philippi) are stages 8 and 9 of Part 2 (§5).

### 8.5 Emmaus — 1 stage (31)

Four candidate sites, at very different distances from Jerusalem:
- **Emmaus Nicopolis (Imwas)**, confidence `disputed`.
- **Qaloniya / Motza**, confidence `disputed`.
- **El-Qubeibeh**, confidence `low`.
- **Abu Ghosh**, confidence `low`.

Segment-31 is stage 1 of Part 5 (§5).

### 8.6 Visual treatment: options for the PO and the human

The card asks for two or three options with trade-offs, leaving the choice to the human. All three keep today's rule unchanged (no line is drawn, and every candidate stays listed); they differ only in how a reader sees that this numbered stage exists, without implying a path.

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Number on the stop itself.** The stage's number appears as a small pill fixed to the destination stop's dot (reusing today's pill style, ADR-0041 item 4), instead of at a line's midpoint. | A numbered stage is visible at its stop even with no line. | Needs new placement logic (anchored to a point, not a line). With two disputed ends (for example segment-05, Bethany beyond the Jordan → Cana), it is not obvious which stop should carry the number. |
| **B. Floating number at the midpoint, no line drawn.** The number pill sits where a line's midpoint would be, computed from the two stops' coordinates, but no line is drawn under it; a visually distinct style (for example a dashed outline, or a lighter tint) marks it as "no known path." | Keeps the same visual position readers already expect for a stage's number; clearly distinct from a drawn line. | A floating number with nothing connecting it may look like a rendering bug rather than a deliberate choice, unless the distinct style is very clear; needs a short legend or the panel's existing "Site disputed" tag to explain it. |
| **C. A short dashed line between the stops, numbered as usual.** This is the card's own example ("a numbered place with a dashed hint"). | Matches readers' expectation that a number sits on a line; least new logic, closest to today's rendering for stages with a line. | **The human already rejected dashed/dotted lines at MC9** ("No dotted lines please. Just add disclaimers at the end"), which is why ADR-0041 item 3 draws every line solid and item 1 moved every caveat to the end. Using a dashed line here would need the human to revisit that decision specifically for this case (a line that shows no path exists, rather than an uncertain path), which is a different question from the one MC9 decided, but should be put to the human plainly rather than assumed. |

This note does not recommend one of the three; the card asks that the look be decided with the human, and the PO/Frontend Engineer are better placed to prototype and show it (as the card's own "Routes tab" scope item already plans).

## 9. Proposed data shape

**Recommendation: one route per part, like Paul's four.** The reasoning follows.

### 9.1 What changes either way

Regardless of which shape is chosen: the 32 stages' own content (`from`, `to`, `scripture`, `attestation`, `travelMode`, `lineId`/`noLineReason`, `sources`) does not change; only which "part" each belongs to and its order within that part are new. No line in `data/geo/routes-lines.geojson` is added, removed, or has its geometry changed; only the `routeId` property on its existing 20 features (one per stage with a line) would need to match whichever route id now holds that stage.

### 9.2 Option 1 — one route per part (recommended)

The `jesus` route becomes five (or four, §7) separate entries in `routes.json`'s `routes` array, each with its own `id`, `title`, `fromYear`/`toYear`, `summary`, `bibleText`, `notes` and `sources`, exactly like `paul-1` through `paul-rome` today. Each keeps `structure: "standalone-segments"` (unchanged: no single order is claimed across stages from different Gospels within a part either, only the order this note proposes, which the data could record via each stage's position in its new route's `stages` array, matching how `paul-1`'s stages are already a plain ordered array).

**Pros:**
- **No schema change.** `schema/routes.schema.json`'s `route` type already supports any number of `route` objects; nothing about today's schema assumes one route per person. The validator's one `structure`-specific rule (`scripts/lib/validator.mjs`, "standalone-segments routes require attestation for every stage") already holds for all 32 stages and needs no new logic.
- **Numbering is free.** Every stage's displayed number already comes from `stageLabelNumber(route.structure, stageIndex)` (`app/src/features/routes/routes-data.ts`), which is simply the stage's position in *that route's own* `stages` array. Splitting into five routes means each part's numbering restarts at 1 automatically, with no new code, exactly as `paul-2`'s first stage is already "Stage 1," not "Stage 15."
- **The existing route-switcher needs no new concept.** The "choose a route" list (`app/src/features/routes/routes-panel.web.tsx`) already renders a flat list of buttons, one per route, each showing its title, year range and stage count; it already works for any number of routes and does not group them by person. Five Jesus parts simply become five more buttons, next to Paul's four.
- **"Fit the map to it" works correctly out of the box** (ADR-0041 item 7): fitting a route to its own stages' stops already means fitting to only that part's places, not all 32, which is the actual goal (the human's complaint was that the map looks "way too busy").
- **Each part can carry its own year range**, which could be more precise than one `fromYear`–`toYear` spanning AD 26-33, if the Fact-Checker finds a source for narrower ranges per part (not attempted in this note).
- **Stage ids can stay unchanged** (`segment-01` through `segment-32`), simply distributed across the five new routes' `stages` arrays, keeping every cross-reference in this note, `docs/research/M5-jesus-journeys.md` and `docs/verification/M5-jesus-journeys.md` traceable without renumbering.

**Cons:**
- **Breaks the single `route=jesus` deep link.** `?tab=routes&route=jesus` (and `&stage=N` on it) would need new ids. Concretely, this project's own tests use it today: `tests/browser/browser-checks.spec.mjs` (three places, including a fixed list of all five routes' ids, titles and an expected stop label, and a `?route=jesus&stage=9` deep link) and `scripts/verify-web-export-playwright.mjs` (several hardcoded `route=jesus` URLs, a `"jesus fitted view"` screenshot, and a `hasCountryOfTheGadarenes` lookup keyed to the `"jesus"` route id). All of these are mechanical updates (new ids, same pattern, one row each), but they are real, counted changes for whichever agent does the data/test work next.
- **`routes-lines.geojson`'s 20 `"routeId": "jesus"` properties** need updating to whichever new route holds each stage (a mechanical, scripted change, not a redesign).
- **The route list grows from 5 entries to 9** (4 Paul's + 5 Jesus's, or 4 + 4 under §7's alternative), all in one flat list with no visual grouping by person today. This may be worth a small follow-up (for example a "Paul's journeys" / "Jesus' journeys" heading above each person's group in the list), which this note flags as an open question (§10) rather than deciding.

### 9.3 Option 2 — parts inside one `jesus` route

The route stays one entry, but each stage gains a new field (for example `stage.partId`), and the route gains a new `parts` array (each with an `id`, `title`, `summary` and `scripture` range).

**Pros:**
- Keeps `?route=jesus` working as a single, stable top-level id.
- Keeps one `fromYear`/`toYear` for the whole ministry, if that is preferred to five narrower ranges.

**Cons:**
- **Needs a schema change** (`schema/routes.schema.json`): a new `part` definition and a new optional field on `stage`, plus new validator rules (every stage names a valid part; every declared part is used by at least one stage; part ids are unique within the route) that do not exist today and are not needed under Option 1.
- **Stage numbering would not reset per part for free.** Today's numbering is simply the stage's index in `route.stages`; keeping one flat 32-stage array but wanting "Segment 1" at the start of each part means computing a position *within the active part*, not within the route, which is new logic the existing `stageLabelNumber` helper does not do.
- **The deep-link contract would need a new dimension.** Today `?stage=N` is a single integer, matching a flat array index (and the existing tests assert exactly this, for example `expect(urlParams.stage).toBe(String(stageIndex))`); showing one part at a time would need either a new `&part=` parameter, or non-trivial stage identifiers (such as `"3-2"` for "part 3, stage 2"), either of which is a bigger change to the URL contract than Option 1's plain new route ids.
- **"Fit the map to it" would need new logic** to fit only the active part's stops rather than the whole route's 32, where Option 1 gets this correctly for free from the existing per-route fit behavior.
- **The route-switcher list would need a new, two-level UI** (routes, then parts within a route), a new concept not in any ADR today, where Option 1 reuses the existing flat list unchanged.

### 9.4 Recommendation

**Option 1, one route per part.** It needs no schema or validator change, reuses the Routes tab's existing route list, stage numbering, and "fit to route" behavior exactly as Paul's journeys already use them, and keeps the 32 stages' own ids stable for traceability. Its real costs — new route ids in place of `jesus`, and the mechanical updates that follow in two test files and one GeoJSON file — are small and already scoped precisely in §9.2. This also matches the card's own description of the goal: "Each part is shown on its own, as Paul's journeys are."

**Naming the new routes** is an open question (§10): either short numbered ids in Paul's own style (`jesus-1` … `jesus-5`), or descriptive ids (`jesus-early-ministry`, `jesus-galilee`, `jesus-final-journey`, `jesus-passion-week`, `jesus-resurrection`). Either fits the schema's existing id pattern; this note does not choose between them.

## 10. Open questions

1. **Five parts (§2) or four (§7)?** This note recommends five, keeping "the last week in Jerusalem" and "after the resurrection" separate; the backlog's own example suggests combining them into one, larger final part. Either is defensible; the PO/human should decide.
2. **Part titles.** Are the five titles proposed in §2 clear and neutral enough, or should the PO propose different wording (for example naming the regions north and east of the lake more specifically)?
3. **Segment-22's placement (§4.3, Part 3).** This note followed A. T. Robertson's harmony for consistency, placing it after the Ephraim withdrawal (low confidence, with Stevens and Burton's very different placement given as the alternative). Should the Fact-Checker instead prefer the Stevens and Burton reading (which keeps closer to Matthew's and Mark's own chapter order, with no unincluded detour through Galilee), or leave the published order ambiguous between the two rather than picking one?
4. **Data shape (§9).** Confirms Option 1 (one route per part)? If so, should the new route ids be numbered (`jesus-1`…) or descriptive (`jesus-early-ministry`…)?
5. **Visual treatment for the 12 lineless stages (§8.6).** The card asks that this be shown to the human before merging; this note offers three options without recommending one.
6. **The route list's length.** With five (or four) new Jesus routes alongside Paul's four, should the Routes tab group them by person (a small, separate follow-up), or is a longer flat list acceptable for now?
7. **New bibliography entries (§6).** Please confirm the two proposed entries for Robertson's and Stevens and Burton's harmonies, and independently confirm the public-domain status of the specific Stevens and Burton copy opened (1904 first edition content, per its own preface, inside a scan whose front matter also carries a 1932 notice) before any direct quotation beyond a short section title.
8. **Segment-02's two-moment nature (§4.1).** Out of scope for this card (it is not a disputed site, and the card does not ask to change any stage's own content), but flagged for a future card: the stage's own wording spans both the temptation (right after the baptism) and a later "withdrew into Galilee" moment that both cited harmonies place much later. A future revision could split it into two stages if that is ever judged worthwhile; this note does not propose it now.
