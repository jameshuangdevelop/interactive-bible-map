# M5-11 research note — The ancient lands left undrawn

Research for card `docs/tasks/M5-11-ancient-lands.md`, phase 1, under ADR-0044 item 8. This note does not change any data file; it is the Research Lead's input to the Fact-Checker (phase 2) and the GIS Engineer (phase 3).

## 1. How to read this note

- **Scope.** The audit covers the 24 areas already drawn in `data/geo/ancient-areas.geojson` and `data/timeline.json`, the 15 stops (`-4, 6, 17, 34, 37, 39, 41, 44, 53, 67, 70, 72, 74, 79, 100`), and every land the card names: Polemon's kingdom of Pontus, Armenia Minor, Abilene, Chalcis, Emesa and Iturea; then Sophene, Osroene and the Bosporan kingdom; then Mauretania.
- **Method.** For the audit I read `data/timeline.json` and `data/geo/ancient-areas.geojson` directly with small scripts (listing every area, every entity's `locationId`, every place's `politicalAreaId`, and an all-pairs adjacency check with `@turf/boolean-disjoint` on areas whose bounding boxes are close) rather than guessing from the rendered map. For each land, I opened the ancient authors already in the project's bibliography (Strabo, Tacitus, Josephus, Pliny the Elder) at LacusCurtius and Perseus, the two standard reference works the project already cites (the International Standard Bible Encyclopedia, 1915; William Smith's *Dictionary of Greek and Roman Geography*, 1854), and, for coordinates, Wikidata's API and Pleiades' sanctioned bulk CSV export (`atlantides.org/downloads/pleiades/gis/pleiades_gis_data.zip`, read 2026-10-18; the live site still blocks automated fetches, per `SOURCES.md`). I also read AWMC's own `regional_name_linework` and `roman_empire_ce_200_provinces` files directly from the pinned commit to see what AWMC itself can and cannot separate.
- **Confidence** follows `schema/README.md`'s four values (`high`, `medium`, `low`, `disputed`), applied to how well a cited description or dataset fixes a *border*, not to the land's historical existence, which is usually well attested on its own.
- **Naming.** Proposed new area/entity ids are working labels for the Fact-Checker and GIS Engineer to confirm or change, exactly as the M4 research note's proposed ids were.
- Sources already opened and cited by the M4 research note and its verification (`docs/research/M4-timeline.md`, `docs/verification/M4-timeline.md`, `docs/verification/M4-ancient-geometry.md`) are carried forward without re-quoting; §7 lists only what is new to this note.

## 2. The human's example, answered directly: Syria, Cappadocia and Commagene

At every one of the 15 stops, Syria, Cappadocia and Commagene already have a holder and already share clean, drawn borders with no gap between them:

| Stop (year) | Syria's holder | Cappadocia's holder | Commagene's holder |
|---|---|---|---|
| -4 to 17 | Roman province (imperial legates, from -27) | Client kingdom, Archelaus | Client kingdom, Mithridates then Antiochus |
| 17 | (unchanged) | **Becomes a Roman province** this year | Becomes its own Roman province this year too (a separate, praetorian one, not folded into Syria) |
| 34 | Administers Philip's former tetrarchy directly, 34–37 | Roman province | Roman province |
| 38–41 | (unchanged) | Roman province | Client kingdom restored (38), then unclear (39–41, Caligula's removal is undated), then restored again (41) |
| 41–70 | (unchanged) | Roman province | Client kingdom, Antiochus IV |
| 72 | (unchanged) | Roman province (its own governor may also have governed Galatia from about 69–72, but the map keeps them separate: no ancient source states a merger) | **Annexed to Syria this year** — its area keeps its own shape but now takes Syria's own colour and label |
| 74–100 | (unchanged) | Roman province | (holds Syria's label, as above) |

I checked adjacency directly: `commagene`/`cappadocia`, `commagene`/`syria` and `cappadocia`/`syria` are all **not disjoint** (they touch) in the committed geometry, confirmed with `@turf/boolean-disjoint` on the actual polygons, not just by eye. The `commagene`/`syria` edge is separately verified in `docs/verification/M4-ancient-geometry.md` (border segment 11: "AWMC AD 69 face... Pass"). So nothing is missing exactly at this tripoint, at any stop; this part of the map already does what the human praised Achaia–Macedonia for doing.

**What the human actually saw, I think, is this:** `cappadocia`'s own drawn shape is far larger than "Cappadocia" in the ordinary sense. AWMC's own political layers draw no line at all between Cappadocia proper and three other lands next to it — Pontus Galaticus (part of the province of Galatia from 2 BC), Polemon's independent kingdom of Pontus (until 63 or 64), and Armenia Minor (held by a changing set of client kings) — so the M4-02 Research Lead's reference-town check placed all four inside *one* AWMC face, literally named by AWMC's own towns "Armenia Minor / Cappadocia / Pontus Galaticus / Pontus Polemoniacus" (`docs/research/M4-timeline.md` §2.25). I checked that finding's own inputs directly against AWMC's and Pleiades' raw data myself (§7 below) and found nothing wrong with it; I also found, independently, that the ancient sources agree with it — no ancient author separates these four either (§4.1, §4.2). The PO's ADR-0037 correction (2026-10-07) then chose, reasonably at the time, to treat Pontus and Armenia Minor as small units whose own land stays with Cappadocia, named only in Cappadocia's own prose note (`data/timeline.json`, the `cappadocia-province` 17–69 period's `note`). That is not a bug: it is the documented, deliberate result of AWMC having no line there. The reader sees one undifferentiated "Cappadocia" blob stretching from the Taurus to the Black Sea and east past the Euphrates, with no border inside it, right next to a floating "PONTUS" region label with no area around it (`pontus.json`, `type: "region"`, a label point only) — which is exactly "confusing," even though the Syria/Cappadocia/Commagene edges themselves are fine.

§3's audit and §4.1–§4.2 below report what I found: a real, citable ancient description *does* draw a line between Cappadocia proper and the rest of that AWMC face. It does not, however, give a line separating Pontus from Armenia Minor from each other, so drawing it only partly fixes the blob (it peels Cappadocia off cleanly; what remains next to it still needs to be shown as one area, or as "unclear," rather than two).

## 3. Audit

### 3.1 Timeline holders with no shape at all

Every one of the 32 entities in `data/timeline.json` holds one of the 24 drawn areas (confirmed by a direct check: every `entities[].locationId` that is set points at a place whose `politicalAreaId` is one of the 24, and every area id appears in the areas list). **No entity is currently left holding nothing.** That is because the M4-02 Research Lead's and Fact-Checker's process already found this failure mode once — for Polemon's kingdom of Pontus and for Armenia Minor specifically — and *removed* their entities rather than leave them dangling (`docs/verification/M4-timeline.md`, finding 3 and fix R12; `docs/research/M4-timeline.md` §2.25's "Rule 5" note). So today's gap is not "an entity with no area"; it is "a land that never became its own entity or area in the first place," recorded only as prose on a neighbour's period. That describes Pontus and Armenia Minor exactly, and — I found — Sophene, Osroene, the Bosporan kingdom and Mauretania too, none of which has ever had an entity or area (they are outside the M4 team's focus list entirely, except for Mauretania's political facts, which exist only as prose on `roman-empire.json` and in the M4 research note's §2.23).

### 3.2 Places inside an undrawn land

I checked every one of the 122 current location records: all have either a `politicalAreaId` that resolves to one of the 24 drawn areas, or (for 13 records) no political area at all because they are empires, candidate-split places, natural features, or hand-written regions (`media`, `mesopotamia`, `phrygia`, `pontus`, `roman-empire`, `parthian-empire`). **No current place sits inside any of the lands this card asks about.** That is expected: Abilene, Chalcis, Commagene, Emesa, Sophene, Osroene, the Bosporan kingdom and Mauretania have no place record of their own (none is a Bible place), and Pontus's and Armenia Minor's few towns (Amaseia, Trapezus, Neocaesarea, Nicopolis-in-Armenia-Minor) are not current records either. This matters for priority: nothing in the audit below is blocking a place's own border, only the area labels and the borders between areas.

### 3.3 Borders that meet no neighbour

`docs/verification/M4-ancient-geometry.md` already hunted exhaustively for this failure mode (its G1–G11 items: Raetia, the Gulf of Sirte, the Guadalquivir, the IJ, the lower Danube, the silted gulfs at Miletus and Ephesus, Raphia's coast) and fixed every one it found. I re-ran a lighter version of the same check myself — an all-pairs `@turf/boolean-disjoint` test on every pair of the 24 areas whose bounding boxes are within 0.05° of each other — and found no new case: every pair that should touch (by ordinary geography) does touch in the committed geometry, and every pair that does not touch is separated by water or by a third area, which is correct (for example Bithynia and Thrace are rightly disjoint, separated by the Bosporus and the Hellespont). **I found no new instance of this failure mode.** The open issue in this card is not a gap in the drawn 24 areas; it is land that was never carved out of them to begin with (§3.1).

## 4. The lands, one at a time

### 4.1 Polemon's kingdom of Pontus, and Pontus Galaticus

**Existing record:** `pontus.json`, `type: "region"`, a label point only, `parentId: "roman-empire"`, no `politicalAreaId`. Its own `politicalHistory` (already verified) is accurate background; summarised here only where it bears on the border.

**Can part of it be drawn?** Yes, the edge with Cappadocia, medium confidence. The Greek geographer Strabo, writing under Augustus and Tiberius, gives an explicit line:

> "The boundary of Pontus and Cappadocia is a mountain tract parallel to the Taurus, which has its beginning at the western extremities of Chammanenê, where is situated Dasmenda, a stronghold with sheer ascent, and extends to the eastern extremities of Laviansenê. Both Chammanenê and Laviansenê are prefectures in Cappadocia." (`bib:strabo-geography`, *Geography* 12.2.10, LacusCurtius; the translation is the 1928 Loeb edition, which the hosting site states is public domain, the earlier volumes' copyright having lapsed and the later volumes' never renewed)

Dasmenda is identified with high confidence: `pleiades:619147` ("Dasmenda/[Dasmendron]"), 34.726°E 38.103°N, the Barrington Atlas's own primary identification (a second, less confident candidate, `pleiades:628964` "Dasmenda?", sits about 150 km further east at 36.218°E 38.726°N; I recommend the first). Laviansene's own point is not independently pinned in the sources I opened; Strabo's own ten-prefecture list (12.1.4, already cited in the M4 note for Garsaura and Tyana) places it among Cappadocia's own districts, and a neighbouring prefecture, Sargarausene, is pinned (`pleiades:629068`, 36.621°E 38.688°N), giving the line's general easterly bearing even without an exact second anchor. This is the same kind of partial-but-usable anchor the M4-03 GIS Engineer already used for the Galatia–Cappadocia line (Tyana/Garsaura against Lake Tatta/Iconium/Lystra/Derbe), which the Fact-Checker accepted as "mostly resolved... the anchors fix the two sides, but the exact line between them is still approximate" (`docs/verification/M4-ancient-geometry.md`).

**What this line does and does not solve.** It separates Cappadocia proper from everything AWMC currently lumps in with it. It does **not** separate Pontus from Armenia Minor from each other: Strabo's own text says the two were administered, fought over and granted away together repeatedly ("Culupene and Camisenê... border on both Lesser Armenia and Laviansenê," 12.3.37, two Pontic districts under Queen Pythodoris touching both Cappadocia's Laviansene and Armenia Minor at once), and I found no source distinguishing exactly where one ended and the other began. I recommend one combined area, tentatively `pontus-armenia-minor`, bounded by: this Cappadocia line on the south; the Halys river on the west, with Paphlagonia (`bib:strabo-geography` 12.3.9, already cited in the M4 note, `pleiades:857148`); and the Black Sea coast on the north (already in the project's coastline data). Its eastern edge, toward Colchis and Greater Armenia, has no cited description I found, so it would need to stop at the current empire's edge, approximately, the same way other areas' far edges already do.

**Political complexity inside this combined area.** Pontus (held by Queen Pythodoris, then her son Polemon II) and Armenia Minor (held by a quite different sequence: Archelaus of Cappadocia, then an unnamed gap, then Cotys, then Aristobulus) are *not* the same holder for almost the whole window — see the table below. A single combined area would therefore need to show as unclear, or to default to one holder with a note on the other (the same technique `cappadocia`'s own note already uses), for nearly its whole span. This is a real trade-off and I flag it as an open question for the PO (§6, item 1) rather than choose.

| Stop | Pontus's holder | Armenia Minor's holder | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| -4 | Queen Pythodoris (regent, then ruling in her own right) | Archelaus of Cappadocia (same man as Cappadocia's own king) | `bib:strabo-geography` 12.3.29, 12.3.37; already-cited Dio 54.9.2 (Armenia Minor to Archelaus, 20 BC) |
| 6–17 | Pythodoris (at some point before 17 she married Archelaus; Strabo does not give the year) | Archelaus (until his death, 17) | `bib:strabo-geography` 12.3.29: Pythodoris "married Archelaüs and remained with him to the end" |
| 17–38 | Pythodoris, then (her own son) Polemon II confirmed 38 | **Unclear** — no source names a holder between Archelaus's death and Cotys (already flagged in `cappadocia`'s own note and in `docs/verification/M4-timeline.md`) | already-cited Dio 59.12.2 |
| 38–54 | Polemon II | Cotys (son of the king of Thrace) | already-cited Dio 59.12.2 |

| 63/64–72 | **Given up**, joins the province of Galatia as "Pontus Polemoniacus" | Aristobulus, until annexed ~72 | already-cited ISBE "Pontus"/"Galatia"; World History Encyclopedia "Vespasian" |
| 72–100 | Part of Galatia | Annexed (province, administered with Cappadocia or Galatia — the sources do not say which) | as above |

**Pontus Galaticus** (the part of old Pontus — west of Polemon's kingdom, administered with Bithynia since Pompey, then transferred to Galatia in 2 BC, per already-cited ISBE "Galatia") sits, geographically, between Bithynia and Polemon's kingdom. I found no separate cited line for it either; it would be the western part of the same combined area above, with its own 2 BC change (already named at the AD 6 stop) adding a third holder-history to track inside one shape.

**Confidence:** medium for the Cappadocia-facing edge (one precisely anchored point, one general bearing); the area's other edges and its own internal political complexity are open.

### 4.2 Armenia Minor (Lesser Armenia)

No current record. Shares the Cappadocia-facing line in §4.1 (the same Strabo passage draws Cappadocia's edge against both Pontus and Armenia Minor at once — Strabo does not distinguish which part of "the rest" borders Cappadocia at any given point along that line). Its own years and holders are in the table in §4.1. Proposed id: `armenia-minor`, or folded into `pontus-armenia-minor` as above.

Strabo's narrative description of Armenia Minor's own extent, independent of the Cappadocia line, is thin: at 12.3.1 he says only that Mithridates Eupator, before Pompey, held "the sea-coast extending to Colchis and Lesser Armenia," implying it lies east of Pontus proper and reaches toward Colchis, without a river or town marking its own far boundary. I found no further description in Tacitus, Josephus or the two standard reference works for this specific land (as opposed to the political events that happened in it, which are already well sourced in `cappadocia`'s own note). **Confidence: medium for the shared western edge with Cappadocia; no source found for its other edges.**

### 4.3 Abilene

Already thoroughly researched in the M4 note (§2.4, §2.24 #4) and confirmed unchanged by this note: I re-opened the ISBE 1915 "Abilene" entry (`bib:isbe-abilene`) in full and found nothing beyond what is already cited — Abila's location "18 Roman miles from Damascus on the way to Heliopolis," no description of the tetrarchy's own edges. It stays inside `syria`, as now. **Confidence: not drawable; no new source found.**

### 4.4 Chalcis

Already flagged by the M4 note as politically well attested but geographically undescribed (§2.5). I found one new, partial lead: the Greek geographer Strabo, describing the Massyas plain (the Beqaa valley) in the same passage that names Chalcis's rulers Ptolemy son of Mennaeus and, later, Sampsiceramus's family at Arethusa, calls Chalcis "the acropolis, as it were, of the Massyas," with "the beginning of this plain" at Laodicea near Libanus (`bib:strabo-geography`, 16.2.18, 16.2.10). This suggests Chalcis's own kingdom was centred on (perhaps controlled, rather than exactly equalled) the Massyas plain, which is itself a real, bounded valley between Mount Lebanon and the Anti-Lebanon range. But Strabo's wording describes Chalcis's *relationship* to the plain (its citadel), not a stated border, and the M4 note's own doubt flag — that the Iturean kingdom's old, larger "land from Damascus to the sea" (ISBE "Ituraea") must not be read onto the smaller, later Chalcis — applies here too: a plain's shape is not the same thing as a kingdom's border, and Mount Lebanon and Anti-Lebanon, while real ridges, are not stated anywhere I found to be Chalcis's own edges. **I recommend this stay undrawn**, inside Syria as now, but flag the Massyas-plain description for the Fact-Checker's own judgement, since it is more than the M4 note had. **Confidence: low.**

### 4.5 Emesa

Politically well documented (kings Sampsiceramus, Iamblichus, and later Azizus and Sohaemus, already cited via Josephus, *Antiquities* 20.8.4, and *Jewish War* 7, in the M4 note's bibliography scan) but, like Chalcis, geographically thin. Strabo's own account of the dynasty (16.2.10) centres it on Arethusa (modern Rastan), north of Emesa itself, "at no great distance" from Chalcis and Heliopolis, without a boundary; William Smith's *Dictionary of Greek and Roman Geography* describes only the city of Emesa itself — "reckoned by Ptolemy to... the district of Apamene, on the... eastern bank of the Orontes," with Pliny assigning it "a desert district beyond Palmyra" (`bib:smith-dictionary-emesa`, new entry, Perseus) — which locates the city, not the kingdom's edges. I found no cited line between Emesa's land and its neighbours (Chalcis, Damascus, Palmyra). **This stays undrawn**, inside Syria as now, matching the current build's own provenance note ("Small units such as Abilene, Chalcis and Emesa stay inside Syria," `data/geo/ancient-areas.geojson`). **Confidence: not drawable; no new source found.**

### 4.6 Iturea

Not a land needing its own shape. The WEB text of Luke 3:1 calls Philip "tetrarch of the region of Ituraea and Trachonitis" (`scripture:Luke 3:1`; the project's own records use the spelling "Iturea" for this area's name, per ADR-0026's majority-translation rule, which governs record titles rather than direct quotation), but the M4 note already found that Josephus's own district lists for Philip's tetrarchy never use that name, and the standard reference ISBE concludes it is "not clear whether Luke intended to indicate two separate parts of the dominion of Philip, or used names which to some extent overlapped" (`bib:isbe-ituraea`, already cited). The older, larger Iturean kingdom (under the first Lysanias, before our window) was broken up by the time this map's window opens into exactly the pieces already modelled separately: Philip's tetrarchy (`philip-tetrarchy-lands`), Abilene and Chalcis. I found no additional, distinct "Iturea" territory left over once those three are accounted for. **No new area is proposed**; this is a naming question (which lands Luke's word covers), already presented neutrally in the existing records, not a drawing gap.

### 4.7 Sophene

No current record. **This is the strongest finding in this note**, and the first candidate for the "outside the empire, where a source gives its extent" case ADR-0037 rule 5 explicitly anticipated but never used.

**Political existence.** The Roman historian Tacitus records that in AD 54, early in Nero's reign, facing a Parthian threat to Armenia, Rome granted "Lesser Armenia... to Aristobulus, the district of Sophene to Sohaemus, each receiving royal insignia" (`bib:tacitus-annals`, *Annals* 13.7; a translator's note on "Sophene" there reads "a strip of territory along the south-western frontier of Armenia"). This Sohaemus is a different man from Sohaemus of Emesa (§4.5); Tacitus elsewhere notes he "supported Vespasian in the Civil War [AD 69], and sided with Rome against the Jews," so his kingdom still stood at least that long; I found no source for when or whether it ended before our window's close.

**Its extent is described**, not just its existence. William Smith's *Dictionary* gives a real boundary:

> "SOPHENE... a district of Armenia, lying between Antitaurus and Mount Masius, separated by the Euphrates from Melitene in Armenia Minor, and by Antitaurus from Mesopotamia. Its capital was Carcathiocerta." (`bib:smith-dictionary-sophene`, new entry, Perseus)

I then opened Smith's own primary citation directly (Strabo's book 11) rather than rely on his summary alone, and Strabo confirms it in his own words, independently of Smith:

> "Mt. Masius is the mountain which lies above the Mygdonians of Mesopotamia on the south, in whose country is Nisibis, whereas Sophenê is situated in the northern parts, between Masius and Antitaurus. The Antitaurus takes its beginning at the Euphrates and the Taurus and ends towards the eastern parts of Armenia, thus on one side enclosing the middle of Sophenê... The royal city of Sophenê is Carcathiocerta." (`bib:strabo-geography`, *Geography* 11.14.2, LacusCurtius)

This matches, and extends, Strabo's own briefer mention elsewhere that "Melitenê... is situated opposite to Sophenê; and the Euphrates River flows between it and Commagenê," with the fortress Tomisa on "the far side of the river... belonging to the Cappadocians" until it was later "sold to the ruler of Sophene" (`bib:strabo-geography`, 12.2.1; already-cited Cappadocia chapter, new quotation). Taken together, three of Sophene's four sides have a named physical feature, not an invented one: the Euphrates on the west (opposite Cappadocia's Melitene district), the Antitaurus on the south (toward Mesopotamia), and Mount Masius on the north (above which, Strabo adds, lies Gordyene and then Mount Niphates — meaning Sophene's own north/east corner, toward the rest of Armenia and Gordyene, is where the description runs out). Carcathiocerta, its capital, is `wikidata:Q4069441`, 40.083°E 38.241°N; Arsamosata, a second city of Sophene (likely its later Roman-period centre), is `wikidata:Q646120` / `pleiades:874354`, 39.511°E 38.661°N (the two sources agree to five decimal places); Pleiades' own point for the district as a whole is `pleiades:874707`, 39.403°E 38.749°N.

**One caveat:** Smith's "Melitene in Armenia Minor" conflicts with Strabo's own, better-attested claim that Melitene is a prefecture of Cappadocia ("Melitenê... a tenth portion of the country," 12.1.4, 12.2.1, already cited in the M4 note), not of Armenia Minor. I do not resolve this; I flag it for the Fact-Checker, since it could affect exactly where Sophene's western line should run relative to the already-drawn Cappadocia/Pontus-Armenia-Minor land in §4.1.

**Why this is outside the empire, not a client kingdom like Commagene.** I checked directly: Carcathiocerta, Arsamosata and Sophene's own representative point all fall **outside** every one of the 24 drawn areas, including `other-roman-lands` (a point-in-polygon test against the actual committed geometry). This is not an oversight: Sophene, like Armenia and Parthia, sat in the genuinely contested Rome–Parthia borderland east of the Euphrates, where client rulers were repeatedly Roman and Parthian nominees in turn — unlike Commagene, Chalcis or Nabataea, all west of the river and never under a Parthian nominee in this window. The visual spec already has a style ready for exactly this case ("Outside the empire... none is drawn in M4"), and the schema already has the `kind: "outside-empire"` value, unused so far. **Confidence: medium** — three of four sides (west, south, north) are described by a named river or range, read directly in Strabo and confirmed independently by Smith; the north-east corner, toward Gordyene and the rest of Armenia, is not.

### 4.8 Osroene

No current record, and **I recommend it stay undrawn**, for the same reason as Armenia and Parthia. Political existence is attested: the Roman historian Tacitus names "the Arab prince Acbarus" (the Abgarid dynast more usually transliterated Abgarus) detaining a Roman-backed claimant to the Parthian throne "in the town of Edessa" in AD 49; a translator's note there calls Edessa "the capital of Osroëne" (`bib:tacitus-annals`, *Annals* 12.12). But Osroene was a Parthian-aligned buffer state throughout this window — it only became a Roman client after Trajan's Parthian war (from AD 114) and was not securely Roman until the Severan period — and I found no ancient description of its own territorial edges. **A negative finding worth recording:** Pleiades/the Barrington Atlas *does* carry entries titled "Osrhoene" (`pleiades:991406`, `pleiades:658561`, `pleiades:1001935`), each with an attached polygon — but each polygon is explicitly marked `location_precision: "rough"` and is a plain 0.5°–5° rectangle (for example `POLYGON((35 35, 40 35, 40 40, 35 40, 35 35))`), the Barrington Atlas's own placeholder for "somewhere in this grid cell," not a border. Using one would be inventing a shape, exactly what ADR-0037 item 3 forbids; I recommend against it. Edessa itself is `pleiades:658457` / `wikidata:Q1190403`, 38.787°E 37.149°N, for reference only. **Confidence: not drawable.**

### 4.9 The Bosporan kingdom

No current record, and **I recommend treating it like Britain: out of scope**, not pursued further. Its capital, Panticapaeum (modern Kerch, Crimea), is `wikidata:Q906661`, 36.469°E 45.351°N — about 45°N, far north of every other land this project draws, and outside AWMC's drawn Roman extent (confirmed the same way as Sophene and Osroene, a point-in-polygon test against the committed geometry). It is named in no New Testament passage (1 Peter 1:1's "Pontus" is the Roman-provincial region on the south shore of the Black Sea, never extended by any ancient or modern source to Crimea or the Taman peninsula), and no current place record is anywhere near it. I did not research its political history or any possible boundary beyond confirming these two points, since the card's own "out of scope" note already excludes "lands far outside the map's reach, such as Britain," and this land fits that description as well as Britain does. **Confidence: out of scope, not assessed further.**

### 4.10 Mauretania (Caesariensis and Tingitana)

No current area (its land is part of the undifferentiated `other-roman-lands`). **This is the other strong finding in this note.** Unlike the eastern lands above, Mauretania is a real, long-settled Roman province — its own existing political history is already in the M4 note (§2.23, "the empire's outer edge"): a client kingdom under Juba II, then his son Ptolemy, until Caligula had Ptolemy killed (already cited Dio 59.25, c. AD 40); a revolt under the freedman Aedemon followed; and the Roman general Suetonius Paulinus suppressed it in AD 42, reaching Mount Atlas, after which Claudius organised the territory into two provinces. I confirmed the AD 42 date and its source precisely: it is Pliny the Elder's own *Natural History*, read in the 1855 Bostock & Riley translation (public domain, hosted at Perseus; `bib:pliny-natural-history`, new entry):

> "On our entrance into Africa, we find the two Mauritanias, which, until the time of Caius Caesar [Caligula], the son of Germanicus, were kingdoms; but, suffering under his cruelty, they were divided into two provinces... Suetonius Paulinus, whom we have seen Consul in our own time, was the first Roman general who advanced a distance of some miles beyond Mount Atlas." (*Natural History* 5.1, via Perseus; the translator's own notes there date Ptolemy's death to AD 40 under Caligula and Paulinus's campaign to AD 42 under Claudius specifically)

**A cited line exists for both internal edges.** The Greek geographer Strabo, describing the coast from the Pillars of Hercules eastward under Juba II (his own contemporary), gives:

> "...the Molochath River, which forms the boundary between the lands of the Maurusians and the Masaesylians." (`bib:strabo-geography`, 17.3.6)

and, further east:

> "Between Caesareia and Tretum is a large harbour called Salda, which is now a boundary between the territories subject to Juba and the Romans." (`bib:strabo-geography`, 17.3.12)

Pliny confirms both rivers independently and by name, in a translator's footnote that quotes his description of Mauretania's two provinces: Tingitana extends "to the river Moluga, now called the Molucha and Molochath, or Malva and Malvana" (the modern Moulouya, Morocco), and "this people [east of that river] had remained in undisputed possession of the country situate between the rivers Molochath... and Ampsaga, which formed the Caesarian Mauritania" (`bib:pliny-natural-history`, 5.1, with the translator's note). So: Mauretania Tingitana's eastern edge (with Caesariensis) and Mauretania Caesariensis's eastern edge (with Numidia/Africa, inside `other-roman-lands`) are each a named river, already independently attested by two first-rank ancient geographers, with a named town (Saldae) additionally marking the Africa-facing edge under Juba II. Saldae is `pleiades:305142`, 5.058°E 36.741°N; Iol Caesarea (Mauretania Caesariensis's own capital, later Juba II's and Ptolemy's residence) is `pleiades:295279`, 2.192°E 36.607°N; Volubilis (Mauretania Tingitana's inland capital) is `pleiades:275740`, -5.555°E 34.073°N. The Molochath/Mulucha river itself is Pleiades' own "unlocated" entry (`pleiades:289941`), whose directory note nonetheless identifies it with "the Moulouya valley" — a real, mappable modern river the GIS Engineer can use via OpenStreetMap, the same technique already used for the Yarmuk, the Jordan and the Lamus.

**AWMC may also help, pending confirmation.** AWMC's own `roman_empire_ce_200_provinces` line layer has several line segments in the right region (roughly 5–9°E, 32–37°N) that a GIS Engineer should check for a usable rule-3 approximation, the same way the Cilicia/Syria and Egypt/Arabia lines already use that layer; I could not confirm from the raw file alone whether these segments actually separate Mauretania from Numidia, since this specific export's `LPOLY_`/`RPOLY_` attributes are blank (0) throughout, unlike the project's own built `ancient-areas.geojson`, whose composition process does not depend on those attributes (it polygonises the raw lines and names the resulting faces by reference towns, the same method §3.1 describes). The southern edge needs no new citation: it is already inside the existing empire's edge, which fades into the Sahara/Gaetulian tribal lands the same way the eastern empire's edge already does elsewhere, per Strabo's own account ("the parts deep in the interior are indeed mountainous and desert... held by the Gaetulians," 17.3.9, same chapter). **Confidence: medium-high** — two independent first-rank ancient sources agree on both internal rivers; the AWMC cross-check is pending; the southern edge is the existing empire's edge, unchanged.

Proposed ids: `mauretania-caesariensis`, `mauretania-tingitana`.

## 6. Open questions for the PO

1. **Pontus and Armenia Minor: one combined area, shown as unclear for most of the window, or leave as now?** §4.1 gives a real, medium-confidence line separating this land from Cappadocia proper. But once separated, the land itself has at least two, sometimes three, different simultaneous holders (Pontus under the Pythodoris/Polemon line; Armenia Minor under a quite different, sometimes gapped, sequence) for nearly the whole 4 BC – AD 100 window, with no cited line to split them further. Three ways to show this, in rising order of change from today: (a) leave both inside Cappadocia as now, since separating them does not, by itself, resolve who held what; (b) draw the one new area, defaulting to one holder's name with the other named in its own note, the same technique `cappadocia`'s current note already uses, just moved one area outward; (c) draw the one new area and show it as `uncertain` for the spans where the two differ (which is most of the window), with the detail in its note. I lean toward (b) or (c) over (a), since it is the only option that actually puts a border where the human's example points, but the choice of (b) vs (c) is a product call about how often "unclear" dashing should appear, which is the PO's to make (compare ADR-0037's own P1/P2/P3 decisions, which were exactly this kind of call).
2. **Sophene: draw it as the first "outside the empire" shape, or hold it until its north-east corner is found?** §4.7 gives three of its four sides (west, south, north); the fourth, toward Gordyene and the rest of Armenia, is not described anywhere I found. Drawing a shape with three sourced edges and one approximate one (closed off, for instance, at the existing empire's edge or at a straight line to the nearest attested town) is the same kind of compromise the project already accepts elsewhere (for instance Perea's own southern end, "a labelled rule-3 approximation," per the Fact-Checker's own M4-03 note) — but Sophene would be the very first land drawn entirely outside the empire's edge, which is a visually new thing for the map to show, not just a new area inside it. Worth the human's eyes at MC13 before committing to it.
3. **Chalcis: is the Massyas-plain description (§4.4) enough to draw a low-confidence line, or should it wait?** I recommend waiting, but the Fact-Checker may read Strabo's "acropolis of the Massyas" differently than I did.
4. **Mauretania: two provinces, or one, for the map's purposes?** The ancient sources and the existing political history already distinguish Caesariensis from Tingitana from AD 42; I assumed two areas is the better default (mirroring Cilicia Pedias/Tracheia and Lycia/Pamphylia, which are also one province pair split by one river each), but if the GIS Engineer's AWMC check cannot confirm the internal line confidently, one area ("Mauretania") with the river noted but not drawn is a fallback.
5. **Should the Sources popover's note on undrawn lands keep naming "Polemon's kingdom of Pontus" as its example**, once part of it (the Cappadocia-facing edge) is drawn? If the PO adopts option (b) or (c) above, the land still exists and is still named, just now with a border on one side; the note's wording (card's acceptance criterion: "named only those still undrawn") would need the GIS Engineer's attention regardless of which of questions 1–4 the PO answers.
