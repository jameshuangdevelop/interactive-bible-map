# M5-07 — Route places become places: research notes

Card: `docs/tasks/M5-07-route-places.md`. This file records, per batch, the sources consulted beyond each stop entry's own (already-verified) sources, and the judgement calls made turning a stop entry into a full place record.

## Batch A — the voyage to Rome (15 places)

Sidon, Myra, Cnidus, Salmone, Fair Havens, Lasea, Phoenix, Cauda, Syrtis, Syracuse, Rhegium, Forum of Appius, Three Taverns, Alexandria, Adramyttium.

Every record starts from its stop entry in `data/routes/stops.json` (as it stood before this batch): the candidate(s), with coordinates kept exactly, and the stop's own sources. Added for this batch: a full read of each place's International Standard Bible Encyclopedia (ISBE, 1915, public domain) article (already in `data/bibliography.json` as `bib:isbe-<slug>`, all cited by the stop entries already), a search of the whole WEB text (`data/reference/engwebp_vpl.txt`) for every spelling of each name, and a handful of Wikidata lookups (by QID, read 2026-10-15) to confirm a modern administrative name where the stop entry's `todayName` didn't give one directly. Two BibleGateway spelling checks (KJV and NKJV at Acts 27:12) confirmed the KJV's "Phenice" for Phoenix; no verse text was recorded or quoted from that site, only the one-word spelling, consistent with ADR-0026. One new bibliography entry was added: `bib:smith-dictionary-syrtis-major` (Smith's *Dictionary of Greek and Roman Geography*, 1854, hosted on Perseus Digital Library — the same source and hosting already used for `smith-dictionary-epeirus`, `-lindus`, `-provincia` and `-thracia`), for Syrtis's political-geography note.

### Cross-cutting judgement calls

- **Prominence: Sidon and Alexandria stay "standard."** The schema ties `prominence: "major"` directly to ADR-0029's enumerated 31 places (which get 4–7 images and an AI-reconstruction research brief, an M3.5 process this card's "out of scope" note excludes). Neither Sidon nor Alexandria is on that list, so I did not mark either "major" even though both could be argued important under ADR-0035's separate *ordering* rule (more scripture passages sort earlier on the opening map; Sidon has 12 NT passages here, more than many standard places). Making one "major" would be a milestone-level decision belonging to the PO/human, not a call for one data batch. **Flagged for the PO.**
- **Myra's `parentId` is "pamphylia," not a new "Lycia" record.** No `lycia.json` location record exists (the timeline's `lycia` area has no `locationId`), and creating one was outside this batch's 15 places. By the "about AD 50" convention, Lycia's free federation had already been ended: the Roman historian Cassius Dio records that the emperor Claudius "incorporated them in the prefecture of Pamphylia" in AD 43, seven years before the convention date — the same fact our own `pamphylia.json` already cites for its own history. So Myra's `parentId` points to `pamphylia` (skipping the non-existent "Lycia" node), the same pattern already used for Tarsus (`parentId: syria`, skipping `cilicia`) and for Bethsaida/Caesarea Philippi (`parentId: judea-province`, skipping Philip's former tetrarchy once it was absorbed). Myra's `politicalAreaId` is the timeline's own `lycia` area, so its derived `politicalHistory` still tracks Lycia's own arc (free league to 43, unclear status to 74, then the joint province), not Pamphylia's. **Flagged for the PO** as the one parent assignment resting on inference rather than a source naming Myra's own province directly.
- **Cnidus's `parentId` is "asia" directly**, the same pattern Miletus (also in Caria) already uses, since no `caria.json` region record exists. ISBE's own Cnidus article states it directly: "A city of Caria in the Roman province of Asia."
- **Syrtis has no `politicalAreaId` and `parentId: "roman-empire"`,** the same pattern already used for Phrygia and Pontus where a place's status doesn't fit one of our modeled areas. The Gulf of Sidra lay between Roman Africa (Tripolitania, not modeled anywhere in this project) and Crete-and-Cyrene; Smith's *Dictionary* calls the surrounding coast "Regio Syrtica" without assigning it to one province, so I wrote one hand-authored `politicalHistory` entry describing the hazard's location rather than picking a holder. Its `zoomTier` is "region," not "site": the card's rule only sets zoom tiers for small islands, large islands and capes, and the Syrtis is a gulf hundreds of kilometers across, closer to Malta's "large" case than to a pinpoint cape. **Flagged for the PO** (zoom tier is a judgement call the card didn't cover explicitly).
- **Fair Havens and Phoenix are `type: "site"`,** not `natural-feature`: the card's natural-feature rule covers islands, capes and the Syrtis specifically; these two are harbors/roadsteads without their own ancient civic identity, closer to how `pool-of-siloam` or `gethsemane` are modeled than to Patmos or Malta. Both are standalone points (not nested inside another of our city records), so, following `bethany-beyond-the-jordan`'s precedent for a standalone `site`, their `zoomTier` is "city," not "site."
- **Lasea is `type: "town"`** (ISBE calls it "a town on the South coast of Crete" directly).
- **Forum of Appius is `type: "town"`; Three Taverns is `type: "site"`.** ISBE states Forum of Appius "had the rank of a municipality" in the first century AD; it gives no equivalent civic-status statement for Three Taverns, which it describes only as a road station and crossroads (it became a bishopric only in later centuries, well after Paul's visit). **Flagged for the PO** as a close call resting on an argument from silence for Three Taverns.
- **Salmone, Fair Havens, Lasea, Phoenix and Cauda all carry `parentId: "crete"`** (the region), `politicalAreaId: "crete-cyrene"` (the province), matching how `crete.json` itself and `libya.json` are parented.
- **Disputed Phoenix omits `names.modern`** per the schema's rule for disputed places (its two candidates, Loutro and Finikas, each keep their own modern name in their `label`). Its summary presents both candidates neutrally and does not pick one, per house style for disputed sites.
- **Names kept from the stop entries' own resolved spellings:** Cauda/Clauda, Forum of Appius/Appii Forum/The Market of Appius, and Phoenix/Phenice (the KJV's spelling, confirmed directly against BibleGateway) all follow ADR-0026's already-settled majority-reading titles, with the minority spellings kept in `alternate` for search. Sidon's `alternate` adds "Zidon," the other King James Version spelling ISBE's own Sidon article names.
- **Sidon's `otConnections` (10 entries) are a representative selection, not every occurrence.** The WEB text names Sidon or "the Sidonians" about 35 times across Genesis, Joshua, Judges, Samuel, Kings, Chronicles, Ezra, Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, Joel and Zechariah. Citing all of them would be unlike every other record's practice (the current maximum is 4, at Damascus); I chose ten that, between them, touch every book group the card's own note named ("Genesis, Joshua, Judges, the prophets") plus Israel's historical books, each with a `ref` scoped tightly enough that its `note` describes only what that passage itself says.
- **Sidon's `scripture` array lists all 12 WEB New Testament passages** naming Sidon, since the card asks for completeness there, unlike `otConnections`.
- **Alexandria's `scripture` array includes Acts 6:9 and 18:24** ("the Alexandrians," "an Alexandrian by race"), demonyms rather than the place name itself, following the precedent `crete.json` already set by including Acts 2:11's "Cretans" the same way.

### Fix round (after the Fact-Checker's first verification, `docs/verification/M5-route-places.md`)

The Fact-Checker passed 4 of 15 records (Salmone, Fair Havens, Phoenix, Syracuse) and found 31 fixes (F1–F31) in the other 11, plus three questions for the PO (Q1–Q3). The PO ruled on all three and on the four judgement-call questions from the first draft. This section records what changed and why; `docs/PROGRESS.md` → Open questions quotes the rulings themselves.

- **The new `lycia` record (Q3, the parent-change ruling).** Acts 27:5 calls Myra "a city of Lycia," so Myra's `parentId` now points to a new region record, `lycia.json`, instead of `pamphylia`. Lycia's own history gives Cassius Dio's AD 43 report (the Lycians' revolt, Claudius reducing them to servitude and incorporating them into Pamphylia's prefecture) before the later, separate union with Pamphylia as one province under Vespasian, citing the same sources the timeline's own `lycia` area already uses for each span, so nothing here is a new historical claim. The timeline's `lycia` area now carries `locationId: "lycia"`, so selecting its name on the timeline opens the region's panel, matching the other 17 linked areas.
- **Myra's own history (F11)** is corrected the same way: Dio's report with its date, then the uncertainty (AD 43–74) last, instead of the unsupported "by the time of Paul's voyage Lycia's affairs were administered together with its neighboring province," which neither Dio nor the record's own `politicalHistory` note supported.
- **Sidon's `otConnections` (Q1)** are cut from ten to the five the PO named: Genesis 10:15-19; 1 Kings 17:8-24 (the Zarephath episode in full, which Luke 4:26 recalls); Isaiah 23:2-12 and Ezekiel 28:20-23 (the two prophets' oracles that lie behind Jesus' "more tolerable for Tyre and Sidon" sayings); and Joel 3:4-8, which the PO judged clearly strong enough to keep as the fifth. Each note now describes only its own cited range; F7's and F8's wording fixes are folded into this rewrite.
- **Sidon's summary (F1)** no longer carries Old Testament material (the firstborn-son and "great Sidon" facts stay only in `otConnections`), and F2–F6 remove or re-source every clause a cited source didn't actually state: "a little north of Tyre," the uncited "Artaxerxes III," a reversed account of the 351 BC revolt, and the law school moving the wrong direction.
- **Fair Havens is now `type: "natural-feature"` and Phoenix is now `type: "town"` (Q2).** Pleiades calls Kaloi Limenes "an embayment" and calls the Loutro candidate for Phoenix "an ancient coastal city…that served as the port of inland Anopolis," a description now quoted in that candidate's own `support` text. Both places keep their city zoom tier.
- **Syrtis (F18–F20)** needed a second Smith's *Dictionary* article, "Syrtica Regio" (new bibliography entry `bib:smith-dictionary-syrtica-regio`, read directly on Perseus), since "Syrtis Major" alone does not say which province held either shore. Syrtica Regio, the strip between the two Syrtes, "formed a part of the Roman province of Africa" after Carthage's fall; Cyrenaica, within the Roman province of Crete and Cyrene (ISBE "Crete"), lay well to the east. The politicalHistory `entity` text now matches this, and the "this project has no record for Africa" aside moved out of `history` into the entity text itself, as Phrygia's does.
- **Three Taverns (F25)** had its two welcoming parties backward: Forum of Appius, at the 43rd Roman milestone, is farther from Rome than Three Taverns at the 33rd, so the group that reached it travelled farther, not "only as far." Fixed to match ISBE's own order. The NKJV's "Three Inns" (confirmed directly at Bible Gateway, Acts 28:15) is added to `names.alternate` (F26).
- **Not applied:** the report's two Pleiades "Barrington Atlas note" modern names for Forum of Appius ("Faiti") and Three Taverns ("near Cisterna"). Reading both places' Pleiades JSON records directly (`names`, `locations[].references`, `connectsWith`) found no occurrence of either string, so I could not verify them myself and left Three Taverns' `names.modern` unset, as before; Forum of Appius uses "Near Latina" instead (Wikidata P131), which I could verify.
- **Two corrections to this research note itself**, both from the Fact-Checker's report: the OpenBible regeneration pruned eight stale slugs, not six (also `philippi` and `thessalonica`), and the WEB names Sidon or "the Sidonians" 37 times in the Old Testament, not 35 (the first count missed Deuteronomy 3:9). Both are fixed above.
- Every other fix (F9, F10, F12–F17, F19–F24, F27–F31) re-sourced a clause, fixed a citation gap, or corrected a wording overstatement against the source the report named, without changing any candidate, coordinate, type, zoom tier, prominence, parent or political-area decision beyond the two rulings above.

### Re-check round (after the Fact-Checker's re-check, `docs/verification/M5-route-places.md` §A7)

The re-check verified 9 of 15 records (Cnidus, Salmone, Fair Havens, Phoenix, Cauda, Syracuse, Rhegium, Forum of Appius, Three Taverns) and found 11 more fixes (F32–F42) in the remaining seven (Sidon, Myra, Lasea, Syrtis, Alexandria, Adramyttium) plus the new Lycia record. None of the nine verified records were touched, except Three Taverns, which the PO separately instructed me to add a modern name to (below).

- **Sidon's otConnections notes (F32, F33) are reworded, not re-chosen.** The PO's first ruling picked which five Old Testament passages to keep; it did not license the claim, added during that rewrite, that Jesus' "more tolerable for Tyre and Sidon" sayings "draw on" or have Isaiah 23 and Ezekiel 28:20-23 "lying behind" them. No cited source makes that connection, so both notes now describe only what their own verses say, with a neutral cross-reference ("Jesus also names Tyre and Sidon together...") rather than a claimed dependency. The Joel 3:4-8 note is also corrected: the "silver... gold... finest treasures" in v. 5 belong to the LORD (the speaker), not to Judah, and v. 8 describes a two-step sale (the LORD sells Tyre, Sidon and Philistia's children to Judah; Judah then sells them on to the Sabeans), which the note had collapsed into one step. The 1 Kings 17:8-24 note now says "during a drought," since the word "famine" sits just outside that cited range (it is in 17:1 and 17:7, and in Luke 4:25).
- **Myra's history (F34)** is corrected again: it had read that Vespasian, on making Lycia a province, "placed it under its own governor again," which is not what either cited source says. Suetonius states only that Vespasian made a province of Lycia (implying freedom before his reign, not after); a translator's note to Tacitus's *Histories* (2.9) separately states that Galatia, Pamphylia and Lycia already formed one province together by AD 69, a year before the Vespasian reorganization most sources date to AD 74. The reworded text states each source's own claim rather than combining them into an inference neither makes.
- **Lasea (F35, F36):** "Pleiades' and Wikidata's own coordinates" (reader-facing History text naming two databases, against ADR-0033) is now "modern gazetteers." The claim that Lasea lay near Gortyna, inland Crete's chief city, now cites `bib:isbe-phoenix` (where that detail actually appears) alongside `bib:isbe-crete`, which the summary had cited alone.
- **Syrtis (F37)** needed a correction, not just a new source: Smith's *Dictionary*'s "Cyrenaica" article (new bibliography entry `smith-dictionary-cyrenaica`, read on Perseus) states that Cyrenaica's own western boundary was fixed at the Great Syrtis itself ("the waters of the greater Syrtis washed its shore"), contradicting the prior round's claim that Cyrenaica lay "well to the east" of the gulf. History and the politicalHistory entity note are reworded to say the Syrtis lay at the boundary between Roman Africa and Cyrenaica, within the wider Crete-and-Cyrene province, and `bib:isbe-syrtis`, dropped in the prior round's rewrite, is restored alongside the two Smith's *Dictionary* articles and `bib:isbe-crete`.
- **Alexandria and Adramyttium's "grain ship" wording (F38, F39).** Acts names two different ships of Alexandria: the one Paul's centurion found at Myra (Acts 27:6), which the ISBE calls a grain ship, and another, wintered on Malta, that later carried Paul's party to Italy (Acts 28:11) — Acts does not say the second ship also carried grain. The prior wording let "a grain ship according to the ISBE" read as describing both; both records now attach that description only to the Myra ship, with `scripture:Acts 27:38` (the crew jettisoning the wheat) cited alongside Acts 27:6 for the grain cargo itself.
- **Lycia (F40–F42).** Pleiades' own description, "Lycia was a region of southwestern Asia Minor," replaces "the rugged country at the southwestern tip of Asia Minor": the ISBE's "southeast" is a source error (Wikidata's and Pleiades' own coordinates for Lycia, about 31 km apart, both sit in Asia Minor's southwest), so the claim now cites Pleiades (place 638965) directly rather than the ISBE. The AD 43 Cassius Dio report and the Vespasian-era union, previously stated in the summary, move into a new History entry ending with the status-uncertain caveat last, per ADR-0041 item 1 — matching how Myra's own history states the same facts. `parentId` changes from `galatia` to `pamphylia`: Dio names "the prefecture of Pamphylia" specifically as what Lycia was incorporated into in AD 43, not Galatia, so Pamphylia is the better-attested parent for the span this project models (Lycia's `politicalAreaId` stays `lycia`, so its derived `politicalHistory` is unaffected by the parent change). Finally, "Lycia's cities of greatest interest to Bible students" is corrected to match the ISBE's own sentence, "Patara, Myra and Phaselis are of interest to Bible students."
- **Three Taverns' modern name (PO instruction, not a numbered fix).** The Fact-Checker found "near Cisterna" in Pleiades' `details` field (the Barrington Atlas notes) for place 423106, which my own search of `names`, `locations[].references` and `connectsWith` had missed in the prior round. `names.modern` now reads "Near Cisterna," citing the same Pleiades entry already in this record's candidate and summary sources. Forum of Appius, verified in the re-check, is unchanged ("Near Latina").

## Batch B — Paul's journeys (14 places)

Seleucia, Attalia, Samothrace, Amphipolis, Apollonia (Macedonia), Assos, Mitylene, Chios, Samos, Trogyllium, Cos, Rhodes, Patara, Ptolemais.

Every record starts from its stop entry in `data/routes/stops.json` (as it stood before this batch): the candidate(s), with coordinates kept exactly, and the stop's own sources. Added for this batch: a full read of each place's ISBE article (all already in `data/bibliography.json`, cited by the stop entries already), a search of the whole WEB text for every spelling of each name (confirmed complete: every place's own NT occurrence count matches what is now in its `scripture` array, including a fourth occurrence of "the country of the Gadarenes" at Luke 8:37 that batch C's stop entry had not carried over — see below), and each site's Pleiades JSON `details` field (card item 7), which surfaced one improvement: Seleucia's Barrington Atlas note gives "Kapısuyu/Kaboussié" as the modern village next to the ruin, more specific than the stop's own "Hatay Province, Türkiye" (a Wikidata "located in" statement one administrative level up), so `names.modern` is now "Near Kapısuyu," citing the same Pleiades entry already in the candidate's sources. Every other site's Barrington note matched what the stop entry already had (Attalia "Antalya," Samothrace "Samothrake," Amphipolis "Amphipolis," Apollonia "near Polina," Assos "Beyramkale," Mitylene "Mytilene, on Lesbos," Chios "Khios"/"GRE" (uninformative), Samos "Samos," Trogyllium "Dip Burnu," Cos "Kos," Patara "Gelemiş," Ptolemais "Tell Acco" (the precise archaeological mound; "Acre" is kept as the modern name, since it is what orients readers to a real, named, continuously inhabited city, per ADR-0028's "usefulness" principle), so none of those needed a change. Rhodes's `details` field holds a historical aside ("In 164 BC, Rhodes came under Roman control"), not a modern-name note; per ADR-0017 this is a database's own comment, not a cited historical source, and it is not used (a separately sourced, fuller Roman-era history is given instead, below).

No new bibliography entries were needed: every ISBE article, and the classical sources used for Rhodes and Samos (below), were already in `data/bibliography.json` from earlier tasks.

### Types, tiers and parents (card item 6)

- **Islands kept as place-defining names, with no town of their own distinct from the island (Samothrace, Chios, Samos, Cos, Rhodes), are `type: "natural-feature"`,** matching the stop entries' own candidate labels ("(island)"). Samothrace, Chios, Cos and Samos follow Patmos: city tier. **Rhodes follows Malta: region tier** (card item 6), since Acts' "the next day to Rhodes" is usually read, like "the island was called Malta" (Acts 28:1), as naming the island the ship called at, not only its city; the single kept candidate (the city of Rhodes, at the island's northeastern tip, where the ship in fact would have called) is unchanged, and the candidate's own support quotes ISBE's "an island (and city)" to state why both readings describe the same record. **Trogyllium, a promontory, is `type: "natural-feature"` at site tier** (card item 6).
- **Mitylene is `type: "city"`:** Acts names the city itself ("came to Mitylene"), not the island of Lesbos, which the New Testament never names; parented directly to `asia`, the same pattern Patmos, Cnidus, Miletus and Troas already use for Aegean sites with no modeled sub-region of their own. Chios, Samos, Cos and Trogyllium are parented to `asia` the same way.
- **Assos is `type: "city"`, parented to `mysia`:** unlike Troas, whose own record gives reasons for *not* parenting it to Mysia (ISBE's "Asia" article lists Mysia and the Troad as separate lands), ISBE's "Assos" article states directly, with no hedge, "An ancient city of Mysia in the Roman province of Asia," the same unambiguous wording Adramyttium's own (verified) record already relies on for the same parent.
- **Seleucia and Ptolemais are parented to `syria`,** the same pattern Sidon and Tyre already use (no modeled "Phoenicia" region); their `politicalHistory` is the identical two-entry table those verified records already carry, since both are coastal cities of the same Roman province throughout.
- **Attalia is parented to `pamphylia`,** the same pattern Perga already uses, with the identical "status uncertain" table Perga and Pamphylia's own verified records carry.
- **Samothrace, Amphipolis and Apollonia are parented to `macedonia`.** Samothrace lies off the Thracian coast, not Macedonia's, but the closest precedent already in the dataset, Neapolis — explicitly "on the coast of Thrace" in its own (verified) summary — is itself parented to `macedonia`, since the Roman province's own borders ran along this coast past the older ethnic-geographic boundary of Thrace; Samothrace follows the same reasoning. All three share Macedonia's own four-entry `politicalHistory` table, already verified on Macedonia, Philippi, Thessalonica and Neapolis.
- **Patara is parented to the new `lycia` record** (batch A), the same `politicalAreaId` and three-entry `politicalHistory` table Myra already carries, since Acts never distinguishes the two Lycian cities' standing and ISBE's own "Lycia" article names Patara alongside Myra throughout.

### Samos and Rhodes: no `politicalAreaId`, a fully sourced `politicalHistory`

Both islands' Roman-era status changed in ways the dataset's modeled areas do not capture, and — unlike Syrtis, which simply sits outside every modeled area — both genuinely spent real stretches of this project's AD -4–101 window as free states that were not inside the province of Asia at all. Running `validate:data` confirmed that any record linked to a `politicalAreaId` (place- or candidate-level) must reproduce that area's own timeline-derived table exactly (`REQUIRE_DERIVED_POLITICAL_HISTORY` is hard-coded `true` in `scripts/validate-data.mjs`, unlike the library's own off-by-default setting), so a record whose own sourced history does not match Asia's unbroken table cannot carry `politicalAreaId: "asia"` while also stating that history. Both records therefore follow Syrtis's precedent: `parentId: "asia"` (the nearest province, and, for each island, the one it belonged to for most of the modeled span) but no `politicalAreaId`, with every `politicalHistory` interval sourced directly:
- **Samos:** ISBE's own "Samos" article states the island "was joined to the Roman province of Asia" in 84 BC and "in 17 BC it became a civitas libera, through the favor of Augustus"; Suetonius (*Vespasian* 8, already cited by Myra and Lycia for the same emperor's reforms elsewhere) separately lists Samos among the places Vespasian "made provinces... taking away their freedom." Suetonius gives no year for this; the table uses AD 70, the start of his reign, as an approximate date, stated as such in the entry's own `note`.
- **Rhodes:** ISBE's "Rhodes" article independently dates "part of the Roman province of Asia" to 44 AD. Reading Cassius Dio's *Roman History* directly (book 60, the year of Claudius's British triumph) finds the specific act: "The Rhodians were deprived of their liberty because they had impaled some Romans" (60.24.4) — matching ISBE's year exactly, and far more specific than ISBE's own unsourced-sounding date. Reading Tacitus's *Annals* directly (book 12, the year of Nero's marriage to Octavia) finds the restoration: "the Rhodians recovered their liberties, so often forfeited or confirmed as the balance varied between their military services abroad or their seditious offences at home" (12.58, AD 53). Suetonius's *Vespasian* 8 again supplies the final loss, undated, so AD 70 is used the same way as for Samos. Each source is cited only for what it itself states (Dio for 44, Tacitus for 53, Suetonius for the final change, ISBE for corroborating 44 independently); no source is asked to support a date it does not give.

Both records' `history[]` gives this same span in full prose, and `politicalHistory` is the dated version of the same facts. **Flagged for the PO:** whether this dual "no `politicalAreaId`, but a full sourced table" treatment is the right call for a "standard" place, or whether a shorter treatment (a single `asia-province` entry plus a history note, as Cnidus's and Mitylene's own "free city" status is handled) would fit the card's scope better; the fuller treatment was chosen because, unlike Cnidus's or Mitylene's undated free-city status, Samos's and Rhodes's own sources give precise, well-attested years for each change.

### Other judgement calls

- **"Country of the Gadarenes" aside:** this disputed site (batch C) needed the same "no `politicalAreaId`" treatment for a different reason — see below.
- **Cnidus's and Mitylene's free-city status** (ISBE: Cnidus "held the rank of a free city" within Asia; Mitylene was "made a free city by Pompey") is undated and does not interrupt either record's ordinary provincial holder, so, matching Cnidus's own already-verified treatment (batch A), it is stated only in `history[]`, not as a `politicalHistory` break.
- **Chios's visit from Herod the Great** (ISBE "Chios," citing Josephus, *Antiquities* 16.2.2): reading the Loeb-equivalent CCEL translation of that chapter directly (Whiston's, already `bib:josephus-antiquities`) found the fuller story — Herod, sailing to join the Roman general Marcus Agrippa (not to be confused with the Biblical Herod Agrippa) on campaign toward the Bosphorus, was held up by a north wind at the island the translation spells "Chius," where he funded the rest of a stoa's rebuilding that the city alone could not afford. Cited directly from Josephus, alongside ISBE's own shorter summary of the same episode.
- **Ptolemais's modern name stays "Acre," not the more archaeologically precise "Tell Acco"** that Pleiades' own Barrington note gives, since ADR-0028 prioritizes a name ordinary readers can place; Acre is a real, named, inhabited city occupying substantially the same site, unlike Cnidus's or Forum of Appius's sites, which have no town of their own at all.
- **Attalia, Samothrace, Amphipolis, Apollonia, Assos, Mitylene, Chios, Samos, Trogyllium, Cos, Rhodes and Patara's `otConnections` are all empty.** None of these Greek or Hellenistic foundations is named anywhere in the Old Testament (checked directly against the whole WEB text); Seleucia is likewise an empty array for the same reason. **Ptolemais is the one exception:** the International Standard Bible Encyclopedia's own "Ptolemais" article states it is "Same as 'Acco' in Jg 1:31," so it carries one `otConnections` entry for that verse.

## Batch C — Jesus' journeys (3 places)

The country of the Gadarenes (Gerasenes/Gergesenes), Bethphage, Ephraim.

### The country of the Gadarenes: a disputed site spanning two political spheres

The stop entry's three candidates sit in genuinely different first-century jurisdictions: Kursi, on the lake's own shore, lies in Gaulanitis, within Philip's former tetrarchy (the same `philip-tetrarchy-lands` timeline area Bethsaida and Caesarea Philippi already use); Gadara and Gerasa are both Decapolis cities, which ISBE's own "Decapolis" article describes as "independent of the local tetrarchy, and answerable directly to the governor of Syria" from about the time of Pompey's settlement of the region (65 BC). Reading ISBE's "Gadara" article directly adds a further wrinkle for Gadara specifically (though not Gerasa, where no such episode is recorded): Augustus gave the city to Herod the Great in 30 BC, so for 26 years, until Herod's death in 4 BC, it was part of his kingdom, not Syria's direct territory — the same source credits Pompey with first giving the city "a free constitution" in 63 BC, before that.

The schema allows a `politicalHistory` entry per `candidates[]` index, keyed to that candidate's own `politicalAreaId`, and the first draft of this record used exactly that: Kursi tagged to `philip-tetrarchy-lands` (copying Bethsaida's and Caesarea Philippi's own verified table verbatim), Gadara and Gerasa both tagged to `syria`. This failed `validate:data`'s derived-political-history check (above), since Gadara's and Gerasa's own sourced tables (with the free-city and Herodian-era intervals) do not match the plain `syria` area's own two-entry table. Rather than flattening Gadara's and Gerasa's own documented history to match that simpler table, every `politicalAreaId` was removed from this record's candidates, the same choice already made for Samos and Rhodes, and every `politicalHistory` interval (fourteen, across the three candidates) is individually sourced, using real timeline holder ids (`herod-the-great`, `philip-tetrarchy`, `roman-province-syria`, `agrippa-i-kingdom`, `roman-province-judea`, `agrippa-ii-kingdom`) wherever one genuinely applies, and a plain sourced label (no `holderId`) only for Gadara's own pre-Herodian free period, which matches no existing timeline entity.

**`parentId` is `judea-province`, flagged for the PO.** This is a genuine three-way judgement call, not a clean majority: Kursi (confidence `disputed`) is the only candidate on the lake shore and the only one in Philip's former tetrarchy, which this project's two existing precedents for the same territory (Bethsaida, Caesarea Philippi) both parent to `judea-province`; Gadara (also `disputed`) and Gerasa (`low`) are both Decapolis cities under Syria's governor. Counting candidates by area gives Syria a plurality (two of three, though one is the record's lowest-confidence candidate); counting by the precedent this project already has for lake-adjacent Gaulanitis sites favors `judea-province`. This note follows the second reasoning, since Kursi is the only candidate the event's own narrative (a boat crossing to "the other side" of the lake, a herd rushing down a bank directly into the water) fits without qualification, but the choice is close, and `syria` is a fully defensible alternative.

**`otConnections` is empty.** ISBE's "Gerasa" article describes the Kursi candidate's shore as where "the steep edges of the Bashan plateau drop close upon the brink of the lake," which could suggest a connection to the Old Testament region of Bashan (Og's kingdom, Deuteronomy 3:1-11); this was not added, since the connection would apply unevenly across the record's three candidates (Gadara and Gerasa lie south of the Yarmuk, in the territory the Old Testament calls Gilead, not Bashan) and no source states that this specific disputed site lies in Bashan.

**One missing New Testament occurrence, corrected.** A full WEB search for "Gadarenes" found three hits, not the two the stop entry's `scripture` array carried: Mark 5:1, Luke 8:26, and Luke 8:37 ("All the people of the surrounding country of the Gadarenes asked him to depart from them"), which the stop entry, built around the route's own stage boundaries rather than every naming of the place, had not included. All four NT verses naming the place by any of its three readings (Matthew 8:28, Mark 5:1, Luke 8:26, Luke 8:37) are now in `scripture`.

### Bethphage

`parentId` is `jerusalem`, not `judea` (which Bethany, its constant companion in the Synoptics, itself uses): Bethphage's site, Et-Tur (Kafr et-Tur), sits inside modern Jerusalem's built-up area on the Mount of Olives ridge, unlike Bethany's modern town, Al-Eizariya, a separate West Bank municipality outside the city; this follows the same parent every other Mount-of-Olives-ridge record already uses (Gethsemane, Golgotha, the Mount of Olives, the Temple Mount, the pools of Bethesda and Siloam), all likewise exempt from `names.modernCountries` under ADR-0028's Jerusalem rule. `politicalAreaId` and the six-entry `politicalHistory` table are the same `judea-samaria-idumea` table Jericho's own (verified) record already carries. `names.alternate` now holds "Bethsphage," the WEB's own manuscript reading at all three passages (the PO's final title ruling kept "Bethphage" as the title, since the six compared English versions do not differ among themselves; the schema's `names.alternate` field, which the stop-entry schema lacked, now lets the WEB's own spelling stay searchable).

**One `otConnections` entry: Zechariah 9:9.** Matthew's own account states the reason for the donkey and its colt directly — "Now all this has happened, that it might be fulfilled which was spoken through the prophet" (21:4), quoting Zechariah 9:9's "your King comes to you... riding on a donkey" at 21:5 — so this is the Gospel's own stated connection (per ADR-0017, `scripture:` support for what a passage itself says), not an inference. Mark's and Luke's own accounts of the same triumphal-entry scene do not include this citation.

### Ephraim

`parentId` is `judea`; `politicalAreaId` and `politicalHistory` reuse Jericho's own six-entry `judea-samaria-idumea` table, since the sources place Ephraim in the same hill country, near the edge of the Judean wilderness.

**Three `otConnections` entries, from ISBE's own cross-reference.** ISBE's "Ephraim (2)" article (the stop's main source) ends "See EPHRON," which leads to ISBE's "Ephron (2)" article (a new bibliography entry, `isbe-ephron-2`, by the same author, E. W. G. Masterman): its own first paragraph states that 2 Chronicles 13:19's "Ephron" (margin: "Ephraim"), Joshua 18:23's "Ophrah," and 2 Samuel 13:23's "Ephraim," by Baal-hazor, are "thought by many to be identical" with each other, "localized at the lofty town of eT Taiyibeh" — the same site identification the stop entry's own candidate already rests on. All three Old Testament references are added, each with ISBE's own hedge ("thought by many," not asserted as certain). The quite different, better-known "hill country of Ephraim" (the tribe's own territory, further north) is not connected here: ISBE's "Ephraim (2)" article is explicit that this is a separate, smaller place from the tribal territory, identified only with the three specific verses above.

## Fix round (batches B and C, after the Fact-Checker's verification, `docs/verification/M5-route-places.md`)

The Fact-Checker passed 3 of 14 batch B records (Attalia, Assos, Cos) and all of batch C's structure, but found 29 fixes (F46–F74) across the rest, plus three questions (Q1–Q3). The PO ruled on all three directly. This section records what changed and why.

- **Q2 (island zoom tier) applied by area.** The PO ruled that islands larger than Malta take region tier. Malta's own record cites `wikidata:Q233` for a land area of 316 km²; checked the same way (Wikidata's P2046 claim, read 2026-10-16), Chios is 842.5 km² and Samos 476 km², both larger, so both move from `city` to `region` tier, joining Rhodes (already `region`). Cos, at 290.3 km², is smaller than Malta and keeps `city` tier, unchanged from the verified draft. Samothrace, at 178–184 km² (Wikidata carries two differing claims, both well under Malta's), also keeps `city` tier.
- **Q3 (Samothrace's political placement) resolved as the PO's option (a).** No cited source places Samothrace in the province of Macedonia (F47): ISBE's own "Samothrace," "Macedonia" and the Livius "Macedonia" article the prior `politicalHistory` table cited never name the island, and the ancient map layer already draws it in `other-roman-lands`, not `macedonia`. Smith's *Dictionary of Greek and Roman Geography* (new bibliography entry, `smith-dictionary-samothrace`, read directly on Perseus) states plainly, "In Pliny's time Samothrace was a free state." Following the Syrtis precedent the PO named — a `natural-feature` parented directly to `roman-empire`, no `politicalAreaId`, a single hand-written `politicalHistory` entry whose `entity` text itself carries the caveat — Samothrace's `parentId` is now `roman-empire` and its `politicalHistory` is one entry (−4 to 101) reading "Free state under Roman rule," sourced to Smith's own sentence, with a note explaining that "Pliny's time" means the mid-to-late first century AD and that no source dates when this status began or whether it changed within the span this map covers.
- **Ruling 2 (the Gadarenes' `parentId` is `syria`) supersedes this file's earlier batch C note**, which had flagged the choice between `judea-province` (Kursi's precedent) and `syria` (Gadara's and Gerasa's) for the PO. The Fact-Checker found that a cited source supports Syria for every one of the three candidate areas, including Kursi: ISBE's "Gadara" states Gadara's own jurisdiction, as the chief city of the district, "extended over the country East of the Sea, including the lands of the subordinate town, GERASA," and ISBE's "Gerasa" places that subordinate town at Kursi itself ("Gerasa itself is probably represented by the ruins of Kurseh"). So Kursi's shore is, on the record's own cited sources, Gadara's territory, which "after Herod's death... was joined to the province of Syria, 4 BC." Kursi's seven-entry `politicalHistory` table, copied from Bethsaida's and Caesarea Philippi's own Philip's-tetrarchy table, is replaced with Gadara's own three-entry table (free city 63–30 BC, Herod the Great 30–4 BC, Syria 4 BC–AD 101), each note stating that ISBE's attribution runs through Gadara's jurisdiction rather than Kursi's own attested history, and the last note stating plainly that the map's ancient layer still draws this shore inside Philip's former tetrarchy, as a drawing simplification the records no longer follow.
- **Bethphage's `parentId` changes to `judea` (Q1), not `jerusalem`.** The PO ruled this directly, for consistency with its constant Synoptic companions Bethany and Emmaus, both `judea`, keeping the place → region chain rather than skipping straight to the city. This takes Bethphage out of ADR-0028's Jerusalem-parent exemption, which depends on the `parentId` chain actually including the `jerusalem` record, not on whether `names.modern` happens to contain the word "Jerusalem." The record therefore gains `names.modernCountries: ["West Bank"]` — the same value Bethany itself carries, both sites being on the Jerusalem side of the Green Line that the rest of this project already treats as West Bank for Bethany, Emmaus and Jericho. **This is not simply the Fact-Checker's literal F72 instruction** ("restore the stop entry's deleted ', Jerusalem' suffix on `names.modern`"), which was written to fit the old `parentId: "jerusalem"` framing before the PO's ruling changed the parent; restoring that suffix while also adding a country would read oddly next to Bethany's own bare "Al-Eizariya," so `names.modern` stays "Et-Tur (Kafr et-Tur)," unchanged, matching Bethany's pattern instead. Flagged here for the Fact-Checker's attention, since it departs from F72's literal wording.
- **The Victory of Samothrace's date (F48).** ISBE's 1915 dating ("set up here by Demetrius Poliorcetes circa 300 BC") is the Louvre's own catalogue entry for the statue (accession Ma 2369, new bibliography entry `louvre-winged-victory`, read 2026-10-16), which instead dates it "-200 / -175," findspot "Samothrace Paléopolis... (Cabirion)," discovered in 1863, place of creation "Rhodes (?)." The record now gives the Louvre's dating first and ISBE's older view last, explicitly marked as since-revised, per ADR-0041 item 1. **License note for the Fact-Checker:** the Louvre's collections site states, in its terms of use, that short quotation of its pages is permitted with attribution; this record cites only factual catalogue data (a date range, a findspot, a discovery year), not its descriptive prose or any image, the same cite-only treatment already given to custodia.org and livius.org elsewhere in this project's bibliography.
- **Samos's and Rhodes's post-Augustan dates (F55, F56, F59, F60).** Checked Cassius Dio directly (54.7.4, 54.9.7): Augustus wintered on Samos twice, the second time in the year of the consuls Marcus Apuleius and Publius Silius (20 BC), when Dio states he "gave the islanders their freedom" — three years earlier than ISBE's own "17 BC," which the record now keeps only as the encyclopedia's separate figure, in a note. For Vespasian's withdrawal of freedom from Samos and Rhodes (Suetonius, *Vespasian* 8.4, undated), the record had used "the start of his reign, AD 70" as an invented approximate date; Tacitus (*Histories* 2.79) and Suetonius (*Vespasian* 6.3) both date Vespasian's accession itself to 1 July, AD 69, so "AD 70" was also simply wrong. Samos now follows the same 70–79 `uncertain-roman-side` span the verified timeline already uses for Achaia's identical Vespasian sentence, becoming part of the province of Asia again from 79 (citing ISBE's "Asia" article, which names Samos "apparently" among the province's islands). Rhodes, which ISBE never places back in a named province after Vespasian (its "Asia" article's island list does not include Rhodes), keeps `uncertain-roman-side` for the whole span from 70 to 101, rather than inventing a return to a named province with no source for it.
- **Introductions added (ADR-0033):** "King Philip of Macedon" (Samothrace), "the Roman general Pompey" (the Gadarenes), "the Maccabees" (Ptolemais and Samos, each independently, since each record's own text is self-contained — new bibliography entry `isbe-maccabees`, ISBE's "Maccabaeus; Maccabees" article), "Westcott and Hort, The New Testament in Greek" (the Gadarenes, following ISBE "Gerasa"'s own introduction of the same edition), and "the Talmud" (Bethphage, new bibliography entry `isbe-talmud`), each with a brief, sourced descriptor at first mention within its own record.
- **Ephraim's Old Testament notes (F73) were a genuine misreading, not just a wording slip.** The prior draft's three `otConnections` notes had all three of Joshua 18:23 (Ophrah), 2 Samuel 13:23 (Ephraim, by Baal-hazor) and 2 Chronicles 13:19 (Ephron/Ephraim) "localized" together at et-Taiyibeh. Re-reading ISBE's "Ephron (2)" article directly shows it hedges the 2 Samuel link specifically with "perhaps," a weaker hedge than "thought by many" for the other two; more importantly, ISBE's "Ephraim (2)" article — this record's own main source — treats 2 Samuel 13:23's Ephraim as a separate entry, "(1)," probably Eusebius's Ephraim 20 Roman miles north of Jerusalem, near Sinjil and el-Lubban, distinct from entry "(2)," John's Ephraim, "probably" et-Taiyibeh. Rather than present the record's own main source and its cross-referenced source as disagreeing, 2 Samuel 13:23 is dropped from `otConnections`, keeping only Joshua 18:23 and 2 Chronicles 13:19, which both sources agree are "thought by many" identical with each other and localized at et-Taiyibeh.
- Every other fix (F46, F50–F54, F57, F58, F61–F64, F69, F70–F72, F74) re-sourced a clause, fixed a citation gap or attribution, or corrected a wording overstatement against the source the Fact-Checker's report named, without changing any candidate, coordinate, type or prominence decision beyond the tier and parent changes above.
