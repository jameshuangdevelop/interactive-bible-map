# M5-07 — Route places become places: research notes

Card: `docs/tasks/M5-07-route-places.md`. This file records, per batch, the sources consulted beyond each stop entry's own (already-verified) sources, and the judgement calls made turning a stop entry into a full place record.

## Batch A — the voyage to Rome (15 places)

Sidon, Myra, Cnidus, Salmone, Fair Havens, Lasea, Phoenix, Cauda, Syrtis, Syracuse, Rhegium, Forum of Appius, Three Taverns, Alexandria, Adramyttium.

Every record starts from its stop entry in `data/routes/stops.json` (as it stood before this batch): the candidate(s), with coordinates kept exactly, and the stop's own sources. Added for this batch: a full read of each place's International Standard Bible Encyclopedia (ISBE, 1915, public domain) article (already in `data/bibliography.json` as `bib:isbe-<slug>`, all cited by the stop entries already), a search of the whole WEB text (`data/reference/engwebp_vpl.txt`) for every spelling of each name, and a handful of Wikidata lookups (by QID, read 2026-10-15) to confirm a modern administrative name where the stop entry's `todayName` didn't give one directly. Two BibleGateway spelling checks (KJV and NKJV at Acts 27:12) confirmed the KJV's "Phenice" for Phoenix; no verse text was recorded or quoted from that site, only the one-word spelling, consistent with ADR-0026. One new bibliography entry was added: `bib:smith-dictionary-syrtis-major` (Smith's *Dictionary of Greek and Roman Geography*, 1854, hosted on Perseus Digital Library — the same source and hosting already used for `smith-dictionary-epeirus`, `-lindus`, `-provincia` and `-thracia`), for Syrtis's political-geography note.

### Cross-cutting judgement calls

- **Prominence: Sidon and Alexandria stay "standard."** The schema ties `prominence: "major"` directly to ADR-0029's enumerated 31 places (which get 4–7 images and an AI-reconstruction research brief, an M3.5 process this card's "out of scope" note excludes). Neither Sidon nor Alexandria is on that list, so I did not mark either "major" even though both could be argued important under ADR-0035's separate *ordering* rule (more scripture passages sort earlier on the opening map; Sidon has 12 NT passages here, more than many standard places). Making one "major" would be a milestone-level decision belonging to the PO/human, not a call for one data batch. **Flagged for the PO.**
- **Myra's `parentId` is "pamphylia," not a new "Lycia" record.** No `lycia.json` location record exists (the timeline's `lycia` area has no `locationId`), and creating one was outside this batch's 15 places. By the "about AD 50" convention, Lycia's free federation had already been ended: the Roman historian Cassius Dio records that the emperor Claudius "incorporated them in the prefecture of Pamphylia" in AD 43, seven years before the convention date — the same fact our own `pamphylia.json` already cites for its own history. So Myra's `parentId` points to `pamphylia` (skipping the non-existent "Lycia" node), the same pattern already used for Tarsus (`parentId: syria`, skipping `cilicia`) and for Bethsaida/Caesarea Philippi (`parentId: judea-province`, skipping Philip's former tetrarchy once it was absorbed). Myra's `politicalAreaId` is the timeline's own `lycia` area, so its derived `politicalHistory` still tracks Lycia's own arc (free league to 43, unclear status to 74, then the joint province), not Pamphylia's. **Flagged for the PO** as the one parent assignment resting on inference rather than a source naming Myra's own province directly.
- **Cnidus's `parentId` is "asia" directly**, the same pattern Miletus (also in Caria) already uses, since no `caria.json` region record exists. ISBE's own Cnidus article states it directly: "A city of Caria in the Roman province of Asia."
- **Syrtis has no `politicalAreaId` and `parentId: "roman-empire"`,** the same pattern already used for Phrygia and Pontus where a place's status doesn't fit one of our modeled areas. The Gulf of Sidra lay between Roman Africa (Tripolitania, not modeled anywhere in this project) and Crete-and-Cyrene; Smith's *Dictionary* calls the surrounding coast "Regio Syrtica" without assigning it to one province, so I wrote one hand-authored `politicalHistory` entry describing the hazard's location rather than picking a holder. Its `zoomTier` is "region," not "site": the card's rule only sets zoom tiers for small islands, large islands and capes, and the Syrtis is a gulf hundreds of kilometers across, closer to Malta's "large" case than to a pinpoint cape. **Flagged for the PO** (zoom tier is a judgement call the card didn't cover explicitly).
- **Fair Havens and Phoenix are `type: "site"`,** not `natural-feature`: the card's natural-feature rule covers islands, capes and the Syrtis specifically; these two are harbors/roadsteads without their own ancient civic identity, closer to how `pool-of-siloam` or `gethsemane` are modeled than to Patmos or Malta. Both are standalone points (not nested inside another of our city records), so, following `bethany-beyond-the-jordan`'s precedent for a standalone `site`, their `zoomTier` is "city," not "site."
- **Lasea is `type: "town"`** (ISBE calls it "a town on the South coast of Crete" directly).
- **Forum of Appius is `type: "town"`; Three Taverns is `type: "site"`.** ISBE states Forum of Appius "had the rank of a municipality" in the first century AD; it gives no equivalent civic-status statement for Three Taverns, which it describes only as a road station and crossroads (it became a bishopric only in later centuries, well after Paul's visit). **Flagged for the PO** as a close call resting on an argument from silence for Three Taverns.
- **Salmone, Fair Havens, Lasea, Phoenix and Cauda all carry `parentId: "crete"`** (the region), `politicalAreaId: "crete-cyrene"` (the province), matching how `crete.json` itself and `libya.json` are parented.
- **Disputed Phoenix omits `names.modern`** per the schema's rule for disputed places (its two candidates, Loutro and Finikas, each keep their own modern name in their `label`). Its summary presents both candidates neutrally and does not pick one, per house style for disputed sites.
- **Names kept from the stop entries' own resolved spellings:** Cauda/Clauda, Forum of Appius/Appii Forum/The Market of Appius, and Phoenix/Phenice (the KJV's spelling, confirmed directly against BibleGateway) all follow ADR-0026's already-settled majority-reading titles, with the minority spellings kept in `alternate` for search. Sidon's `alternate` adds "Zidon," the other King James Version spelling ISBE's own Sidon article names.
- **Sidon's `otConnections` (10 entries) are a representative selection, not every occurrence.** The WEB text names Sidon or "the Sidonians" about 35 times across Genesis, Joshua, Judges, Samuel, Kings, Chronicles, Ezra, Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, Joel and Zechariah. Citing all of them would be unlike every other record's practice (the current maximum is 4, at Damascus); I chose ten that, between them, touch every book group the card's own note named ("Genesis, Joshua, Judges, the prophets") plus Israel's historical books, each with a `ref` scoped tightly enough that its `note` describes only what that passage itself says.
- **Sidon's `scripture` array lists all 12 WEB New Testament passages** naming Sidon, since the card asks for completeness there, unlike `otConnections`.
- **Alexandria's `scripture` array includes Acts 6:9 and 18:24** ("the Alexandrians," "an Alexandrian by race"), demonyms rather than the place name itself, following the precedent `crete.json` already set by including Acts 2:11's "Cretans" the same way.

### Mechanical changes in the same batch

- `data/routes/routes.json`: every `{"kind": "route-stop", "stopId": "<batch-A id>"}` reference (23 in all: `from`, `to`, `via[]` and `mentioned[]`, across the voyage to Rome and, for Sidon, Jesus' journeys too) became `{"kind": "location", "locationId": "<id>"}`, keeping `candidateIndex` where the original reference had one (Phoenix's `mentioned` entry, candidate 0).
- `data/routes/stops.json`: the 15 converted entries removed (32 → 17, leaving batches B and C).
- `scripts/lib/route-line-rules.mjs`: the hardcoded stop keys `"route-stop:cauda"` (in `ISLAND_VIAS`) and `"route-stop:salmone"` / `"route-stop:cauda"` (in `SIDE_PASSES`, stage-03 and stage-04) renamed to `"location:cauda"` / `"location:salmone"`, since the key format encodes the reference's `kind`. This is a plain rename with no change to which island, side or gate the rule describes; `npm run check:route-lines` confirms every line still reproduces byte for byte and the audit still passes (71 lines, 0 failing).
- `tests/validator.test.mjs`: the one test that looked up Cnidus and Salmone's coordinates by `kind === "route-stop"` updated to `kind === "location"` to match.
- `data/reference/openbible-ids.json` and its generated TypeScript twin: regenerated with `npm run build:openbible-ids`, which scans `data/locations/*.json` for `openbible:` sources. This both added the 15 new slugs and pruned eight stale ones (`antioch-1`, `athens`, `caesarea`, `colossae`, `corinth`, `ephesus`, `philippi`, `thessalonica`) that no current location record actually cites any more — a pre-existing drift the regeneration corrected as a side effect, not something this batch's own records caused.

### Fix round (after the Fact-Checker's first verification, `docs/verification/M5-route-places.md`)

The Fact-Checker passed 4 of 15 records (Salmone, Fair Havens, Phoenix, Syracuse) and found 31 fixes (F1–F31) in the other 11, plus three questions for the PO (Q1–Q3). The PO ruled on all three and on the four judgement-call questions from the first draft. This section records what changed and why; `docs/PROGRESS.md` → Open questions quotes the rulings themselves.

- **The new `lycia` record (Q3, the parent-change ruling).** Acts 27:5 calls Myra "a city of Lycia," so Myra's `parentId` now points to a new region record, `lycia.json`, instead of `pamphylia`. Lycia's own history gives Cassius Dio's AD 43 report (the Lycians' revolt, Claudius reducing them to servitude and incorporating them into Pamphylia's prefecture) before the later, separate union with Pamphylia as one province under Vespasian, citing the same sources the timeline's own `lycia` area already uses for each span, so nothing here is a new historical claim. The timeline's `lycia` area now carries `locationId: "lycia"`, so selecting its name on the timeline opens the region's panel, matching the other 17 linked areas.
- **Myra's own history (F11)** is corrected the same way: Dio's report with its date, then the uncertainty (AD 43–74) last, instead of the unsupported "by the time of Paul's voyage Lycia's affairs were administered together with its neighboring province," which neither Dio nor the record's own `politicalHistory` note supported.
- **Sidon's `otConnections` (Q1)** are cut from ten to the five the PO named: Genesis 10:15-19; 1 Kings 17:8-24 (the Zarephath episode in full, which Luke 4:26 recalls); Isaiah 23:2-12 and Ezekiel 28:20-23 (the two prophets' oracles that lie behind Jesus' "more tolerable for Tyre and Sidon" sayings); and Joel 3:4-8, which the PO judged clearly strong enough to keep as the fifth. Each note now describes only its own cited range; F7's and F8's wording fixes are folded into this rewrite.
- **Sidon's summary (F1)** no longer carries Old Testament material (the firstborn-son and "great Sidon" facts stay only in `otConnections`), and F2–F6 remove or re-source every clause a cited source didn't actually state: "a little north of Tyre," the uncited "Artaxerxes III," a reversed account of the 351 BC revolt, and the law school moving the wrong direction.
- **Fair Havens is now `type: "natural-feature"` and Phoenix is now `type: "town"` (Q2).** Pleiades calls Kaloi Limenes "an embayment" and calls the Loutro candidate for Phoenix "an ancient coastal city…that served as the port of inland Anopolis," a description now quoted in that candidate's own `support` text. Both places keep their city zoom tier.
- **Syrtis (F18–F20)** needed a second Smith's *Dictionary* article, "Syrtica Regio" (new bibliography entry `bib:smith-dictionary-syrtica-regio`, read directly on Perseus), since "Syrtis Major" alone does not say which province held either shore. Syrtica Regio, the strip between the two Syrtes, "formed a part of the Roman province of Africa" after Carthage's fall; Cyrenaica, within the Roman province of Crete and Cyrene (ISBE "Crete"), lay well to the east. The politicalHistory `entity` text now matches this, and the "this project has no record for Africa" aside moved out of `history` into the entity text itself, as Phrygia's does.
- **Three Taverns (F25)** had its two welcoming parties backward: Forum of Appius, at the 43rd Roman milestone, is farther from Rome than Three Taverns at the 33rd, so the group that reached it travelled farther, not "only as far." Fixed to match ISBE's own order. The NKJV's "Three Inns" (confirmed directly at Bible Gateway, Acts 28:15) is added to `names.alternate` (F26).
- **Not applied:** the report's two Pleiades "Barrington Atlas note" modern names for Forum of Appius ("Faiti") and Three Taverns ("near Cisterna"). Reading both places' Pleiades JSON records directly (`names`, `locations[].references`, `connectsWith`) found no occurrence of either string, so I could not verify them myself and left Three Taverns' `names.modern` unset, as before; Forum of Appius uses "Near Latina" instead (Wikidata P131), which I could verify.
- **Two corrections to this research note itself**, both from the Fact-Checker's report: the OpenBible regeneration pruned eight stale slugs, not six (also `philippi` and `thessalonica`), and the WEB names Sidon or "the Sidonians" 37 times in the Old Testament, not 35 (the first count missed Deuteronomy 3:9). Both are fixed above.
- Every other fix (F9, F10, F12–F17, F19–F24, F27–F31) re-sourced a clause, fixed a citation gap, or corrected a wording overstatement against the source the report named, without changing any candidate, coordinate, type, zoom tier, prominence, parent or political-area decision beyond the two rulings above.

### Re-check round (after the Fact-Checker's re-check, `docs/verification/M5-route-places.md` §A7)

The re-check verified 9 of 15 records (Cnidus, Salmone, Fair Havens, Phoenix, Cauda, Syracuse, Rhegium, Forum of Appius, Three Taverns) and found 11 more fixes (F32–F42) in the remaining seven (Sidon, Myra, Lasea, Syrtis, Alexandria, Adramyttium) plus the new Lycia record. None of the nine verified records were touched, except Three Taverns, which the PO separately instructed me to add a modern name to (below).

- **Sidon's otConnections notes (F32, F33) are reworded, not re-chosen.** The PO's first ruling picked which five Old Testament passages to keep; it did not license the claim, added during that rewrite, that Jesus' "more tolerable for Tyre and Sidon" sayings "draw on" or have Isaiah 23 and Ezekiel 28:20-23 "lying behind" them. No cited source makes that connection, so both notes now describe only what their own verses say, with a neutral cross-reference ("Jesus also names Tyre and Sidon together...") rather than a claimed dependency. The Joel 3:4-8 note is also corrected: the "silver... gold... finest treasures" in v. 5 belong to the LORD (the speaker), not to Judah, and v. 8 describes a two-step sale (the LORD sells Tyre, Sidon and Philistia's children to Judah; Judah then sells them on to the Sabeans), which the note had collapsed into one step. The 1 Kings 17:8-24 note now says "during a drought," since the word "famine" sits just outside that cited range (it is in 17:1 and 17:7, and in Luke 4:25).
- **Myra's history (F34)** is corrected again: it had read that Vespasian, on making Lycia a province, "placed it under its own governor again," which is not what either cited source says. Suetonius states only that Vespasian made a province of Lycia (implying freedom before his reign, not after); a translator's note to Tacitus's *Histories* (2.9) separately states that Galatia, Pamphylia and Lycia already formed one province together by AD 69, a year before the Vespasian reorganization most sources date to AD 74. The reworded text states each source's own claim rather than combining them into an inference neither makes.
- **Lasea (F35, F36):** "Pleiades' and Wikidata's own coordinates" (reader-facing History text naming two databases, against ADR-0033) is now "modern gazetteers." The claim that Lasea lay near Gortyna, inland Crete's chief city, now cites `bib:isbe-phoenix` (where that detail actually appears) alongside `bib:isbe-crete`, which the summary had cited alone.
- **Syrtis (F37)** needed a correction, not just a new source: Smith's *Dictionary*'s "Cyrenaica" article (new bibliography entry `smith-dictionary-cyrenaica`, read on Perseus) states that Cyrenaica's own western boundary was fixed at the Great Syrtis itself ("the waters of the greater Syrtis washed its shore"), contradicting the prior round's claim that Cyrenaica lay "well to the east" of the gulf. History and the politicalHistory entity note are reworded to say the Syrtis lay at the boundary between Roman Africa and Cyrenaica, within the wider Crete-and-Cyrene province, and `bib:isbe-syrtis`, dropped in the prior round's rewrite, is restored alongside the two Smith's *Dictionary* articles and `bib:isbe-crete`.
- **Alexandria and Adramyttium's "grain ship" wording (F38, F39).** Acts names two different ships of Alexandria: the one Paul's centurion found at Myra (Acts 27:6), which the ISBE calls a grain ship, and another, wintered on Malta, that later carried Paul's party to Italy (Acts 28:11) — Acts does not say the second ship also carried grain. The prior wording let "a grain ship according to the ISBE" read as describing both; both records now attach that description only to the Myra ship, with `scripture:Acts 27:38` (the crew jettisoning the wheat) cited alongside Acts 27:6 for the grain cargo itself.
- **Lycia (F40–F42).** Pleiades' own description, "Lycia was a region of southwestern Asia Minor," replaces "the rugged country at the southwestern tip of Asia Minor": the ISBE's "southeast" is a source error (Wikidata's and Pleiades' own coordinates for Lycia, about 31 km apart, both sit in Asia Minor's southwest), so the claim now cites Pleiades (place 638965) directly rather than the ISBE. The AD 43 Cassius Dio report and the Vespasian-era union, previously stated in the summary, move into a new History entry ending with the status-uncertain caveat last, per ADR-0041 item 1 — matching how Myra's own history states the same facts. `parentId` changes from `galatia` to `pamphylia`: Dio names "the prefecture of Pamphylia" specifically as what Lycia was incorporated into in AD 43, not Galatia, so Pamphylia is the better-attested parent for the span this project models (Lycia's `politicalAreaId` stays `lycia`, so its derived `politicalHistory` is unaffected by the parent change). Finally, "Lycia's cities of greatest interest to Bible students" is corrected to match the ISBE's own sentence, "Patara, Myra and Phaselis are of interest to Bible students."
- **Three Taverns' modern name (PO instruction, not a numbered fix).** The Fact-Checker found "near Cisterna" in Pleiades' `details` field (the Barrington Atlas notes) for place 423106, which my own search of `names`, `locations[].references` and `connectsWith` had missed in the prior round. `names.modern` now reads "Near Cisterna," citing the same Pleiades entry already in this record's candidate and summary sources. Forum of Appius, verified in the re-check, is unchanged ("Near Latina").
