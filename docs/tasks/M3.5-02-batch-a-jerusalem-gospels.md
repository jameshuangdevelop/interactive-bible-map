## Places
Jerusalem (`jerusalem`), the Temple Mount (`temple-mount`), Golgotha (`golgotha`), Gethsemane (`gethsemane`), the Mount of Olives (`mount-of-olives`), Bethlehem (`bethlehem`), Nazareth (`nazareth`), Capernaum (`capernaum`), the Sea of Galilee (`sea-of-galilee`), Cana (`cana`), Jericho (`jericho`), Bethany (`bethany`) and Bethany beyond the Jordan (`bethany-beyond-the-jordan`).

## The rule
ADR-0029 (images), ADR-0017 (every factual statement cited; Wikipedia never the only source), ADR-0020 (Commons via Wikidata first), and `docs/LICENSES.md` (accepted image licenses). The period is about AD 30–60, the time of Jesus and the apostles. Where a place's location is disputed (for example Cana or Bethany beyond the Jordan), the brief covers what the candidate sites share and says so, and the images show the candidates neutrally, never favouring one.

## Phase 1 — Research Lead: research briefs
For each place, write `content/image-prompts/<id>.md`, headed "*Place* — research brief for AI-generated reconstructions", with these sections. Each statement cites source ids, adding `bib:` entries to `data/bibliography.json` where needed:
1. **Setting and layout:** the terrain, water, roads, walls and the town's size in about AD 30–60.
2. **What stood then:** the main buildings and spaces, each with when it was built, and whether it was finished, standing or ruined by AD 30–60 (for example, Herod's Temple was still being finished).
3. **Materials and colours:** stone, plaster, roofing, paint, as the excavations and sources describe them.
4. **Landscape and plants:** farming, trees, the season's look.
5. **People and daily life:** dress, trades, traffic, in general terms.
6. **Key sites to show:** 2–4 views that would best convey the place, for the photos and the prompts.
7. **Not known:** what the sources don't settle, which an image must keep generic, distant or out of frame.

Aim for 400–800 words each, from excavation reports, scholarly reconstructions (for example the Holyland Model's sources for Jerusalem), and ancient writers such as Josephus. Run `npm run validate:data`. Commit: `docs(content): add research briefs for images batch A`.

## Phase 2 — Media Curator: photos and prompts
1. **Photos:** bring each place to **5–10** images under `.github/agents/media-curator.agent.md`: the overall feel of the place, a mix of `modern` and `site` views, and a freely licensed `reconstruction` (a model, drawing or painting of the ancient place) where one exists. Cover the brief's key sites. Replace any current image that shows only fragments, keep good ones with their ids, and number new ones after the highest existing id. Put the single best overall view first. Each `kind` must be right. `npm run check:images` must pass.
2. **Prompts:** under each brief, write 1–3 prompts (`<id>-ai-01`, …): first an overview of the place as it looked in about AD 30–60, then key sites. Each prompt states its viewpoint, the time of day and the light, and what is in frame, keeps to the brief, and says how to handle what the brief lists as unknown. Aim for the detail and atmosphere of a historical game's establishing shot, with no people in the foreground and no text in the image.

Commit: `data(media): add images and prompts for batch A`.

## Phase 3 — Fact-Checker
Check every brief statement against its sources, every prompt against its brief (flag anything anachronistic, such as later domes, churches or walls), and every image: license from its Commons page, that it shows this place (categories and description), its `kind`, the overall-feel rule, and that it loads. Write `docs/verification/M3.5-images-a.md`, and fix or send back every problem. Commit: `docs(verification): verify images batch A`.

## Out of scope
Generating AI images (M3.5-07), the "About" text, standard places, and app code.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] Every place has 5–10 images, with at least one `modern` and one `site` view (or a note in the verification report where none is freely licensed), and all load.
- [ ] Every place has a cited brief and 1–3 checked prompts.
- [ ] The validator passes; the Fact-Checker's report has no open findings.
