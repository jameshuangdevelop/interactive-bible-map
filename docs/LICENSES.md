## Accepted image licenses
Per Media Curator brief rule #5, the project accepts only:
- **Public domain** (PD-old, PD-US-gov, CC0, etc.)
- **CC BY** (any version, including accepted jurisdiction ports and the 3.0 IGO variant — see below)
- **CC BY-SA** (any version, including accepted jurisdiction ports and the 3.0 IGO variant — see below)

**unless this list is expanded later by an ADR.** Wikimedia Commons' own free-content policy (`commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Licensing`, read 2026-09-23) means every file hosted there is *free*, but Commons hosts a substantially wider range of free licenses than the three above — confirmed at `commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Reusing_content_outside_Wikimedia/licenses/en` and `commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Copyright_tags` (both read 2026-09-23), which document GFDL (a documentation license "not accepted for new files on Commons except under specific circumstances," but still present on legacy files), GPL/LGPL (used for some icons/screenshots), the Free Art License, and assorted "various free licenses" including government/open-data licences (e.g. national open-government licences) as all commonly tagged on Commons. Being hosted on Commons is therefore **not** sufficient evidence of an accepted license. **The Media Curator must read and record each file's exact license from its own description page and reject anything outside the three accepted bands above — explicitly including GFDL-only files, Free Art License (FAL)-only files, GPL/LGPL-only files, and government/open-data-licence-only files** — even though all of these are freely hosted on Commons. A file that is multi-licensed (e.g. GFDL *and* CC BY-SA) is fine to use under its CC BY-SA option.

**IGO-variant licenses (added M2-02, `docs/verification/M2-batch-1.md`):** Commons also hosts files under jurisdiction-specific IGO variants of the standard Creative Commons licenses (for example **CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO**, used for photographs UNESCO and other intergovernmental organizations publish). These variants keep the same permissions and the same Attribution/ShareAlike conditions as their standard counterpart; the IGO text only substitutes UN/IGO-specific dispute-resolution and immunity clauses in place of the standard jurisdiction clause. An IGO-variant license is therefore **accepted** under the same band as its non-IGO counterpart (e.g. CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO under the CC BY-SA band), provided the Media Curator records the **exact** variant string (including "IGO") rather than the plain version number, since the two are legally distinct documents.

**Ported (jurisdiction) licenses (added M2-03, `docs/verification/M2-batch-2.md`):** Before version 4.0, Creative Commons translated and legally adapted each license to individual jurisdictions' copyright law — for example **CC BY-SA 2.0 de**, the German port used for one Corinth image. A ported license is designed to grant the **same substantive permissions and impose the same conditions** (Attribution, and ShareAlike where applicable) as its unported/international counterpart of the same version; CC's own FAQ on porting states a port should not offer more or less freedom than the generic license it adapts (`creativecommons.org/faq`, "What are the international ('unported') Creative Commons licenses, and why does CC offer 'ported' licenses?"). Porting **stopped entirely at 4.0**: CC's own announcement of the 4.0 suite states the license was written to be "ready-to-use around the world, without porting," so ports exist only for the **1.0–3.0** license versions, never 4.0 (`creativecommons.org/version4`). A ported license is therefore **accepted** under the same band as its unported counterpart (e.g. CC BY-SA 2.0 de under the CC BY-SA band), provided the Media Curator records the **exact** ported string (the version plus its two-letter, lowercase jurisdiction code, e.g. "2.0 de") rather than the plain version number, since a port and its unported original are legally distinct documents. Ports and the IGO variant do not combine — there is no "IGO" port and no port of the 3.0 IGO license — and no file may claim a port on 4.0, since none exists; a file recorded that way is misdescribed and must be re-checked directly on its Commons page.

**Reading the two variant rulings together:** for any CC BY or CC BY-SA license, the accepted forms are: the plain version number (1.0 through 4.0); a 1.0–3.0 version with its exact two-letter jurisdiction port appended; or exactly "3.0 IGO". No other suffix, and no combination of a port with "IGO", is accepted.

**What the UI must show for share-alike (CC BY-SA) images:** we show each photo as Commons serves it (the file itself, or Commons' own scaled copy), so CC BY-SA's Attribution condition applies (§3(a) of the CC BY-SA 4.0 legal code), not the fuller ShareAlike condition (§3(b), which applies only to "Adapted Material" we produce). Scaling is a technical modification that "never produces Adapted Material" (§2(a)(4)), and cropping a photo to fit the panel adds no creative material of ours. If any future feature recolors, composites or otherwise adapts an image, that adaptation must also be offered under the same or a BY-SA-compatible license and carry its own ShareAlike notice; the Frontend Engineer and Media Curator revisit this if such a feature is built. What every image's credit contains, and where it appears, is set out next.

### Image credits: what they contain and where they appear (M3-19)
Ruled on 2026-10-05 for ADR-0032, after reading each legal code on creativecommons.org, CC's FAQ, Commons:Reusing content outside Wikimedia, Commons:Credit line, and the credit requests on the photos' Commons pages. On that date the 275 images use 13 license strings: CC BY-SA 4.0 (98), CC BY-SA 3.0 (45), CC0 1.0 (31, our AI images), CC BY-SA 2.0 (27), CC0 (14), CC BY 3.0 (13), Public domain (12), CC BY 4.0 (12), CC BY 2.0 (11), CC BY 2.5 (9), and one each of CC BY-SA 3.0 de, CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO and CC BY-SA 2.5. This is a reading of the licenses, not legal advice.

**What each credit contains**
- **Photos from Commons:** the creator's name, the exact license name linked to its legal text, and a link to the Commons file page. The name takes the form the file's Commons page asks for, in its "Attribution" field or permission text: version 4.0 asks for the creator "in any reasonable manner requested by the Licensor (including by pseudonym if designated)" (§3(a)(1)(A)(i)), and versions 2.5 and 3.0 add the name of "another party or parties" the author designates "for attribution", such as "Pikiwiki Israel" (§4(b) in CC BY, §4(c) in CC BY-SA).
- **Public domain and CC0 photos:** no credit is legally required. CC0 1.0 sets no attribution condition, and Commons:Credit line says "Public Domain media do not require a credit line or any kind of attribution; however, reusers are encouraged to attribute the author (if known)". They get the same entry as every other photo, with "Public domain" or "CC0" as the license.
- **AI images:** the credit line in "AI-generated reconstructions (M3.5-07)" below.
- **Changes:** the panel and its thumbnails crop photos to fit their frames (CSS `object-fit: cover`), and the large viewer shows each photo whole. CC BY and CC BY-SA 4.0 require us to "indicate if You modified the Licensed Material" (§3(a)(1)(B)), and CC's FAQ names "cropped a photo" as such a change. Versions 2.0–3.0 require this only for adaptations, but CC recommends it anyway. One note at the top of the "Photo credits" list covers every image: "Photos are unmodified, except that the panel crops them to fit. Open a photo to see it whole." This replaces the earlier rule of an "unmodified" mark in each credit, which the app never showed.

**Where credits appear.** Each image's full credit may move from under the panel's image to a numbered "Photo credits" list at the end of the same panel, for every license in use:

| Licenses (images) | What the legal code says about placement | Ruling |
|---|---|---|
| CC BY 4.0, CC BY-SA 4.0 (110) | §3(a)(2): "You may satisfy the conditions in Section 3(a)(1) in any reasonable manner based on the medium, means, and context in which You Share the Licensed Material. For example, it may be reasonable to satisfy the conditions by providing a URI or hyperlink to a resource that includes the required information." | Allowed. A list in the same panel, linked from the image, is closer than the separate resource the clause itself accepts. |
| CC BY 3.0, CC BY-SA 3.0, CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO, CC BY-SA 3.0 de (60) | §4(b) in CC BY, §4(c) in CC BY-SA and the IGO variant: credit "reasonable to the medium or means You are utilizing", which "may be implemented in any reasonable manner; provided, however, that in the case of a Adaptation or Collection, at a minimum such credit will appear, if a credit for all contributing authors of the Adaptation or Collection appears, then as part of these credits and in a manner at least as prominent as the credits for the other contributing authors" (the IGO text says "contributors"). The German port, Abschnitt 4.c): credit "in jeder angemessenen Form" (in any reasonable form), and for a Sammelwerk (collection) "zumindest ebenso hervorgehoben ... wie die Hinweise auf die übrigen Rechteinhaber" (at least as prominent as the credits for the other rights holders). | Allowed, under the prominence rules below. |
| CC BY 2.0, CC BY-SA 2.0, CC BY 2.5, CC BY-SA 2.5 (48) | §4(b) in CC BY, §4(c) in CC BY-SA: credit "reasonable to the medium or means You are utilizing"; "Such credit may be implemented in any reasonable manner; provided, however, that in the case of a Derivative Work or Collective Work, at a minimum such credit will appear where any other comparable authorship credit appears and in a manner at least as prominent as such other comparable authorship credit." | Allowed, under the prominence rules below. |
| Public domain (12), CC0 (14), CC0 1.0 (31, AI) | No credit condition. | Allowed; credited as a courtesy. |

Versions 2.0–3.0 also require "a copy of, or the Uniform Resource Identifier for, this License with every copy" we display (§4(a)). The linked license name in each entry, in the same panel, meets this, and the viewer repeats it. A licensor can't fix where credit goes: CC's FAQ answers "Can I insist on the exact placement of the attribution credit?" with "No." Two photos' Commons pages still say where their authors would like it:
- athens-02 (Thomas Wolf, CC BY-SA 3.0 de) allows credit "direkt am Bild oder in einem gesonderten Bildquellenverzeichnis" (next to the image or in a separate list of picture credits), as long as it is "eindeutig zuordbar und leicht aufzufinden" (clearly matched to the image and easy to find). "Angaben als Mouseover reichen nicht aus" (mouseover text is not enough).
- rome-08 (David Iliff, CC BY-SA 2.5) asks for credit "preferably in a prominent location near the image". This is a preference, not a condition, and the viewer's credit line meets it.

**Prominence (versions 2.0–3.0).** The panel combines separate works (photos, our text and WEB verses), so it is a "Collective Work" or "Collection" in these licenses' own terms ("such as encyclopedias and anthologies"), and their prominence clause applies. The panel's comparable credits are its Sources: [n] markers in the text, and full entries in a numbered list at the end. So:
1. "Photo credits" comes directly after Sources and before the footer, with the same heading style and the same type size, colour and link style as the Sources entries. It is never collapsed, hidden behind a toggle, or set in smaller type, and it still appears when a place has no Sources.
2. No image gets an author credit where the others don't. Under the panel's image, none does; in the large viewer, every image does. Captions and the kind labels ("Today", "AI-generated reconstruction") name no author, so they are not comparable credits.

**What stays by the image, and how each credit is tied to its image**
1. Entries are numbered in gallery order: entry *n* is the image the counter shows as "*n* / *N*". A place with one image has no counter, and its entry is 1.
2. **Required:** a small, visible link by the panel's image (for example "Credit" after the caption, in caption type) leads to that image's entry, as the [n] markers lead to Sources. It must be a real link, not a tooltip or hover text. It keeps every credit clearly matched and easy to find, as athens-02 requires, including at places with one image and no counter.
3. The "AI-generated reconstruction" label stays on AI images (ADR-0029 §7.6), and the large viewer keeps the full credit line under each image.
4. If an image fails to load, its entry stays, so the numbers still match.

### Collapsed credits (M3-21)
Ruled on 2026-10-05 for ADR-0033 item 3, after reading again each legal code's credit clauses on creativecommons.org, CC's FAQ, CC's wiki page "Recommended practices for attribution", Commons:Reusing content outside Wikimedia, Commons:Credit line, and the Commons pages of athens-02 and rome-08. It amends "Image credits: what they contain and where they appear (M3-19)", which merges first: where that section's prominence rule 1 says the list "is never collapsed, hidden behind a toggle", this section applies instead. The rest of M3-19 stands. This is a reading of the licenses, not legal advice.

**Ruling.** "Photo credits" may start collapsed, for every license in use, but only while Sources starts collapsed in exactly the same way.

| Licenses | What decides it | Ruling |
|---|---|---|
| CC BY 2.0, CC BY-SA 2.0, CC BY 2.5, CC BY-SA 2.5 | §4(b) in CC BY, §4(c) in CC BY-SA: credit "may be implemented in any reasonable manner", but in a Collective Work "at a minimum such credit will appear where any other comparable authorship credit appears and in a manner at least as prominent as such other comparable authorship credit". The comparable credit is Sources (M3-19). | Allowed while Sources is collapsed the same way. The list then appears where Sources appears, and just as prominently. |
| CC BY 3.0, CC BY-SA 3.0, CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO | §4(b) in CC BY, §4(c) in CC BY-SA and the IGO variant: the same "any reasonable manner", and in a Collection, "if a credit for all contributing authors ... appears", credit "at least as prominent as the credits for the other contributing authors" (the IGO text says "contributors"). | Allowed on the same condition. |
| CC BY-SA 3.0 de | Abschnitt 4.c): "in jeder angemessenen Form" (in any reasonable form), and "zumindest ebenso hervorgehoben ... wie die Hinweise auf die übrigen Rechteinhaber" (at least as prominent as the credits for the other rights holders). athens-02's page also asks for credit that is "eindeutig zuordbar und leicht aufzufinden" (clearly matched and easy to find), and says "Angaben als Mouseover reichen nicht aus" (mouseover text is not enough). A visible heading with its count, and a visible "Credit" link that opens the entry on a click rather than a hover, meet this. | Allowed on the same condition. |
| CC BY 4.0, CC BY-SA 4.0 | §3(a)(2): "any reasonable manner based on the medium, means, and context in which You Share the Licensed Material. For example, it may be reasonable to satisfy the conditions by providing a URI or hyperlink to a resource that includes the required information." There is no prominence clause. | Allowed. A collapsed list in the same panel, opened by a link by the image, is closer than the separate linked resource the clause accepts. |
| Public domain, CC0, CC0 1.0 (AI) | No credit condition. | Allowed. |

CC's guidance agrees that credit one step away is reasonable on the web. Its FAQ says, for all CC licenses, "you may satisfy the attribution requirement by providing a link to a place where the attribution information may be found", and its wiki says "There is no single best or correct way of attribution, as long as your attribution is reasonable and suited to the medium you're working with." Commons sets no placement rule of its own: Commons:Credit line lets reusers choose a credit line "reasonable to the medium or means" as long as all required components are present. None of these pages mentions collapsed or click-to-open credits.

**Conditions.** All of these must hold. If one can't, "Photo credits" starts open.
1. **Same as Sources.** The same heading level and style, the same toggle control, the same count format and the same starting state. If Sources ever starts open, so does Photo credits. A place without Sources still shows Photo credits, and it may start collapsed there too, since no comparable credit appears. A reader who opens or closes either section changes only their own view, and that choice may carry over to later places.
2. **Collapsed heading.** It stays a heading and reads "Photo credits · *N*", in the same form as "Sources · 13". *N* is the number of entries, which is the number of images in the gallery, AI and public-domain images included.
3. **"Credit" link.** It stays visible under the panel's image whether the list is open or closed. Selecting it, by mouse, touch or keyboard, opens Photo credits if it is closed (it never closes it), brings that image's entry into view and moves focus to it. Its text stays "Credit" and its accessible name stays "Credit for image *n*".
4. **Entries stay in the page.** As a conservative way to meet the 2.0–3.0 licenses' license-link requirement (§4(a)), the entries stay in the page while collapsed: collapsing hides the list but does not remove it, so each entry's license link stays with the photo. This is project guidance, not license text. The clause itself says only that we must "include a copy of, or the Uniform Resource Identifier for, this License with every copy" of the photo we display (3.0 adds "(URI)"; the German port says "stets ... beifügen", always attach). Unlike the credit clause, it has no "reasonable manner" wording, and it says nothing about collapsed lists.
5. **Crop note.** "Photos are unmodified, except that the panel crops them to fit. Open a photo to see it whole." stays first in the list, unchanged.

**The large viewer's credit line** credits the viewer's own enlarged copy, and it meets rome-08's wish for credit "preferably in a prominent location near the image". On its own it is not enough for the panel's copies of any CC BY or CC BY-SA image. It shows only after a photo is opened, nothing points to it, and for versions 2.0–3.0 it does not appear where Sources appears. It would be enough for public-domain and CC0 images, which need no credit, but they keep their entries so that the numbers match the gallery.

**Nearest allowed design if a condition can't be met** (for example, Sources starts open, or the "Credit" link can't open the list): "Photo credits" starts open, directly after Sources, as M3-19 ruled. The reader may still collapse it, and the app may remember that for later places in the same visit.

## WEB (Bible text) attribution wording
> Scripture quotations are from the **World English Bible (WEB)**, a public-domain translation of the Bible (66-book Protestant-canon edition, eBible.org). "World English Bible" is a trademark of eBible.org; this project is not produced, reviewed, or endorsed by eBible.org.

This wording is edition-agnostic and works for either `engwebp` (US spelling, "LORD"/"GOD") or `engwebpb` (British/international spelling, "LORD"/"GOD") — both are public domain with identical trademark status, confirmed on their own eBible.org pages (read 2026-09-23). The edition itself (US vs. British spelling) is the human's choice at CP1; see `docs/verification/M1.md` §2 Q4. The Classic editions (`eng-web`/`eng-webbe`, which use "Yahweh" and include the Apocrypha) remain out of scope per the brief's 66-book canon rule.

## AI-generated reconstructions (M3.5-07)
Decided in M3.5-07 (`docs/research/M3.5-ai-generators.md`) after the human chose the Gemini API with Nano Banana Pro (`gemini-3-pro-image`) on 2026-10-01 (ADR-0029 §7). Google's Gemini API Additional Terms of Service (effective 2026-03-23) were read again on 2026-10-01. This is a reading of published terms, not legal advice.

- **License:** every image in `media/ai/` is released under **CC0 1.0 Universal** (<https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/>). In the United States, purely AI-generated material has no copyright, and prompts alone don't make the user an author (US Copyright Office, *Copyright and Artificial Intelligence, Part 2*, 2025; *Thaler v. Perlmutter*, D.C. Cir. 2025). Where some right does exist elsewhere, CC0 waives it. Creative Commons recommends CC0 for such works. The briefs and prompts in `content/image-prompts/` stay CC BY-SA 4.0.
- **Generator terms** (Gemini API Additional Terms of Service, effective 2026-03-23):
  - Google "won't claim ownership" of generated content, and "may generate the same or similar content for others". Nothing in the terms limits how others reuse the images, so CC0 is consistent with them.
  - "You're responsible for your use of generated content, and for the use of that content by anyone you share it with." This is why every image is checked against its cited brief before it is published, and why it carries the label below.
  - The ban on using the Services "to develop models that compete with the Services" binds the project, not people who reuse the images.
- **Under 18:** Gemini API users must be 18 or over, and must not use the Services as part of a website or app "that is directed towards or is likely to be accessed by individuals under the age of 18". Young readers will use this map, so **the app never calls the Gemini API**. The maintainer, who must be 18 or over, generates each image with `npm run generate:ai` on their own machine, and the site serves only the finished files in `media/ai/`.
- **Professional use:** the Gemini API is "for professional or business purposes, not for consumer use". We read generating images for a published reference project as professional use, not personal consumer use.
- **Paid tier:** generate on the paid tier. On unpaid use, Google uses prompts and outputs to improve its products, and human reviewers may read them.
- **SynthID:** "All generated images include a SynthID watermark" (Gemini API image-generation guide, last updated 2026-09-23). The mark is invisible and designed to withstand cropping, filters and lossy compression, so it stays in our WebP files. We never try to remove or obscure it. Images from the API carry no visible watermark.
- **Label:** every AI image shows "AI-generated reconstruction" on the image in the app (ADR-0029 §7). Google's terms forbid passing off AI-generated content as made by a human.
- **Credit line:** `AI-generated reconstruction · Google Gemini API (Nano Banana Pro) · CC0 1.0 · Based on: research brief`. The tool name comes from the record's `generator.tool`. "CC0 1.0" links to the CC0 deed, and "research brief" links to the place's cited brief in `content/image-prompts/`, which lists the sources the image is based on (ADR-0029 §7.6). This is the line the app has shown since M3.5-06, as described in the CP3.5 summary the human approved (#37). It replaces an earlier draft (`… · Interactive Bible Map, made with <tool>, <date> · …`), which named the project to meet OpenAI's terms; Google's terms ask for no credit, and CC0 needs none. Since M3-19 the line appears in the large viewer and in the panel's "Photo credits" list, while the label stays on the image. We ask reusers to keep the label.
- **Media record values:** `author` is `Interactive Bible Map (AI-generated with Google Gemini API)`, `license` is `CC0 1.0`, `generator` holds the tool, model and date from the side-car file, and `basedOn` lists the brief's source ids.
- **Inputs:** text prompts only. A reference image may be used only if it is public domain or CC0, and it must be recorded with the image.
- **Metadata:** converting to WebP may drop the C2PA metadata that Google adds to some images; no clause in Google's terms forbids this. The maintainer keeps the original files outside the repository for as long as the images are published (`media/ai-incoming/` is ignored by Git).

### Ruling: hiding disputed boundaries
Hiding boundary features where `disputed = 1` (OpenMapTiles) or `disputed = true` (Protomaps, Shortbread) in **our hosted style** changes **no** attribution or license obligation. A filtered rendering is still an ODbL Produced Work (§4.5(b)); the style file contains no OpenStreetMap data; the §4.3 credit stays exactly as before; and the "modified" notice that Liberty's CC BY license already requires covers the change (the strings list it). This holds only while the filtering is done in the style. Deleting the features from the tile data and serving the result would create a Derivative Database, which must be released under the ODbL and offered in full or as an alteration file (§4.4, §4.6). Full reasoning: `docs/verification/M3-basemap.md` §3.

## Do-not-use / not-yet list
- **ORBIS interactive web app** (`orbis.stanford.edu`) — figures, screenshots, or tiles read directly from the live app. Its own footer credits its background tiles to AWMC under **CC BY-NC 3.0** (non-commercial), and no other terms page could be found. Use only the CC BY 3.0 flat-file node/edge dataset.
- **Perseus Digital Library** texts — no content license was found on the pages checked (`/hopper/copyright` is 404; `/hopper/opensource` covers only the Hopper software, not the texts). Cite-only if ever independently confirmed; do not quote. This covers Perseus's own editions and translations, not public-domain works it hosts, such as Smith's *Dictionary of Greek and Roman Geography* (1854), whose text may be quoted (see the public-domain row above).
- **DAAHL (Digital Archaeological Atlas of the Holy Land)** — `daahl.ucsd.edu` did not resolve during M2-03 verification (2026-09-23) and no archived copy carried a license/terms page; secondary sources describe only an implied permission requirement, not a CC/public-domain grant. M2-03's research draft used a `bib:daahl-askar-site` entry as the sole `coordinateSource` for `sychar`'s Askar candidate; that use is rejected until DAAHL's own license is confirmed directly on the source (see `docs/verification/M2-batch-2.md`).
- **Wikipedia text** — cite as a pointer to primary/secondary sources only; never copy or closely paraphrase, per brief rule #6, even though CC BY-SA would technically permit reuse with attribution.
- **eBible.org Classic editions** (`eng-web`, `eng-webbe`) — include the Apocrypha/Deuterocanon and use "Yahweh"; excluded by the brief's 66-book Protestant canon rule. Only `engwebp`/`engwebpb` are in scope.
- **Any Wikimedia Commons file whose license is not PD/CC0, CC BY, or CC BY-SA (including IGO variants of these)** — Commons excludes NC/fair-use/ND uploads, but it does host GFDL-only, Free Art License (FAL)-only, GPL/LGPL-only, government/open-data-licence-only, and bare `{{Attribution}}`-only files, none of which this project accepts (unless expanded later by an ADR). The Media Curator must read and record the specific license per file and reject anything outside those three bands (or their IGO variants), not assume Commons hosting implies acceptability.
- **Commons' bare `{{Attribution}}` / "Attribution only license" tag** (found on `File:Niin_120.jpg`, M2-02 batch 1 re-verification, `docs/verification/M2-batch-1.md`) — this is a generic, non-versioned, uncodified permission grant ("the copyright holder... allows anyone to use it for any purpose, provided attribution"), not a specific Creative Commons legal instrument. It is not "CC BY `<version>`" even though it grants similar-in-spirit permissions, so it does not fall within the accepted bands. Do not record it as a CC BY version.
- **OpenBible.info fields sourced from OpenStreetMap** — any `coordinates_source`/`secondary_sources` entry with `type: "osm"`/`"osm_group"` (`modern.jsonl`), or a `precise_geometry_id` (`ancient.jsonl`). Use a non-OSM coordinate source instead; see the one-rule policy above and `docs/verification/M1.md` Q3.
- **Copyrighted academic works** (journal articles, the Anchor Bible Dictionary, the print Barrington Atlas, etc.) — cite only, per brief rule #4/§2.6; never copied or paraphrased closely enough to be a derivative reproduction.
- **Verbatim quotations from cite-only sources** (ruling added M2-05, `docs/verification/M2-batch-3.md`). A record in `data/` may quote a source word for word only when that source's text is public domain, CC0, CC BY or CC BY-SA (for example WEB scripture, Wikidata, Pleiades or OpenBible.info), because the quotation then becomes part of the CC BY-SA 4.0 data under a compatible license. Quoting any other source (NonCommercial-licensed text such as World History Encyclopedia, custom-terms text such as Livius.org, UNESCO WHC listing text whose license is not stated, or all-rights-reserved works) would depend on fair use or a quotation exception, which the brief rules out. Such sources stay in `sources[]` as `bib:` citations, and the clause they support is written in the project's own words.
- **Adobe Firefly outputs, including partner models used inside Firefly** (M3.5-07). Adobe's Generative AI Product Specific Terms (effective 2026-04-23) forbid removing Content Credentials (§3.1), which our WebP conversion can drop. They also forbid allowing third parties to use outputs to train AI (§3.3), which conflicts with releasing the images under CC0 or any other open license.
- **FLUX.1 [dev] outputs** (M3.5-07). Its license is for non-commercial and non-production use only.

## CP1 decisions needing the human
1. **WEB edition:** `engwebp` (US spelling) vs. `engwebpb` (British/international spelling). Licensing is identical for both (public domain, same trademark status, both 66-book Protestant canon, both render God's name as "LORD"/"GOD"). Purely an editorial choice. **Recommendation:** `engwebp`, as the more common default for a US-hosted, English-first audience — but either is fully compliant.
2. **Basemap/tile provider** (M1-02's stack recommendation: OpenFreeMap, runner-up self-hosted Protomaps PMTiles). Both trace back to OpenStreetMap ODbL data and require the same OSM attribution notice; the exact wording and any additional provider-specific credit (e.g. "OpenMapTiles") depends on which is chosen. **Resolved:** CP1 chose OpenFreeMap (ADR-0009) and CP3a chose its Liberty style (ADR-0022). The wording is under "Basemap (M3-07)" above.
