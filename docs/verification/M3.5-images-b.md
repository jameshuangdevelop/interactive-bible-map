# M3.5-03 verification — Images, batch B: Paul's letters and the capitals

Independent verification of the Research Lead's briefs and the Media Curator's images and prompts for the 12 batch-B places (`rome`, `corinth`, `galatia`, `ephesus`, `philippi`, `colossae`, `thessalonica`, `crete`, `athens`, `antioch-syria`, `caesarea-maritima`, `damascus`), against the card (`docs/tasks/M3.5-03-batch-b-paul-capitals.md`, which follows M3.5-02), ADR-0017, ADR-0020, ADR-0026, ADR-0029 and `docs/LICENSES.md`. The branch head at the start was `9d4f306` (briefs from Phase 1, images and prompts from `c4d9602`, the lead-image follow-up `039bd0e` and the image sizes `2281784`). Reviewed 2026-10-01.

**Result:** every brief, prompt and image now passes. All 12 briefs needed changes. The tables below list 98 findings, all fixed in this commit: 59 in the briefs, 13 in the prompts (some rows cover several prompts) and 26 in the image data. The most common problems were:

- **Clauses their cited source does not state** (most briefs). The Materials and Landscape sections were the weakest: "brick-faced concrete", "limestone", "olives and vines", "forested slopes" and "mudbrick" appeared with sources that say nothing about them. Where no source describes a city's materials or plants, the brief now says so, and the prompts keep them generic.
- **Anachronism flags with the wrong source.** The Library of Celsus, Philippi's forum and Thessalonica's forum were dated "after Paul" on the strength of UNESCO's "Roman Imperial period" or the 1915 ISBE, neither of which gives those dates. The flags are now sourced to the Austrian Academy of Sciences (Celsus, 2nd century AD) and the Greek Ministry of Culture's Odysseus pages (Philippi's forum, Antonine; Thessaloniki's forum, late 2nd century; the Galerian complex, about AD 300).
- **Factual errors.** A prompt said Caecilia Metella's tomb was built after Paul (it is older). Crete's brief called Knossos "ancient history" (ISBE lists it among Crete's most important cities). Colossae was placed "between" Laodicea and Hierapolis (it lies east of both). Colossae was said never to have been excavated (a survey began in 2021 and excavation has now begun).
- **Scripture said more than the verse does.** Acts 18:18 does not say Paul sailed from Cenchreae. Acts 28:16 does not name the Via Appia. Acts 14:12 has "Jupiter" and "Mercury", not Hermes and Zeus. Acts 17:17 and 2 Corinthians 11:32 were misquoted in a caption and a brief.
- **Neutrality.** Two places took sides: the brief and a prompt set Paul's Areopagus speech on the hill, and the brief and captions equated Mt Cadmus with Honaz Dağı. Both are now neutral.

Four images were added: Galatia's new lead and a reconstruction, a Colossae site view, and Caesarea's hippodrome. None were removed. Every place keeps 5–10 images, with at least one `modern` and one `site` view.

## Method

1. **Sources opened.** For every bracketed citation in the 12 briefs (sections 1–7), I read the cited source and searched it for each clause:
   - **ISBE (1915):** the 15 cited articles, plus "Appii Forum", "Antioch of Pisidia" and "Colossae".
   - **Platner & Ashby on LacusCurtius:** Circus Maximus, Forum Romanum, Carcer, Insulae, Via Appia, Aqua Claudia, Amphitheatrum Flavium, Arcus Titi and Saturnus, Aedes.
   - **Loeb translations on LacusCurtius:**
     - Tacitus, *Annals* 14.27 and 15.38–43;
     - Strabo 8.6.20–22, 12.8.13–17 and 16.2.4–7;
     - Suetonius, *Augustus* 40 and 60;
     - Cassius Dio 69.16.
   - **Josephus (Whiston, CCEL):** *Antiquities* 15.9.6 and 16.5.3, and *Jewish War* 1.21.11.
   - **UNESCO (through the Wayback Machine, since whc.unesco.org refuses scripted requests):** Ephesus, Hierapolis-Pamukkale, Philippi, Thessalonika and Damascus.
   - **Other web sources:** World History Encyclopedia (WEH) "Corinth", Livius "Nabataeans" and "Damascus", the Odysseus pages for Philippi, the Thessaloniki Agora and the Galerian palace, the Austrian Academy of Sciences' Celsus Library page (2 December 2024), and the Biblical Archaeology Society's (BAS) interview with Colossae's excavation director (10 July 2026).
   - **Datasets:** Pleiades 570182, 658381, 638811, 638884 and 658598.
2. **Quotations.** A script checked every quoted string in the briefs against the fetched source texts and the WEB snapshot (`data/reference/engwebp_vpl.txt`). Two mismatches were found and fixed. I also looked up 28 verses that the briefs and captions paraphrase.
3. **Images.** For all 71 images, then the 4 added ones, I fetched the Commons API (`prop=imageinfo|categories`, `iiprop=url|size|extmetadata`, at most 2 requests at a time). I compared `LicenseShortName`, the artist, the size, the description and the categories with each record. I looked at all 12 contact sheets, opened 960 px thumbnails of 7 doubtful images, and viewed every candidate before adding it.
4. **Prompts.** I read each prompt against its brief, sentence by sentence, and listed every "Keep out" line next to its brief's "Not known" section.
5. **Checks.** I ran `npm run validate:data`, `npm test`, `npm run fill:image-sizes` and `npm run check:images` before and after the changes.

All 75 license strings match Commons' `LicenseShortName` exactly, and all are in the accepted bands: Public domain, CC0, CC BY 2.0/2.5/3.0/4.0, and CC BY-SA 2.0/2.5/3.0/3.0 de/4.0.
