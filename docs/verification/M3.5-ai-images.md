## Hard cases
- **Colossae and Thyatira** are barely excavated; both prompts were kept to the brief's own generic, honest guidance (a grassy valley town) rather than borrowing detail from neighboring, better-excavated cities.
- **Cana, Golgotha and Bethany beyond the Jordan** have two live candidate sites each; each prompt was written to be true of either candidate rather than favoring one, and the keep-out lines name the specific site features to flag if the generated image leans toward either.
- **Galatia and Crete** are regions, not single cities; both prompts show a representative landscape rather than inventing one named capital, per each brief's own caution.
- **Jerusalem and the Temple Mount** carry the most riding on them, since the human's own free-model tests (ADR-0029 §7) show models shrinking the sanctuary and adding modern cities, domes and battlements; both prompts now state the sanctuary's size by comparison to the colonnades around it and spell out a purely period-consistent background.

## Prompt check (Fact-Checker)

Checked on 2026-10-01, before any image was generated. Each `-ai-01` paragraph was read against its brief, ADR-0029 §7 and the curator's prompt rules. "Paragraph" here means exactly the text `npm run generate:ai` sends, extracted with the script's own `extractPromptTextFromMarkdown`. The shapes added for the Temple, the Antonia and Herodium were checked against Josephus in Whiston's translation (`bib:josephus-jewish-war`, `bib:josephus-antiquities`; Project Gutenberg text):
- *War* 5.5.4: the sanctuary's front is 100 cubits high and wide and the building is "forty cubits narrower" behind. Its first gate is 70 cubits high, with "no doors", and its front is "covered with gold all over".
- *War* 5.5.6: gold plates that at sunrise "reflected back a very fiery splendor"; the parts not gilded were "exceeding white".
- *War* 5.5.7: the priests at the altar wore "fine linen".
- *War* 5.5.8: the Antonia is shaped like a tower, with "four other distinct towers at its four corners", 50 and 70 cubits high.
- *War* 1.21.10 and *Antiquities* 15.9.4: Herodium is a hill "of the shape of a woman's breast" with "circular towers".

**Verdicts:** *Pass* means no change was needed. *Pass after fixes* means the fixes listed were made in the prompt (and, where noted, its keep-out line), and the prompt now passes. 29 of the 31 prompts were changed. All 31 are 150–230 words, end with the style line, include "No text, signs or inscriptions anywhere", show only a few small figures in plain dress, and name no biblical figure. The three disputed sites (Cana, Golgotha, Bethany beyond the Jordan) stay neutral between their candidates.

**Accepted without change:** tiled roofs on Greek and Roman cities, which the Smyrna, Pergamum, Sardis, Thyatira and Laodicea briefs give as general guidance; "photographed" in the opening lines, which is a style word; and priests in white linen at the Temple (*War* 5.5.7).

**Risky to generate:**
- **Jerusalem**, the model test, is the most complex prompt. When checking the image, look first at the sanctuary's shape, scale and direction.
- **The Temple Mount** and **the Mount of Olives** share that risk. With Jerusalem, they make three views of the Temple from the east, so wait for Jerusalem's result before generating them.
- **Rome:** the Colosseum is strongly tied to this view.
- **Gethsemane:** a night scene with two groups may produce a haloed or recognisable figure.

**Notes for the Research Lead** (brief wording; no effect on the current prompts):
1. Bethlehem: cite Josephus (*War* 1.21.10; *Antiquities* 15.9.4) for Herodium's shape, which the prompt now uses.
2. Jerusalem: add the sanctuary's and the Antonia's shapes from *War* 5.5.4–6 and 5.5.8, which the prompt now uses.
3. The Temple Mount: "above it hung a golden vine" should say that the vine hung over the inner gate, behind the doorless porch (*War* 5.5.4).
4. Corinth: the diolkos ran across the isthmus between the two gulfs, at its narrowest point, not "between the two harbors" of Lechaeum and Cenchreae; check ISBE's and World History Encyclopedia's wording.
5. Capernaum and the Sea of Galilee: cite a source for the excavated boat's size (8.2 m), since the cited Wikidata item gives none.
